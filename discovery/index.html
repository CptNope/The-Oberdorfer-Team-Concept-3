SummaryDiscovery

01

The identity already exists.It needs a system.

2 typefaces

The PDF's font data names Cormorant Garamond Light and Light Italic, and Manrope ExtraLight. Every headline is Light. These are the exact faces, not guesses.

6 colors

Sampled from the pages: navy #071D2D, ivory #F7F3EC, ink #142A38, gold #B49A65, sandstone #E9E2D5, slate #59656A. All are close to the brief's estimates.

2.45 : 1

The contrast of the gold labels on ivory. That is fine in print at this size, but it fails WCAG AA on screen. The system adds a deeper Gold Ink (5.1:1).

1 illustration

The only drawing is the house linework on page 7. Decoration is otherwise limited to hairlines and the inset frame. That restraint is the luxury.

4 source credits

Photos and data are credited: Wikimedia CC BY-SA, Redfin with an access date, the Town of Wakefield, and the CFPB. The habit of citing sources is a brand asset.

3 flags

The page 6 food photo is AI-generated, as disclosed. "Top Producer" is not documented. 406-250-7620 is a Montana area code. All three need confirmation.

Page 1 · Cover

Messaging
"Fall in love with your home." Issue line "Fall 2026 / Local living. Beautiful homes." Contents tease "The Fall Field Guide". "Powered by Real Estate Wealth Acquisition and Preservation Brokerage".

The gold Cormorant wordmark sits top-center. The masthead "WAKEFIELD" is very large, light capitals, with "AT HOME" widely tracked beneath. Small Manrope caps carry the issue line and section labels.
Color
A midnight-navy field with a full-bleed photo, darkened toward the top and bottom so the type holds. Gold for the wordmark and frame.
Composition
An inset gold hairline frame. A symmetric masthead, with the cover line at bottom left and the contents at bottom right. The photo fills the page.
Photography
The couple at the door of a stately home with autumn plantings, in warm evening light. Confirm photo approval
Takeaway
The strongest single asset. The website hero should be this cover, translated.

Strengths

A distinctive, restrained luxury look that suits every price point, not only mansions.

A personal, honest voice, with sources and dates even in marketing copy.

Local editorial content that builds relationships, a channel competitors rarely match.

Opportunities

Contrast: gold labels and ExtraLight text are too faint for screens.

Geography: the masthead and copy are Wakefield-only, so the brand needs a Massachusetts-wide frame.

No logo system yet: the wordmark is typeset, with no monogram, favicon or lockups. Photography is partly sourced from Wikimedia and partly AI-generated.

Three ways forward.All from the magazine.

Each sketch shows the first screen of the homepage. All three keep the navy, ivory and gold, and the light serif.

Concept A

Editorial Heritage

The closest to the magazine: a centered serif masthead, inset double frame, navy and ivory pages, restrained gold and large light headlines.

Strength: instantly recognisable to the clients. Risk: it can feel static and print-bound on screen, and centered layouts struggle with dense listing content.

Massachusetts real estateLuxury at every price point.

Concept B

Modern Architectural

A contemporary evolution: a visible 12-column grid, asymmetric splits, linework and dimension lines from page 7, and tighter, more precise type.

Strength: handles listings, filters and data with ease. Risk: cooler and less personal, and it drifts furthest from what the clients like.

Luxury at every price point.Husband. Wife. Your Team.Let's talk

Concept C

Warm Contemporary

A softer, more personal take: sandstone grounds, arched portrait frames, rounded buttons, italic headlines and lifestyle-led storytelling.

Strength: the most approachable for first-time buyers. Risk: it loses the navy authority and edges toward the generic "cream and serif" look many brands share.

Recommended and chosen

Chosen Oct 15, 2026
Massachusetts real estateLuxury at every price point.

A + B: Editorial Heritage on an architectural grid.

From A, the identity: the cover becomes the hero inside the inset frame. Light Cormorant headlines that answer each other. Navy for covers and closings, ivory for reading. The running heads and folios as page furniture.

From B, the structure: a 12-column grid with asymmetric splits for real content such as listings, filters, agent directories and data. The dimension-line rule and the house-drawing illustration family. Precise, square components.

Why: Brandon and Kaitlyn asked to keep the magazine. A alone keeps it but cannot carry a property-search site, and B alone carries the site but loses the warmth. Together they stay recognisably the magazine and work as a website. C's warmth comes through photography and voice instead of softer shapes.
