Living in Newton, MA

Learn the villages first,then find the street that fits.

Newton is a city of thirteen villages in Middlesex County, about eight miles west of downtown Boston. Much of its housing is older: as of the 2000 Census, only 19 percent of its homes had been built after 1960. The Green Line D branch and the Framingham/Worcester commuter rail line connect it to Boston.

About Newton

Newton sits about eight miles west of downtown Boston and has no single center. Instead it is a patchwork of thirteen villages, from Auburndale and Waban to Nonantum and Chestnut Hill, and many of them have a small downtown of their own. The villages have no legal boundaries, so addresses can puzzle first-time visitors. The city goes by The Garden City. It began as Cambridge Village in 1681, took the name Newton in 1766 and became a city in 1874.

Water and transit shape the map. The Charles River runs along the city's north and west sides, past Newton Lower Falls and Newton Upper Falls, both former small industrial sites, and under Echo Bridge at Hemlock Gorge. The Green Line D branch runs through the middle of the city, with stops including Newton Centre and Riverside, while the Framingham/Worcester Line stops at Newtonville, West Newton and Auburndale. Commonwealth Avenue carries the Boston Marathon over Heartbreak Hill, between Walnut Street and Boston College.

Most of Newton's land is residential: in the city's FY2026 classification hearing packet, residential property made up 92.23 percent of taxable value, and 16,947 of its 26,888 residential parcels were single-family homes, alongside 5,883 condominiums and 2,490 two-family houses. The largest planned housing site is Riverside, the Green Line terminus, where the MBTA approved an 85-year lease of part of the parking lots for mixed-use development in 2009 and the city rezoned the site for a larger project in late 2019. A revised two-phase plan put to the MBTA board in August 2024 calls for about 545 apartments and a garage in the first phase, and an office or lab building of about 150,000 square feet with about 100 more units in the second. Under the state's MBTA Communities law, Newton appears on the state's list of compliant municipalities.

Homes in Newton

A 2003 city survey of mid-20th-century housing reported that, as of the 1960 Census, 70.5 percent of Newton's housing units were single-family dwellings and 20.5 percent were two-family homes. It also noted that, as of the 2000 Census, only 19 percent of the city's dwellings had been built after 1960. The 2020 Census counted 33,320 housing units in the city. In the FY2026 assessment, 16,947 of 26,888 residential parcels were single-family, 5,883 were condominiums, 2,490 two-family, 252 three-family and 159 apartment buildings.

Neighborhoods and villagesNewton

Where you mightlive in Newton.

Newton Centre

Newton's largest village downtown, a triangle of shops around Beacon Street, Centre Street and Langley Road, with the Green Line station on Union Street and Crystal Lake, a 33-acre natural lake, in the village.

Newtonville

A commuter suburb that grew after the railroad arrived in 1834, with its center along Walnut Street, a Framingham/Worcester Line stop, and a Star Market built on air rights over the Massachusetts Turnpike.

West Newton

Northern village with a Framingham/Worcester Line stop; its village center along Washington Street is a National Register district, and West Newton Hill and Webster Park are historic residential districts listed in 1986.

Auburndale

Western village on the Charles River, split by the Turnpike, with a commuter rail stop, Lasell University, the Plummer Memorial Library and a local historic district of about 275 properties established in 2005; Norumbega Park operated here until 1963.

Newton Upper Falls

Mill village on the Charles River, where Newton's first mill was built in 1688; about 60 acres between Route 9 and Elliot Street form a National Register district listed in 1986, and Eliot station on the Green Line serves it.

Newton Highlands

A village platted as a commuter suburb after the Charles River Railroad was built in 1852, with late 19th-century houses, a historic district around its center, and Newton Highlands and Eliot stations on the Green Line D branch.

Getting around

Commuting from Newton

The Framingham/Worcester commuter rail line stops at three northern villages, and the Green Line D branch runs through the city's center to Riverside. The Massachusetts Turnpike crosses the northern villages, and I-95/Route 128 runs along the western edge near Lower Falls.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line: Newtonville, West Newton, Auburndale
  • Green Line D branch (subway): Newton Centre, Riverside

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Things to doNewton

Places to knowin Newton.

Crystal Lake

A natural lake in Newton Centre with a city-run swim beach, bathhouse and summer lifeguards.

Jackson Homestead and Museum

Historic Newton's museum at 527 Washington Street, with exhibits on city history and the Underground Railroad.

Hammond Pond Reservation

A DCR reservation off Hammond Pond Parkway with trails, rock formations to climb and paddling on the pond.

Hemlock Gorge Reservation

A 23-acre DCR reservation on the Charles River, known for Echo Bridge and its echo.

Heartbreak Hill

The Boston Marathon's climb on Commonwealth Avenue between Walnut Street and Boston College.

Auburndale Cove

A picnic and recreation area on the Charles River in Auburndale.

Bullough's Pond

A former mill pond in Newtonville, reshaped as a landscape feature in the late 19th century, near the Johnny Kelley statue at the start of Heartbreak Hill.

West Newton Cinema

A Washington Street movie house opened in 1937 and expanded to six screens in 1990; a fundraising campaign reached its $5.6 million goal in August 2024 to keep it open.

What is the median home price in Newton, MA?

For calendar year 2025, The Warren Group reports a single-family median sale price of $1,825,000 in Newton, across 525 sales. In 2024 the median was $1,690,000 on 482 sales. Source: The Warren Group, MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town.

Does Newton have commuter rail?

Yes. The MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line stops at Newtonville, West Newton and Auburndale. The Green Line D branch also runs through the city, with stops including Newton Centre and Riverside.

Which school district serves Newton?

Newton Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

What is the property tax rate in Newton, MA?

For fiscal year 2026, Newton's rates are $9.69 per $1,000 of assessed value for residential property and $18.06 per $1,000 for commercial property, according to the city's assessing department. In fiscal 2025 they were $9.80 and $18.34.

What are Newton's villages?

Newton has thirteen villages: Auburndale, Chestnut Hill, Newton Centre, Newton Corner, Newton Highlands, Newton Lower Falls, Newton Upper Falls, Newtonville, Nonantum, Oak Hill, Thompsonville, Waban and West Newton. They have no legal boundaries.

What is planned at Riverside station?

A revised two-phase plan presented to the MBTA board in August 2024 calls for about 545 apartments and a garage in the first phase at the Riverside Green Line terminus, with an office or lab building and about 100 more units in the second.
