Living in Braintree, MA

Saltbox houses on Washington Street,and a station where three rail lines meet.

Braintree is a Norfolk County community of 39,143 people (2020 Census), about ten miles south of Boston, where I-93 and Route 3 meet. Most of its housing, 64 percent, is single-family detached homes. Braintree station on Ivory Street is served by the Red Line and two commuter rail lines, and the Greenbush Line stops at Weymouth Landing/East Braintree on the town line. It is legally a city with a mayor and council, though it keeps the name Town of Braintree.

About Braintree

Braintree was colonized in 1635 and incorporated in 1640, and the old town once took in what are now Quincy, Randolph and Holbrook. The town counts John Adams, John Quincy Adams, John Hancock and General Sylvanus Thayer among those born in Old Braintree. Water power on the Monatiquot River drew mills to South Braintree in the 1820s, and the Old Colony railroad arrived there in 1845, making South Braintree a rail junction. East Braintree grew at the head of navigation on the Fore River.

Washington Street carries much of the town's older fabric, including the Thayer Birthplace, built in 1720, and the Asa French House, built about 1699, both now in the town's historic district. Open space includes the Town Forest, Sunset Lake, Smith Beach, the Cranberry Pond Conservation District and Pond Meadow Park, shared with Weymouth, along with an 18-hole public golf course. The town site describes established neighborhoods, small clusters of new homes and several condominium complexes.

Homes in Braintree

A 2024 town feasibility study reports that 64 percent of Braintree's housing is single-family detached units and that about 74 percent of units are owner-occupied. Only 12 percent of units are studios or one-bedrooms. The study counts a 5.42 percent increase in housing units from 2010 to 2020. The town also lists several condominium complexes among its housing.

Where you mightlive in Braintree.

South Braintree

The old industrial center along the Monatiquot River, where mills developed in the 1820s and the Old Colony railroad made a junction in 1845.

East Braintree

The area at the head of navigation on the Fore River, with Colonial-era Capes along the Monatiquot; its rail stop is Weymouth Landing/East Braintree.

Braintree Highlands

Laid out as a street grid in the 1870s and built up in the 20th century; the Thayer house was built here in 1720 before its move.

Washington Street

The town's civic spine since a town house was built midway along it in 1829, with the Thayer and French houses and Town Hall.

Getting around

Commuting from Braintree

Braintree station, at 197 Ivory Street, has a Red Line platform, a commuter rail platform for the Kingston and Fall River/New Bedford lines to South Station, a busway and a 1,197-space garage. Weymouth Landing/East Braintree, on the Greenbush Line, sits on the town line with Weymouth. The seasonal CapeFLYER stops at Braintree on summer weekends on its way to Hyannis. I-93 and Route 3 meet in town.

  • Red Line (subway): Braintree
  • Kingston Line: Braintree
  • Fall River/New Bedford Line: Braintree
  • Greenbush Line (on the Braintree/Weymouth line): Weymouth Landing/East Braintree

Places to knowin Braintree.

Pond Meadow Park

A 320-acre park shared with Weymouth, opened in 1976, with a two-mile paved loop around a 20-acre pond and unpaved trails branching off it.

General Sylvanus Thayer Birthplace

A 1720 farmhouse at 786 Washington Street, birthplace of the West Point superintendent, moved here in 1958 and listed on the National Register in 1974.

Asa French House

A house built about 1699 at 766 Washington Street, now owned by the Braintree Historical Society.

Thayer Public Library

The town library across from Town Hall, on land acquired in 1953 and rebuilt in 1997, with more than 125,000 items.

Blue Hills Reservation

A DCR reservation of more than 7,000 acres and 125 miles of trails, with a paved accessible path on Old Route 128 off West Street in Braintree.

Sunset Lake and the Town Forest

Two of the town's recreation areas, along with Smith Beach and the Cranberry Pond Conservation District.

What is the median home price in Braintree, MA?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, the median single-family sale price in Braintree for calendar year 2025 was $725,000, across 214 sales. In 2024 it was $680,000, across 242 sales.

Does Braintree have commuter rail?

Yes. Braintree station on Ivory Street is served by the Red Line and by the Kingston and Fall River/New Bedford commuter rail lines to South Station. The Greenbush Line stops at Weymouth Landing/East Braintree, on the town line with Weymouth.

Is Braintree a city or a town?

Both, in a sense. It is officially named the Town of Braintree, but it has a mayor and council form of government and is considered a city under Massachusetts law.

Which school district serves Braintree?

Braintree Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.
