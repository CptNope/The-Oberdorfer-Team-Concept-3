Living in New Bedford, MA

Cobblestones above the working harbor,and the train back on Acushnet Avenue.

New Bedford is a Bristol County city of 101,079 people (2020 Census) on the Acushnet River where it meets Buzzards Bay. Its downtown waterfront is part of New Bedford Whaling National Historical Park, established in 1996. The Warren Group counted 401 single-family sales in 2025. Since March 24, 2025, the MBTA Fall River/New Bedford Line has run from New Bedford and Church Street stations to Boston's South Station, and I-195 crosses the city.

About New Bedford

New Bedford was incorporated as a town in 1787 and became a city in 1847. In the first half of the 1800s it was one of the world's most important whaling ports, and in 1857 its harbor counted 329 vessels. The downtown historic district, 11 city blocks and part of a twelfth west of the waterfront, keeps Federal and Greek Revival buildings put up mostly between 1790 and 1855, including Seamen's Bethel and the 1836 U.S. Customhouse. Frederick Douglass settled in the city in 1838 and lived there until 1841.

When whaling faded, cotton mills took over, and the Wamsutta Mills were for a time the world's largest weaving plant. The housing that followed spreads out from downtown: the County Street Historic District, laid out in the 18th century, holds City Hall, the library and large houses in Greek Revival and Gothic Revival styles, while Acushnet Heights keeps wood-frame houses built for factory workers from the 1860s on. Buttonwood Park and its zoo sit to the west, and Fort Taber Park occupies Clark's Point, the city's southern tip on Buzzards Bay.

Homes in New Bedford

The 2020 Census counted 42,781 housing units in New Bedford, about 2,063 per square mile, and 39,208 households. Older neighborhoods mix 19th-century wood-frame houses with downtown commercial and mixed-use blocks, and The Warren Group recorded 401 single-family sales in 2025.

Where you mightlive in New Bedford.

Downtown and the Waterfront

The historic district between Front, Elm and Commercial Streets and Acushnet Avenue, about 11 city blocks of Federal and Greek Revival buildings beside the harbor.

County Street

About 100 acres along County Street, roughly bounded by Acushnet, Page, Middle and Bedford Streets, with City Hall, the library and Greek Revival, Gothic Revival and Late Victorian buildings.

Acushnet Heights

About 20 acres around Clasky Common Park, bounded by Purchase, Linden and County Streets, with wood-frame houses built from the 1860s for factory workers and four 19th-century churches.

Clark's Point

The southernmost point of the city, where the Acushnet River meets Buzzards Bay, home to Fort Taber Park.

Getting around

Commuting from New Bedford

Commuter rail returned on March 24, 2025, after a gap since 1958. New Bedford and Church Street stations sit on the New Bedford branch of the Fall River/New Bedford Line, which runs through East Taunton, Middleborough, Brockton and Quincy to Boston's South Station; sample outbound weekday trips on the current timetable take about 1 hour 40 to 45 minutes, and the timetable also shows weekend trains to New Bedford. I-195 crosses the city toward Providence and Wareham, Route 18 links it to the waterfront, and Route 140 runs north to Route 24 in Taunton. SRTA runs local buses, and SeaStreak ferries sail from New Bedford to Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket, with separate service to Cuttyhunk Island.

Places to knowin New Bedford.

New Bedford Whaling National Historical Park

A fee-free national park in downtown New Bedford, with its visitor center at 33 William Street.

New Bedford Whaling Museum

Founded in 1903 by the Old Dartmouth Historical Society, at 18 Johnny Cake Hill.

Buttonwood Park Zoo

A city-owned, AZA-accredited zoo at 425 Hawthorn Street, with animals from five continents.

Fort Taber Park

A 47-acre park at Clark's Point with the 1857 stone fort, Endicott-era batteries and the Fort Taber/Fort Rodman military museum.

Seamen's Bethel

A white clapboard chapel in the downtown historic district, first built in 1832 and rebuilt after an 1867 fire.

What is the median home price in New Bedford, MA?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, the median single-family sale price in New Bedford for calendar year 2025 was $422,000, across 401 sales. In 2024 it was $395,000, across 412 sales.

Does New Bedford have commuter rail?

Yes. Since March 24, 2025, New Bedford station (536 Acushnet Avenue) and Church Street station (387 Church Street) have been served by the MBTA Fall River/New Bedford Line to Boston's South Station. The current timetable shows trains to New Bedford on weekdays and weekends.

Is there a ferry from New Bedford?

Yes. SeaStreak runs ferries from New Bedford to Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket, and the Cuttyhunk Ferry Company runs scheduled service to Cuttyhunk Island. Check the operators for seasonal schedules.

Which school district serves New Bedford?

New Bedford Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.
