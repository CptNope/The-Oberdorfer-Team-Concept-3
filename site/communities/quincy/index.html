Living in Quincy, MA

Between the harbor and the hills,a city with deep roots.

Quincy is a coastal city in Norfolk County, on Boston Harbor, and the birthplace of John Adams and John Quincy Adams. About a third of its homes are detached single-family houses, and most of the rest are in buildings with two or more units. Four Red Line stations and three commuter rail lines connect it to Boston.

About Quincy

Quincy calls itself the City of Presidents, and the reason is on Franklin Street: the houses where John Adams was born in 1735 and John Quincy Adams in 1767 still stand side by side there. The family's later home, the Old House at Peacefield, and its Stone Library are part of Adams National Historical Park. Quincy split from Braintree as a town in 1792 and became a city in 1888. Granite quarrying was its major industry for more than a century, and in 1826 the Granite Railway carried stone for the Bunker Hill Monument.

The shoreline runs from Squantum and Marina Bay around to Houghs Neck, with Wollaston Beach stretching about 2.3 miles along Quincy Shore Drive. Inland, the Blue Hills Reservation covers roughly a quarter of the city's land, and the old quarries in West Quincy are now a state reservation that climbers use. Four Red Line stations, North Quincy, Wollaston, Quincy Center and Quincy Adams, connect the city to Boston, and the Kingston, Greenbush and Fall River/New Bedford commuter rail lines all stop at Quincy Center.

Homes in Quincy

Housing Quincy, a 2022 study by the Metropolitan Area Planning Council, found that about a third of the city's housing units are detached single-family homes, two-thirds are in buildings with two or more units, and 63 percent were built after 1940. It describes the oceanfront areas of Squantum, Merrymount, Adams Shore and Houghs Neck as having predominantly older single-family housing.

Getting around

Commuting from Quincy

Quincy Center is a stop on three commuter rail lines and the Red Line, which has three more stations in the city. I-93 and US Route 1 run together through Quincy, joined by Route 3 at the Braintree Split, where the road becomes the Southeast Expressway.

  • Kingston Line: Quincy Center
  • Greenbush Line: Quincy Center
  • Fall River/New Bedford Line: Quincy Center
  • Red Line (subway): North Quincy, Wollaston, Quincy Center, Quincy Adams

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Places to knowin Quincy.

Adams National Historical Park

The Adams birthplaces on Franklin Street, the Old House at Peacefield and its Stone Library.

Wollaston Beach and Quincy Shores Reservation

About 2.3 miles of beach along Quincy Shore Drive, with Caddy Park and a jogging and biking path.

Quincy Quarries Reservation

Inactive granite quarries where large-scale American quarrying began, now used for hiking and top-rope climbing.

Blue Hills Reservation

More than 7,000 acres and 125 miles of trails, stretching from Quincy to Dedham and from Milton to Randolph.

Thomas Crane Public Library

The main library, designed by H. H. Richardson and completed in 1882, is a National Historic Landmark.

What is the median home price in Quincy, MA?

For calendar year 2025, The Warren Group reports a single-family median sale price of $706,250 in Quincy, across 418 sales. In 2024 the median was $700,000 on 364 sales. Source: The Warren Group, MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town.

Does Quincy have commuter rail?

Yes. The MBTA Kingston, Greenbush and Fall River/New Bedford lines stop at Quincy Center. The Red Line also serves North Quincy, Wollaston, Quincy Center and Quincy Adams.

Which school district serves Quincy?

Quincy Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

Is there a beach in Quincy?

Yes. Wollaston Beach runs about 2.3 miles along Quincy Shore Drive and is part of the state's Quincy Shores Reservation.
