Living in Auburn, MA

Auburn, where three highways meetand the first liquid-fueled rocket flew.

Auburn is a Worcester County town of 16,889 people (2020 Census), incorporated in 1778 as Ward and renamed Auburn in 1837. Its housing runs from 19th-century mill-village houses at Stoneville to bungalows and Colonial Revival houses of the 1930s and 1940s, and two former elementary schools became apartments in 2022. The Massachusetts Turnpike, I-290 and I-395 meet in town, WRTA buses run to Worcester, and the nearest MBTA station is Worcester Union Station on the Framingham/Worcester Line.

About Auburn

Auburn was incorporated on April 10, 1778, from parts of Worcester, Sutton, Leicester and Oxford, and named Ward for General Artemas Ward; most of it had been Worcester's South Parish, first settled in 1714. In 1837 the Post Office complained that Ward was too similar to the nearby town of Ware, and the town became Auburn. Wellington, Dark and Kettle Brooks flow north and east through town, and in the 19th century they powered mill villages: Jeremy Stone built a brick woolen mill and worker housing at Stoneville in 1834, and Pondville's mills made yarn and satinets. The Norwich and Worcester Railroad began service through Auburn in 1840, and by 1900 a streetcar line ran from Worcester along Southbridge and Central Streets toward Oxford.

On March 16, 1926, Robert H. Goddard launched the first liquid-fueled rocket from what was then the Asa Ward Farm. The site is now inside the town-owned Pakachoag Golf Course on Upland Street, where a granite marker beside the ninth fairway shows the spot; it was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1966. The town's commercial center is Drury Square, where Auburn and Southbridge Streets meet: one- to three-story buildings set back behind parking, with Auburn High School on the northwest side of Southbridge Street and the public library, Goddard Park and fire headquarters on the southeast side. The Auburn Mall, opened in 1971 at 385 Southbridge Street, sits on Route 12 near the junction of the Turnpike, I-290 and I-395.

The state approved Auburn's 2024 Housing Production Plan for July 9, 2024 to July 8, 2029, and certified it on July 21, 2025. Two former elementary schools have been redeveloped by Pennrose as mixed-income rental apartments: 55 units in the Mary D. Stone School at 10 Church Street, opened in May 2022, and 60 units in the Julia Bancroft School at 3 Vinal Street, whose opening was celebrated in December 2022. Under the state's MBTA Communities zoning law, Auburn is classed as an adjacent community that must zone for at least 750 multi-family units on at least 50 acres (the state counts 6,999 housing units in town in 2020), and the state's compliance list (as of October 21, 2026) includes Auburn.

Homes in Auburn

The state's MBTA Communities table counts 6,999 housing units in Auburn in 2020. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1984 survey found two-story, double-chimney multi-family worker houses and one-story duplexes of about 1860 at Stoneville, gable-end, side-passage houses built in large numbers around the mill villages in the 1880s and 1890s, a few triple-deckers, and bungalows and 1930s and 1940s Colonial Revival houses around the Center, Stoneville and Pakachoag.

Where you mightlive in Auburn.

Auburn Center

The civic center on Central Street, where the First Congregational church and first town hall replaced the burned meetinghouse in 1896 and the Merriam Library was built in 1910; bungalows and 1930s and 1940s Colonial Revival houses cluster nearby.

Drury Square

The commercial district where Auburn and Southbridge Streets meet, with low-rise shopping, Goddard Park, the library and fire headquarters, and the Auburn Mall within its study area.

Stoneville

A mill village in the north of town, built around Jeremy Stone's 1834 brick woolen mill, with two-story double-chimney worker houses, one-story duplexes from about 1860, St. Joseph's Church (1869) and a few triple-deckers.

Pondville

A mill village in the east of town where a saw and grist mill turned to yarn in the 1850s and made satinets and Union cassimeres during the Civil War.

West Auburn

A village around a tannery established in 1777 and taken over by the Warren family in 1807, with the 1825 West Auburn Schoolhouse at the corner of Central and Prospect.

Pakachoag

The hill area at the Worcester line, site of a 17th-century Christian Indian village, where bungalows and 1930s and 1940s Colonial Revival houses were built; the town's Pakachoag Golf Course holds the Goddard launch site.

Getting around

Commuting from Auburn

Auburn has no commuter rail station of its own; the nearest is Worcester Union Station, the western end of the Framingham/Worcester Line. I-290 begins in Auburn as the continuation of I-395, at a junction with the Turnpike, Route 12 and Route 20. WRTA Routes 27, 29 and 42 run from Worcester's Central Hub to Auburn Street, the Auburn Mall, Southbridge Street and Washington Street.

Places to knowin Auburn.

Goddard Rocket Launching Site

The spot where Robert Goddard launched the first liquid-fueled rocket on March 16, 1926, marked by granite beside the ninth fairway of the town-owned Pakachoag Golf Course on Upland Street; a National Historic Landmark since 1966.

Goddard Memorial Park

A park beside the fire department headquarters in Drury Square with a model of Goddard's rocket and a Polaris A-1 missile.

Auburn Historical Museum

The 1922 Tuttle Square School at 41 South Street, a Craftsman bungalow-style building leased to the Auburn Historical Society in 1999 and listed on the National Register in 2002; it houses the society's museum.

Eddy Pond

A 100-acre great pond and impoundment of Dark Brook, with a town-managed concrete boat ramp off South Street; fish include largemouth bass, chain pickerel and yellow perch.

Joseph Stone House

A First Period, one-and-a-half-story timber-frame house at 35 Stone Street, begun in 1729 with two bays added in 1790; listed on the National Register in 1986.

Auburn Mall

A single-level mall of 583,739 square feet at 385 Southbridge Street, opened in 1971 and owned by Simon Property Group, with Macy's and a Reliant Medical Group center as anchors.

What is the median home price in Auburn, MA?

The median single-family sale price in Auburn for calendar year 2025 was $475,000, across 188 sales, up from $429,000 across 189 sales in 2024, according to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report. The condo median was $345,000 across 19 sales.

Does Auburn have commuter rail?

No. Auburn has no MBTA station of its own; the nearest is Worcester Union Station, the western end of the Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Routes 27, 29 and 42 connect Auburn with Worcester's Central Hub.

Which school district serves Auburn?

Auburn Public Schools, which serves prekindergarten through grade 12. Review its profile with DESE, and confirm school assignment for any address with the district.

What is the property tax rate in Auburn?

For fiscal year 2026 the town lists a residential rate of $14.24 per $1,000 of assessed value and a commercial rate of $15.83, according to the Auburn Assessing Division.

Which highways meet in Auburn?

The Massachusetts Turnpike (I-90), I-290 and I-395 meet in Auburn, where I-290 begins as the continuation of I-395. Route 12 (Southbridge Street) and US Route 20 also cross the town.

Where did Robert Goddard launch his rocket?

At what was then the Asa Ward Farm in Auburn, on March 16, 1926. The launch site is inside the town-owned Pakachoag Golf Course on Upland Street, marked beside the ninth fairway, and has been a National Historic Landmark since 1966.
