Living in Wakefield, MA

A lake to walk around,a town to come home to.

Wakefield is a Middlesex County town about 10 miles north of Boston, centered on Lake Quannapowitt and a downtown along Main Street. Single-family detached homes make up 55 percent of its housing, and about a third of its structures predate World War II. The Haverhill Line stops at Wakefield and Greenwood stations.

An evening on Lake Quannapowitt. Photo: RedSoxFan274 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, cropped.

About Wakefield

Wakefield is a town of about 27,000 people, 10 miles north of Boston, and it is laid out around Lake Quannapowitt. A paved loop of about 3.6 miles circles the lake, and Wakefield Common sits at its southern end. From there, downtown runs south along Main Street toward Crystal Lake, the town's drinking-water reservoir. The town began as part of Reading. Its South Parish broke off as South Reading in 1812, and in 1868 the town took the name of Cyrus Wakefield, who gave land and money for a new town hall.

Two Haverhill Line stations, Wakefield and Greenwood, serve the town, and I-95/Route 128 runs along its northwest edge. Greenwood covers nearly all of southern Wakefield, while Montrose, Woodville and Lakeside fill in the east and north. On Saturdays from June through October, the farmers market sets up on North Avenue by Hall Park, on the lake's shore. Breakheart Reservation, a state park with two lakes and a swimming beach at Pearce Lake, has an entrance on Hemlock Road, the one closer to the beach.

Homes in Wakefield

The town's Housing Production Plan for FY2026-2030 reports that single-family detached homes are 55 percent of all housing units, down from 62 percent in 2011, and that about a third of structures were built before World War II. Two-family homes, buildings of 20 to 49 units, and three- and four-unit buildings are the next most common types.

Getting around

Commuting from Wakefield

The Haverhill Line stops at Wakefield and Greenwood on the way to North Station, and MBTA bus route 137 also serves the town. I-95/Route 128 runs along the northwest border with exits in town, Route 129 runs through as Main Street, and the town notes that it sits near the junction of I-93 and I-95/128.

Places to knowin Wakefield.

Lake Quannapowitt

A lake with a paved loop of about 3.6 miles, popular for walking, running and recreation.

Wakefield Common

The town green on the south shore of Lake Quannapowitt, where downtown begins.

Hall Park and the Wakefield Farmers Market

A lakeshore park on North Avenue; the market runs Saturdays, 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., from mid-June through October.

Breakheart Reservation

A DCR park with two lakes, a beach at Pearce Lake and an extensive trail system, entered from Hemlock Road.

Lucius Beebe Memorial Library

The public library at Main and Avon Streets downtown, opened in 1923.

What is the median home price in Wakefield, MA?

For calendar year 2025, The Warren Group reports a single-family median sale price of $830,000 in Wakefield, across 195 sales. In 2024 the median was $822,500 on 178 sales. Source: The Warren Group, MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town.

Does Wakefield have commuter rail?

Yes. The MBTA Haverhill Line stops at Wakefield and Greenwood stations on its way to North Station in Boston.

Which school district serves Wakefield?

Wakefield Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

How long is the walk around Lake Quannapowitt?

The town describes the paved loop around the lake as approximately 3.6 miles.
