Living in Marlborough, MA

A shoe-making city at the crossroadsof I-495 and I-290.

Marlborough is a city of 41,793 people (2020 Census) in Middlesex County, where I-495 meets the eastern end of I-290. Its downtown grew from two 1800s shoe-making villages, and its housing includes three-deckers, double houses and converted factory condominiums. The city has no rail station; buses connect to Southborough and Framingham on the Framingham/Worcester Line.

About Marlborough

Families from Sudbury petitioned for a new town in 1656, and Marlborough was incorporated in 1660. The early settlement was largely destroyed during King Philip's War, then rebuilt along the Post Road, where George Washington stopped at Williams Tavern in 1789. Shoemaking defined the 1800s. Samuel and Joseph Boyd opened the first shoe business in 1836, factory output rose into the millions of dollars by 1865, and Marlborough incorporated as a city in 1890. Its land once also included today's Northborough, Southborough, Westborough, Berlin and Hudson.

Downtown took shape from two villages, an East Village along Main and Maple Streets and a West Village along Pleasant and Main, which grew together once the railroad ran between them. In 2014 the city created a 50-acre Downtown Village district to encourage mixed-use building, and some old shoe factories and churches nearby are now condominiums. The Assabet River Rail Trail heads north toward Hudson past an overlook above Fort Meadow Reservoir, and the 615-acre Desert Natural Area protects pitch pine and scrub forest on the Sudbury line.

Homes in Marlborough

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey of Marlborough records central-chimney Colonial houses, Greek Revival and Italianate side-hall houses and cottages, wood-frame row and double houses, and three-deckers and apartment houses in the West Village and along Mechanic Street, with Dutch Colonial and bungalow houses from the early 1900s. The city's economic development office lists former factories and churches converted to condominiums.

Getting around

Commuting from Marlborough

Marlborough has no commuter rail station of its own; MWRTA bus routes connect to the Southborough and Framingham stations on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. I-495, US Route 20 and Route 85 pass through the city, and I-290 has its eastern end here.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line (in neighboring towns): Southborough, Framingham

Things to doMarlborough

Places to knowin Marlborough.

Assabet River Rail Trail

A paved trail from Lincoln Street north into Hudson, passing under Route 85 and over an overlook above Fort Meadow Reservoir; parking at 56 Jefferson Street.

Desert Natural Area

A 615-acre reservation on the Sudbury line with rare pitch pine and scrub forest on sandy glacial soils, reached from Route 20 by Concord Road.

Ghiloni Nature Trail and State Forest

Pine woods along Broadmeadow Brook with an interpretive loop, reached from Ghiloni Park off Concord Road.

Callahan State Park

More than 800 acres of fields, ponds and marked trails shared with Framingham, listed among Marlborough's conservation lands.

Lake Williams Trail

One of the city's mapped conservation trails, at the lake on the old Post Road.

What is the median home price in Marlborough, MA?

The median single-family sale price in Marlborough was $606,500 in calendar year 2025, up from $592,000 in 2024, across 230 sales (233 in 2024), according to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Does Marlborough have commuter rail?

Not within the city. MWRTA bus routes connect Marlborough to the Southborough and Framingham stations on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line.

Which school district serves Marlborough?

Marlborough Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.
