Living in Barnstable, MA

Seven villages between two shores,from the Great Marsh to the Hyannis ferries.

Barnstable is the seat of Barnstable County, a town of 48,916 people (2020 Census) made up of seven villages that run from Cape Cod Bay to Nantucket Sound. Detached single-family homes make up 84.8 percent of its residential properties, and the town's housing plan counts at least 23 percent of its housing units as second homes. Hyannis holds the town's transit links: the seasonal CapeFLYER train from Boston ends at the Hyannis Transportation Center, and ferries leave Hyannis for Nantucket and Martha's Vineyard. U.S. Route 6 crosses the town as a four-lane freeway.

About Barnstable

Bartholomew Gosnold explored this shore in 1602, and English settlers arrived on June 4, 1638, making Barnstable one of the first towns of Plymouth Colony; it was incorporated in 1639. The north side still follows the Old King's Highway, Route 6A, through Barnstable village and West Barnstable, where the 1644 house that forms the original part of the Sturgis Library is the oldest building housing a public library in the United States, and the Olde Colonial Courthouse recalls a 1774 protest led by James Otis and Samuel Adams. Behind them, Sandy Neck runs about six and a half miles along Cape Cod Bay, backed by salt marsh, with Sandy Neck Light at its tip.

The south side faces Nantucket Sound. Hyannis holds the historic Main Street, a large natural harbor and the ferry docks, along with Barnstable Municipal Airport and the Hyannis Transportation Center. West along Route 28, Centerville reaches Craigville Beach, Osterville keeps the Crosby boatbuilding story in its historical museum, and Cotuit sits on Cotuit Bay, the home water of the Cotuit oyster. Inland, Marstons Mills takes its name from Benjamin Marston's fulling mill and keeps a herring run, kettle ponds and a grass airfield dating to 1929. Hyannis is also home to Cape Cod Hospital, the largest medical facility on Cape Cod, and the Cape Cod Central Railroad runs seasonal excursions from there to Sandwich and Sagamore.

Barnstable's 2025 Housing Production Plan counts detached single-family homes as 84.8 percent of residential properties and at least 23 percent of housing units as second homes; rental housing is 24 percent of the stock. Multifamily and mixed-use buildings are allowed by right on only two percent of the town's net land area, and accessory dwelling units were recently allowed by right in all zoning districts. The town had 1,485 units on the state's Subsidized Housing Inventory, 6.73 percent of its 2020 year-round units, against a 10 percent goal. In Hyannis, the Town Council approved new Downtown Hyannis zoning on February 2, 2023, replacing the Hyannis Village districts with seven form-based and hybrid districts that allow buildings of up to four stories and are meant to allow compact housing by right. Barnstable is not one of the 177 MBTA communities.

Homes in Barnstable

Barnstable's 2025 Housing Production Plan executive summary counts detached single-family homes as 84.8 percent of residential properties, and reports that at least 23 percent of the town's housing units are second homes. Hyannis holds about one-fifth of all housing units; single-family homes are 59 percent of its units, and most of its units are rented. The plan names town-owned sites in Marstons Mills, among others, for new year-round housing.

Neighborhoods and villagesBarnstable

Where you mightlive in Barnstable.

Hyannis

The largest village, with the historic Main Street, the harbor and ferry docks, the Hyannis Transportation Center and Barnstable Municipal Airport.

Barnstable Village

The north-side village along the Old King's Highway (Route 6A), with a working harbor, the county complex, the Sturgis Library and the Olde Colonial Courthouse.

West Barnstable

A northwestern village on Route 6A whose land is largely Sandy Neck, the Great Marsh and the West Barnstable Conservation Area, centered on the West Parish meetinghouse of 1717 to 1718.

Centerville

A south-side village with the Centerville Historic District, the Centerville Historical Museum and Craigville Beach in its Craigville neighborhood.

Osterville

A village facing Nantucket Sound, with West Bay, the Wianno neighborhood by East Bay and the Crosby boatbuilding tradition.

Cotuit

The village on Cotuit Bay, midway between Falmouth and Hyannis, where the Cotuit Kettleers play Cape Cod Baseball League games at Lowell Park.

Marstons Mills

An inland village on glacial plains, with the Marstons Mills River, a herring run, kettle ponds such as Mystic Lake and Hamblin Pond, and saltwater access only at Prince Cove.

Getting around

Commuting from Barnstable

Barnstable has no year-round passenger rail; the nearest year-round MBTA Commuter Rail stations are Middleborough and Kingston. In summer, the CapeFLYER runs weekends only from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day between Boston's South Station and the Hyannis Transportation Center, with stops including Braintree, Brockton, Wareham Village, Buzzards Bay and Bourne. The Hyannis Transportation Center is also the terminal for Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority buses, including the Sealine to Falmouth and Woods Hole, and for Plymouth & Brockton and Peter Pan intercity buses. From Hyannis, the Steamship Authority runs year-round ferries to Nantucket and a seasonal high-speed passenger ferry, and Hy-Line runs passenger ferries to Nantucket and a seasonal catamaran to Martha's Vineyard. Barnstable Municipal Airport in Hyannis has scheduled flights to the islands and Boston. U.S. Route 6 crosses the town as a four-lane freeway, and Routes 132 and 149 link Route 6A to Route 28.

Things to doBarnstable

Places to knowin Barnstable.

Sandy Neck

A public barrier beach about 6.5 miles long on Cape Cod Bay, backed by salt marsh, with Sandy Neck Light's 1857 brick tower at Beach Point.

John F. Kennedy Hyannis Museum

A museum at 397 Main Street, Hyannis, on President Kennedy, his family and their connection to Cape Cod; hours change by season.

Sturgis Library

A library at 3090 Main Street (Route 6A) in Barnstable Village whose building is the oldest structure housing a public library in the United States.

Osterville Historical Museum

A museum at 155 West Bay Road, founded in 1931, with the Cammett, Parker and Jelly houses and more than a dozen full-sized Crosby wooden boats.

Hyannis Harbor and Main Street

A large natural harbor, the Cape's largest recreational boating port, beside the historic downtown Main Street shopping district.

Lowell Park, Cotuit

The summer home field of the Cotuit Kettleers of the Cape Cod Baseball League.

Cahoon Museum of American Art

A museum in the Crocker house at 4676 Falmouth Road (Route 28) in Cotuit, with American art from the 18th century to the present, including the folk art and painted furniture of Ralph and Martha Cahoon.

West Parish Meetinghouse

The restored meetinghouse in West Barnstable, built after the church split into East and West parishes in 1717, and still home to the West Parish of Barnstable.

What is the median home price in Barnstable, MA?

The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table reports Barnstable by village rather than as one town. For calendar year 2025, the median single-family sale price was $590,000 in Hyannis (131 sales), $664,450 in Centerville (142 sales), $665,000 in Marstons Mills (101 sales), $814,162 in Cotuit (68 sales), $885,000 in the row labeled Barnstable (60 sales) and $1,165,000 in Osterville (51 sales). West Barnstable has no separate row.

Does Barnstable have commuter rail?

Not year-round. The seasonal CapeFLYER runs weekends from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day between Boston's South Station and the Hyannis Transportation Center. The nearest year-round MBTA Commuter Rail stations are Middleborough and Kingston.

Where do the island ferries leave from in Barnstable?

From Hyannis. The Steamship Authority runs ferries to Nantucket year-round, plus a seasonal high-speed passenger ferry, and Hy-Line runs passenger ferries to Nantucket and a seasonal catamaran to Martha's Vineyard.

Which school district serves Barnstable?

Barnstable Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

What is the property tax rate in Barnstable, MA?

According to the Massachusetts Division of Local Services, Barnstable's FY2026 residential property tax rate is $6.80 per $1,000 of assessed value, and commercial, industrial and personal property pay $5.94. Each property also pays its fire district's rate (FY2026, per $1,000: Barnstable $2.81, Cotuit $2.07, Centerville-Osterville-Marstons Mills $1.15, Hyannis $2.40 residential, West Barnstable $2.66) and a 3% Community Preservation surcharge on the town tax. Qualifying owner-occupants get a 30% residential exemption of $274,787 of assessed value, according to the Town of Barnstable.

What has Barnstable changed in its zoning for housing?

The Town Council approved new Downtown Hyannis zoning on February 2, 2023, allowing buildings of up to four stories and aiming to allow compact housing by right, and the town's 2025 Housing Production Plan reports that accessory dwelling units are now allowed by right in all zoning districts. Barnstable is not subject to the state's MBTA Communities law.
