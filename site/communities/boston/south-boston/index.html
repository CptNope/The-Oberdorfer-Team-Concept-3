Living in South Boston, Boston, MA

Dorchester Heights at the center,Castle Island and three miles of beach and parkland at the end of Broadway.

South Boston is a Boston neighborhood on the former Dorchester Neck, south and east of Fort Point Channel along Dorchester Bay, annexed to Boston in 1804. Its housing includes 19th-century brick and mansard row houses and flat-roofed triple-deckers, and the Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 37,917 people there in 2020, with the South Boston Waterfront counted separately. The Red Line stops at Broadway and Andrew, and Castle Island anchors three miles of shoreline parks.

About South Boston

Where it is

South Boston lies south and east of Fort Point Channel, along Dorchester Bay, with Castle Island beside City Point and Andrew station on its west side. The Seaport, or South Boston Waterfront, north of the residential streets is treated by the city's planners as a separate neighborhood.

South Boston was Dorchester Neck, a narrow peninsula belonging to the town of Dorchester. On the night of March 4, 1776, Continental troops fortified Dorchester Heights, and the British evacuated Boston on March 17. Boston annexed the peninsula in 1804, bridges over Fort Point Channel tied it to downtown, and landfill widened its connection to the mainland. In 1822 land on the peninsula was set aside for public institutions, including a jail and an almshouse; after they moved, East Broadway was extended to the end of the peninsula in 1854 and 4.5 acres became Independence Square.

The streets climb to Dorchester Heights, the highest area of South Boston. Near the top of Telegraph Hill, an 1849 reservoir was later filled and replaced by South Boston High School in 1901, and the rest of the summit was laid out in the 1850s as Thomas Park, now centered on a 115-foot marble monument completed in 1902. Mansard and brick row houses, some with marble and sandstone fronts, went up around Independence Square in the fifteen years after the Civil War. At City Point the granite Fort Independence, built between 1833 and 1848, stands on Castle Island, which the city joined to the mainland by filling the flats between 1890 and the 1920s; Day Boulevard now runs past Pleasure Bay, M Street Beach and Carson Beach.

Recent city planning has looked at the neighborhood's edges. The Boston Planning & Development Agency completed PLAN: South Boston Dorchester Avenue in December 2016, with the projects reviewed under it mostly on Dorchester Avenue, Old Colony Avenue and nearby streets, and a follow-up transportation plan was finalized in 2021. To the north, the waterfront along Fort Point Channel and Boston Harbor was the focus of the city's 1,000-acre Innovation District plan announced in 2010, and the city's research tables report it as the separate South Boston Waterfront neighborhood.

Homes in South Boston

South Boston's housing includes mansard and brick row houses from the 1860s and 1870s around Independence Square and along East Broadway, and triple-deckers, which here were mostly built with flat roofs. The Boston Housing Authority's Mary Ellen McCormack (1936) and Old Colony (1941) developments are in the neighborhood, and the second phase of Old Colony's redevelopment was completed in 2014. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 17,109 occupied housing units, 43.4% of them owner-occupied, compared with 35.3% citywide.

Within the neighborhoodSouth Boston

Corners ofSouth Boston.

City Point

The eastern end of the peninsula near Castle Island, reached by East Broadway and Day Boulevard, where MBTA bus routes 7, 9, 10 and 11 end at the City Point bus terminal.

Independence Square

A 4.5-acre square on East Broadway laid out in 1854, ringed by mansard and brick row houses built mostly in the fifteen years after the Civil War, including the Harrison Loring House at 789 East Broadway, the first house on the square.

Dorchester Heights and Telegraph Hill

The highest area of South Boston, with Thomas Park and the Dorchester Heights Monument on the summit of Telegraph Hill and South Boston High School on the site of an 1849 reservoir; a 40-acre area centered on Thomas Park was listed on the National Register as the Dorchester Heights Historic District in 2001.

West Broadway and Andrew Square

The western side along Broadway and Dorchester Avenue, served by the Broadway and Andrew Red Line stations; Andrew Square is where Dorchester Avenue, Dorchester Street, Southampton Street and Boston Street meet.

Getting around

Commuting from South Boston

The Red Line's Broadway station, at Dorchester Avenue and Broadway, opened in 1917, and Andrew station, at Andrew Square, in 1918; both run north to South Station, where the Fairmount, Needham, Providence/Stoughton and other south-side commuter rail lines end. MBTA bus routes 7, 9, 10 and 11 cross the neighborhood to the City Point terminal, and Andrew connects with routes including the 10, 16, 17, 18 and 171. The Silver Line serves the separate Waterfront area, and the Big Dig put I-93 below ground.

Schools

Public schools

South Boston is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin South Boston.

Castle Island and Fort Independence

A DCR park at 2010 Day Boulevard, where the Castle Island Loop circles the granite Fort Independence, built from 1833 and listed on the National Register in 1970; open sunrise to sunset.

Pleasure Bay, M Street Beach and Carson Beach

Part of a three-mile stretch of beach and parkland along Day Boulevard, with an accessible loop around Pleasure Bay; dogs are not allowed on the beach from May 1 through September 15.

Dorchester Heights Monument

A white marble Georgian Revival tower by Peabody and Stearns, completed in 1902 to mark the fortification of March 4, 1776, and stewarded by the National Park Service as part of Boston National Historical Park.

Thomas Park

A roughly elliptical park of walks and ornamental plantings laid out in the 1850s on the summit of Telegraph Hill on Dorchester Heights, centered on the monument.

Independence Square

A 4.5-acre park created in 1854 on East Broadway, which received 150 trees and granite steps in 1874.

Saint Augustine Chapel

A chapel completed in 1819 and described as the oldest Catholic church in Massachusetts.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in South Boston?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists South Boston on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $1,031,500, across 62 sales. In 2024 it was $1,025,000, across 61 sales. Condominium figures for South Boston could not be read from the table. The same report's condominium median for South Boston was $837,000 in 2025, across 485 sales, against $850,000 across 458 sales in 2024.

Does South Boston have commuter rail?

No commuter rail station is in South Boston itself. The Red Line runs from Broadway and Andrew stations to South Station, where the Fairmount, Needham, Providence/Stoughton and other south-side commuter rail lines end.

Which school district serves South Boston?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

Is the Seaport part of South Boston?

Historically the waterfront north of the residential streets is part of South Boston, but the city's planners tabulate it as a separate neighborhood, the South Boston Waterfront. The population and housing figures in this guide exclude it.
