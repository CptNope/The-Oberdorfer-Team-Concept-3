Living in Allston, Boston, MA

Brick apartments on Commonwealth Avenue,former rail yards and Harvard's Lower Allston campus on the Charles.

Allston is a Boston neighborhood on the Charles River, the eastern part of the former town of Brighton, named in 1868 when its railroad station and post office took the name of the painter Washington Allston; it became part of Boston with Brighton in 1874. It had 24,904 residents in the 2020 Census, as tabulated by the Boston Planning Department. Its housing runs from brick apartment buildings on Commonwealth Avenue to triple-deckers and Victorian houses, and the Green Line B branch runs along Commonwealth Avenue.

About Allston

Where it is

The Charles River forms Allston's northern and eastern edges, across from Cambridge; it borders Brighton, generally along Everett, Gordon and Kelton Streets, as well as the Fenway-Kenmore area and the town of Brookline. The Massachusetts Turnpike crosses it, with Lower (North) Allston north of Cambridge Street and the Turnpike.

Allston grew up as the eastern part of Little Cambridge, which became the town of Brighton in 1807. The Boston and Albany Railroad opened a new depot here in 1867, and in 1868 the station and post office in Brighton's eastern portion took the name Allston, after the painter Washington Allston. Allston became part of Boston when Brighton was annexed in 1874, and it has never been a separate municipality. Railroad and livestock operations shaped the area; the livestock trade ended by the mid-20th century, and the Beacon Park rail yard, later CSX's Beacon Park Yard, stayed in use until 2013. Streetcar service along Commonwealth Avenue began on May 18, 1896, from Governors Square to Brighton Avenue.

Large institutions arrived in the 20th century. Harvard Stadium, an early large structure in reinforced concrete, opened on November 14, 1903, and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987. Harvard Business School, founded in 1908, has its campus in Lower Allston; its Morgan Hall dates from 1927. Braves Field opened on August 18, 1915, at Commonwealth Avenue and Babcock Street on the site of the Allston Golf Club, and Boston University bought it on July 30, 1953; Boston University's schools and facilities now extend from Kenmore Square to Packard's Corner. The Massachusetts Turnpike Extension opened through Allston in 1964 and 1965, largely on the old Boston and Albany right-of-way. The Boston Planning Department counts 358 acres of academic space in Allston, the most of any Boston neighborhood, and notes that Harvard owns more land in Boston than in Cambridge.

Brick apartment buildings line Commonwealth Avenue and nearby streets, wooden triple-deckers stand along Brighton Avenue toward Brighton, and Lower Allston has Victorian single- and multi-family houses built between the 1890s and 1920s. Harvard's Science and Engineering Complex opened in 2020. In 2019 work began on a bus lane from Union Square to Packard's Corner. The largest pending change is MassDOT's I-90 Allston Multimodal Project, which is progressing the design to replace the I-90 viaduct in Allston; MassDOT says cost and engineering analyses by an independent review team are to be examined in 2026. The plans include West Station, proposed at Ashford Street in the former Beacon Park Yard on the Framingham/Worcester Line.

Homes in Allston

Allston's housing is mostly brick apartment buildings, especially on Commonwealth Avenue and nearby streets, with wooden triple-deckers along Brighton Avenue and Victorian single- and multi-family houses from the 1890s to the 1920s in Lower Allston. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 8,040 occupied housing units in Allston, 14.0% of them owner-occupied and 86.0% renter-occupied, compared with 35.3% owner-occupied citywide.

Corners ofAllston.

Lower Allston (North Allston)

The section north of Cambridge Street and the Turnpike, from Everett Street east to the Charles River, centered on Allston Square at Western Avenue and North Harvard Street; Harvard Business School and most of Harvard's athletic facilities are here.

Union Square

Home to the Allston fire station (Engine 41 and Ladder 14), and the start of a bus lane to Packard's Corner built from 2019.

Harvard Avenue and Allston Village

The commercial blocks between Commonwealth Avenue and Cambridge Street, with shops and restaurants and a Green Line stop at Harvard Avenue.

Packard's Corner

Named after Packard's Sales Stable and Riding School, with a Green Line stop; Boston University's schools and facilities extend from Kenmore Square to here.

Getting around

Commuting from Allston

The Green Line B branch runs along Commonwealth Avenue, with stops from Babcock Street to Allston Street, toward Kenmore and downtown. MBTA bus route 57 follows the path of the former A branch along Brighton Avenue, and routes 64, 66, 70 and 86 also serve Allston. Allston has no commuter rail station today; Boston Landing station in Brighton is on the Framingham/Worcester Line, and West Station is proposed in the former Beacon Park Yard. The Massachusetts Turnpike crosses the neighborhood, and the Paul Dudley White Bicycle Path follows the Charles River along its northern and eastern edges.

Places to knowin Allston.

Harvard Stadium

Opened on November 14, 1903, on North Harvard Street, a pioneering large structure in reinforced concrete that seats just over 25,000; a National Historic Landmark since 1987.

Harvard Business School

Founded in 1908, with its campus in Lower Allston; Morgan Hall (1927) and Loeb House (1940) were designed by McKim, Mead & White.

Harvard Science and Engineering Complex

Home to Harvard's John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences, opened in 2020 next to Harvard Business School and the Harvard Innovation Labs.

Nickerson Field

Boston University's field at 285 Babcock Street, on the site of Braves Field, the Boston Braves' ballpark from 1915 to 1952; the entry gate, right field pavilion and old ticket office survive.

Ringer Park

A city park established in 1916, with a Little League field, tennis and basketball courts, a playground, walking paths and a wooded area with trails; design work on its renovation was paused in September 2023 while the city sought grants.

Charles River and the Paul Dudley White Bicycle Path

The river and the Paul Dudley White Bicycle Path border Allston to the north and east, used for canoeing, running and biking.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Allston?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Allston on its own row, the median condominium sale price for calendar year 2025 was $516,250, across 66 sales, against $585,000 across 69 sales in 2024. The single-family median was $889,900 in 2025, but that row is a very small sample: 11 sales in 2025 and 6 in 2024 (when the median was $1,007,500).

Does Allston have commuter rail?

Not today. Boston Landing station in Brighton is on the Framingham/Worcester Line. West Station is proposed at Ashford Street in the former Beacon Park Yard as part of the I-90 Allston Multimodal Project, but it has no confirmed opening date. Allston is served by the Green Line B branch along Commonwealth Avenue and several MBTA bus routes.

Which school district serves Allston?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is by address and set by the district under its own rules; review the district's profile with DESE.

What is the I-90 Allston Multimodal Project?

A MassDOT project to replace the I-90 (Massachusetts Turnpike) viaduct in Allston. Design work is ongoing, and MassDOT says cost and engineering analyses by an independent review team are to be examined in 2026 to decide how to deliver the most transportation benefits with available resources.
