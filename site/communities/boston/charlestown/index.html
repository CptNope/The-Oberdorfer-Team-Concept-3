Living in Charlestown, Boston, MA

Red-brick rows on Breed's Hill,and a navy yard turned to new uses on the harbor.

Charlestown is Boston's oldest neighborhood, founded in 1629 on a peninsula between the Charles and Mystic Rivers and annexed to Boston in 1874, with 19,120 residents in 2020 (2020 Census, Boston Planning Department tabulation). Its housing is largely red-brick row houses on Bunker Hill and Breed's Hill, plus buildings of the former Navy Yard converted to residential and other uses. The Orange Line stops at Community College and Sullivan Square, and the MBTA's Charlestown Ferry runs from the Navy Yard to Long Wharf.

About Charlestown

Where it is

A peninsula north of the Charles River, opposite downtown Boston, bordered by the Mystic River and Boston Harbor, with I-93 along its western edge, the Navy Yard on the harbor, and Sullivan Square where the Charlestown Neck once joined it to the mainland.

Charlestown was laid out by Thomas Graves and settled in 1629, and the City of Boston calls it the city's oldest neighborhood; John Winthrop's company stopped here in 1630 before crossing the Charles River to found Boston. On June 17, 1775, the Battle of Bunker Hill was fought on Breed's Hill, and British forces burned almost the whole town. The first bridge to Boston opened in 1786, and the Navy Yard opened in 1800. Charlestown voters approved annexation on October 7, 1873, by 2,240 to 1,947, and it became part of Boston on January 5, 1874.

The peninsula rises to two small hills, Bunker Hill to the north and Breed's Hill, both covered mostly in housing, much of it red-brick row houses. The 221-foot granite Bunker Hill Monument, reached by 294 steps, was dedicated on Breed's Hill in 1843. City Square, in the southern part of the neighborhood, was the site of the city hall before annexation, and Sullivan Square sits where landfill erased the narrow Charlestown Neck. The Navy Yard closed on July 1, 1974; the National Park Service holds 25 acres as part of Boston National Historical Park, and most of the redeveloped buildings were converted to office, commercial and residential use. The Orange Line has run through Charlestown since April 7, 1975, when Community College and Sullivan Square stations replaced the City Square and Thompson Square stations of the Charlestown Elevated, which had opened in 1901.

Two large changes are under way. The Bunker Hill Housing redevelopment, a partnership of the Boston Housing Authority, the Joseph J. Corcoran Company, Leggat McCall and the Charlestown Resident Alliance, is replacing 1,100 public housing units with 2,699 public, income-restricted and market-rate rental units, with 73,000 square feet of retail and civic space; its first building broke ground in June 2023, and the next, a nine-story building of 265 units, was expected to start in mid to late 2025. On September 28, 2023, after four years of work, the Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted PLAN: Charlestown, which sets out how new growth can fit along the Rutherford Avenue corridor and in Sullivan Square and proposes new zoning to allow more housing and retail in existing industrial areas.

Homes in Charlestown

Charlestown's housing stock is largely red-brick row houses, many near the waterfront, along with homes in converted Navy Yard buildings and the Bunker Hill public housing now being rebuilt. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2014 to 2018 American Community Survey counts 9,536 housing units, 9,050 of them occupied; 46.1% of occupied units were owner occupied and 53.9% renter occupied.

Corners ofCharlestown.

Charlestown Navy Yard

The former naval shipyard on the harbor, open from 1800 to 1974; 25 acres are part of Boston National Historical Park and most of the rest has been redeveloped for office, commercial and residential use.

Monument Square and Breed's Hill

The hilltop around the Bunker Hill Monument, whose museum stands at 43 Monument Square.

City Square

In the southern part of the neighborhood, the site of the city hall before annexation; US 1 passes below City Square and Paul Revere Park.

Sullivan Square

Where the Charlestown Neck once met the mainland, with an Orange Line station and busway; one of the focus areas of PLAN: Charlestown, adopted by the Boston Planning & Development Agency board on September 28, 2023.

Getting around

Commuting from Charlestown

The Orange Line stops at Community College, on Austin Street beneath the I-93 viaduct, and at Sullivan Square, which has a busway; routes 92 and 93 run from Sullivan Square to downtown. The MBTA's Charlestown Ferry links the Navy Yard with Long Wharf. Charlestown has no commuter rail station. I-93 and US 1, by way of the Tobin Bridge, pass through.

  • Orange Line: Community College, Sullivan Square
  • Charlestown Ferry: Charlestown (Navy Yard), Long Wharf (South)

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Places to knowin Charlestown.

Bunker Hill Monument

A 221-foot granite obelisk on Breed's Hill marking the battle of June 17, 1775, dedicated in 1843 and climbed by a spiral stair of 294 steps.

USS Constitution

Launched in 1797 and known as Old Ironsides, the world's oldest commissioned warship still afloat, usually berthed at Pier 1 of the Navy Yard and open to visitors year round with free tours.

USS Cassin Young

A Fletcher-class destroyer that served from 1943 to 1960, berthed at the Navy Yard, which the National Park Service stewards in partnership with the U.S. Navy and the USS Constitution Museum; the Navy Yard grounds are open 24 hours a day.

Paul Revere Park

A five-acre park along the Charles River between the Freedom Trail and the Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge, built as Big Dig mitigation and first opened in 1999.

Phipps Street Burying Ground

A 1.8-acre burial ground created in 1630, where John Harvard is buried; listed on the National Register in 1974.

Winthrop Square (Training Field)

A historic park and former militia training field, home to the Charlestown Civil War Memorial.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Charlestown?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, the median single-family sale price on its Charlestown row for calendar year 2025 was $1,729,250, across 48 sales. In 2024 it was $1,550,000, across 41 sales. Charlestown's condo row could not be read from the PDF.

Does Charlestown have commuter rail?

No. Charlestown is served by the Orange Line at Community College and Sullivan Square, MBTA buses 92 and 93 from Sullivan Square, and the Charlestown Ferry from the Navy Yard to Long Wharf.

Which school district serves Charlestown?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district under its own citywide rules; review the district's profile with DESE.

What is happening with the Bunker Hill housing redevelopment?

The Boston Housing Authority and its private partners are replacing 1,100 public housing units with 2,699 public, income-restricted and market-rate rental units in phases. The first building broke ground in June 2023, according to the City of Boston.
