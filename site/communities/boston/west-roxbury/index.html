Living in West Roxbury, Boston, MA

Centre Street, three Needham Line stops,and Bellevue Hill, the city's highest natural point.

West Roxbury is Boston's southwestern neighborhood, bordering Brookline, Newton, Needham and Dedham, set off from Roxbury as its own town in 1851 and annexed to Boston in 1874. Its housing is mostly single-family homes with some two-family houses, and the Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 31,561 people there in 2020. Three Needham Line stations, Bellevue, Highland and West Roxbury, run to South Station, and Centre Street is the main business district.

About West Roxbury

Where it is

West Roxbury is the southwest corner of Boston: Roslindale and Jamaica Plain lie to the northeast, Chestnut Hill and Brookline to the north, Newton to the northwest, Dedham and Needham to the southwest, and Hyde Park to the southeast; VFW Parkway runs along its western side.

West Roxbury was settled in 1630 as farmland, part of the town of Roxbury, and the Westerly Burying Ground on Centre Street dates to 1683. In 1841 George and Sophia Ripley founded Brook Farm, a transcendentalist community, at the Ellis Farm on what is now Baker Street; Nathaniel Hawthorne's stay there became The Blithedale Romance. The Boston and Providence Railroad opened its West Roxbury Branch from Forest Hills to Dedham in 1849, and on May 24, 1851, West Roxbury, then including Jamaica Plain, was incorporated as a separate town. It became part of Boston on January 5, 1874.

Centre Street is the spine of the neighborhood and its business district, with the Theodore Parker Church, a Boston Landmark whose present building dates from 1900, and the Westerly Burying Ground along it. Bellevue Hill, bounded roughly by LaGrange Street, Centre Street and the West Roxbury Parkway, rises to 330 feet, the highest natural point in Boston and Suffolk County, and carries the Bellevue Standpipe. In 1906 the New Haven Railroad extended the branch from West Roxbury to Needham Junction, the route of today's Needham Line.

Open land frames the streets. Boston's 2008-2014 Open Space Plan counted 645 acres of protected open space in West Roxbury, among them the 105-acre Millennium Park, built on the former Gardner Street landfill with material excavated for the Big Dig and opened on December 7, 2000, and Allandale Woods, which the plan called the city's largest urban wild. The 616-acre Stony Brook Reservation runs south from Washington Street in West Roxbury into Hyde Park, and Brook Farm, where the state bought 148 acres of the original land in 1988, is operated by DCR as a historic site.

Homes in West Roxbury

West Roxbury is mostly single-family homes, with some two-family houses. Boston's 2008-2014 Open Space Plan put single-family homes at about 55% of year-round housing units and noted that over three-quarters of units were built after 1920. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 13,868 occupied housing units, 61.7% of them owner-occupied, compared with 35.3% citywide.

Getting around

Commuting from West Roxbury

The Needham Line stops at Bellevue, Highland and West Roxbury, at 450 LaGrange Street, and runs through Roslindale Village, Forest Hills, Ruggles and Back Bay to South Station. Forest Hills, at the Jamaica Plain end of the line, connects with the Orange Line. Several MBTA bus routes run through or end in the neighborhood, and VFW Parkway and the West Roxbury Parkway are its main parkways.

Schools

Public schools

West Roxbury is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Things to doWest Roxbury

Places to knowin West Roxbury.

Millennium Park

A park on the former Gardner Street landfill, reclaimed with material excavated for the Big Dig and opened in 2000; the city's open space plan lists 105 acres, with 21 acres of playing fields and a canoe launch on the Charles River.

Stony Brook Reservation

A 616-acre DCR reservation, one of the five original reservations of the Metropolitan Park Commission, running south from Washington Street in West Roxbury to Mother Brook in Hyde Park, with hiking and biking trails and fishing at Turtle Pond.

Brook Farm

Site of the 1841 to 1847 transcendentalist community at 670 Baker Street, a National Historic Landmark since 1965, operated by DCR as a historic site.

Westerly Burying Ground

A burying ground on Centre Street, at LaGrange Street, dating to 1683, Boston's seventh-oldest cemetery, listed on the National Register in 1987.

Theodore Parker Church

A Boston Landmark on Centre Street, its 1900 building designed by Henry Seaver, with seven Tiffany Studios windows; the original 1890 church by Alexander Wadsworth Longfellow Jr. is now the parish hall.

Allandale Woods

A wooded city urban wild at the neighborhood's northern edge, described in Boston's open space plan as the city's largest.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in West Roxbury?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists West Roxbury on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $850,000, across 210 sales. In 2024 it was $797,500, across 222 sales. Condominium figures for West Roxbury could not be read from the table. The same report's condominium median for West Roxbury was $573,000 in 2025, across 97 sales, against $480,000 across 98 sales in 2024.

Does West Roxbury have commuter rail?

Yes. The MBTA Needham Line stops at Bellevue, Highland and West Roxbury and runs through Forest Hills, Ruggles and Back Bay to South Station.

Which school district serves West Roxbury?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

What is the highest point in Boston?

Bellevue Hill in West Roxbury, at 330 feet, is the highest natural point in Boston and Suffolk County. The Bellevue Standpipe on its summit is on the Boston Register of Historic Places.
