Living in North End, Boston, MA

Brick apartment buildings on narrow streets,between the Greenway and the harbor, from North Square to Copp's Hill.

The North End is a Boston neighborhood of about a third of a square mile in the city's northeastern corner, bounded by the Rose Kennedy Greenway and by Boston Harbor on two sides, and settled in the 1630s. The Boston Planning Department counted 10,805 residents there in 2020. Most of its housing is four- and five-story brick apartment buildings from the 1880s onward, around colonial landmarks such as the Paul Revere House and Old North Church; Haymarket, North Station and Aquarium stations are within walking distance.

About North End

Where it is

The North End lies northeast of the Rose Fitzgerald Kennedy Greenway, with Cross Street and North Washington Street along its western edge, the mouth of the Charles and Mystic Rivers to the north, and Commercial Street and Atlantic Avenue along Boston Harbor; Hanover Street runs through its middle.

The North End has been lived in since it was colonized in the 1630s. The North Meeting House went up in 1649, and North Square grew around it. On November 27, 1676, a fire destroyed Increase Mather's house, the meeting house and 45 other buildings, and the Paul Revere House was built on the Mather site no later than 1680. The town bought land on Copp's Hill for a burying ground in 1659. Christ Church, known as Old North, was established in 1723, and on the night of April 18, 1775, Robert Newman and John Pulling hung two lanterns in its steeple to signal that British troops were crossing the Charles River.

Most of what stands today is later. From the 1880s, wooden houses were replaced by four- and five-story brick apartment buildings, most of which remain, while a handful of colonial brick houses survive, including the Pierce-Hichborn House (1711) and the Clough House (1712). Twelve sites in the neighborhood are on the National Register of Historic Places. On January 15, 1919, a 2.3-million-gallon tank of the Purity Distilling Company at 529 Commercial Street burst, and the Great Molasses Flood killed 21 people and injured 150. In 1933 the Paul Revere Mall, or Prado, was cut through to connect Hanover Street with Old North Church.

In the 1950s the elevated Central Artery was built along the neighborhood's western side, demolishing hundreds of buildings below Cross Street and walling the North End off from downtown. From the mid-1970s the industrial waterfront along Commercial Street and Atlantic Avenue was converted to housing and business uses. The Big Dig put the highway underground, the elevated artery came down in 2003, and the Rose Fitzgerald Kennedy Greenway, opened in October 2008, now runs on its site, with its North End Parks on either side of Hanover Street. On the harbor, the City of Boston has rebuilt the 4.5-acre Langone Park and Puopolo Playground with a seawall and a harborwalk raised four feet.

Homes in North End

The North End's housing is mostly four- and five-story brick apartment buildings built from the 1880s on narrow streets, along with a few surviving colonial-era brick houses and housing in former wharf and industrial buildings along the waterfront converted from the mid-1970s. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 5,883 occupied housing units, 25.5% of them owner-occupied and 74.5% renter-occupied.

Corners ofNorth End.

North Square

The neighborhood's earliest center, which grew up around the North Meeting House of 1649; the Paul Revere House stands at 19 North Square.

Copp's Hill

The hill where the town bought land on February 20, 1659, for what is now the two-acre Copp's Hill Burying Ground.

Hanover Street and the Prado

Hanover Street is the main street through the neighborhood; the Paul Revere Mall, or Prado, created in 1933, connects it with Old North Church and has an equestrian statue of Paul Revere.

The waterfront

The harbor edge along Commercial Street and Atlantic Avenue, where former industrial wharves were converted to housing and business uses from the mid-1970s, and where Langone Park and Christopher Columbus Waterfront Park are.

Getting around

Commuting from North End

No MBTA station is inside the North End itself. Haymarket (Orange and Green Lines), North Station (Orange and Green Lines, and four commuter rail lines: Fitchburg, Haverhill, Lowell and Newburyport/Rockport) and Aquarium (Blue Line) are each within a five- to ten-minute walk. I-93 runs in tunnel beneath the Greenway on the neighborhood's western edge.

  • Orange Line and Green Line: Haymarket, North Station
  • Blue Line: Aquarium
  • Commuter rail (Fitchburg, Haverhill, Lowell and Newburyport/Rockport Lines): North Station

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

North End is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin North End.

Paul Revere House

Built no later than 1680 at 19 North Square and owned by Revere from 1770 to 1800, the oldest residential building still standing in downtown Boston; open to the public since April 18, 1908, operated by the Paul Revere Memorial Association, and a National Historic Landmark since 1961.

Old North Church (Christ Church)

At 193 Salem Street, established in 1723 and the oldest church building in Boston, where two lanterns were hung in the steeple on April 18, 1775.

Copp's Hill Burying Ground

A two-acre burying ground established in 1659, where Increase and Cotton Mather, Prince Hall and Robert Newman are buried; listed on the National Register on April 18, 1974.

Paul Revere Mall (the Prado)

A brick-walled corridor created in 1933 between Hanover Street and Old North Church, with an equestrian statue of Paul Revere.

Langone Park and Puopolo Playground

A 4.5-acre city park on Boston Harbor, rebuilt with a Little League field, a multisport field, a playground, bocce and basketball courts, an integrated seawall and a harborwalk raised four feet.

Rose Kennedy Greenway North End Parks

Two parcels of the 17-acre Greenway, on either side of Hanover Street, with fountains, lawns and a pergola, on land freed by the demolition of the elevated Central Artery.

Christopher Columbus Waterfront Park

A public park on the North End waterfront, one of the neighborhood's green spaces along with Langone Park.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in the North End?

The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table does not list the North End on its own row; its sales fall within the table's central 'Boston' row, so no North End median is given here. The Boston city guide shows that central row, which combines several neighborhoods and is not a citywide figure.

Does the North End have commuter rail or a subway station?

There is no MBTA station inside the neighborhood. North Station, a short walk away, is served by the Orange and Green Lines and by the Fitchburg, Haverhill, Lowell and Newburyport/Rockport commuter rail lines; Haymarket (Orange and Green Lines) and Aquarium (Blue Line) are also within walking distance.

Which school district serves the North End?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is by address under the district's home-based assignment system; review the district's profile with DESE.

What kind of housing is in the North End?

Mostly four- and five-story brick apartment buildings built from the 1880s, with a few colonial-era brick houses and converted waterfront buildings. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 5,883 occupied units, 25.5% owner-occupied.
