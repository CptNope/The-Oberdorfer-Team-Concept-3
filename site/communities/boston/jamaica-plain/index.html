Living in Jamaica Plain, Boston, MA

A kettle pond, an arboretum and a cemetery park,with the Orange Line running under a linear park.

Jamaica Plain is a Boston neighborhood of 4.4 square miles between Jamaica Pond and Franklin Park, part of the town of West Roxbury until it was annexed to Boston in 1874. Its housing runs from triple-deckers built from the 1870s onward to Victorian houses on former estates such as Sumner Hill. The Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 41,012 people there in 2020. Four Orange Line stations run under the Southwest Corridor Park, and the Needham Line stops at Forest Hills.

About Jamaica Plain

Where it is

Jamaica Plain, originally part of Roxbury, meets Roxbury at Egleston Square. Its sections run from Pondside, by Jamaica Pond and the Jamaicaway, and Parkside, beside Franklin Park, south to Forest Hills, near the Arnold Arboretum and Forest Hills Cemetery.

Settlement south of Parker Hill began shortly after 1630, when Boston and Roxbury were founded, and the name Jamaica first appears on maps in the late 1650s. In 1676 John Ruggles and Hugh Thomas gave land for the community's first school, later the Eliot School. From 1795 to 1886, the Jamaica Pond Aqueduct Corporation supplied water from the pond to Boston, Roxbury and later West Roxbury. The Boston and Providence Rail Road arrived in 1834, and in 1851 estate owners here helped split from Roxbury to form the town of West Roxbury, which voted to join Boston in 1873; annexation took effect in 1874. Breweries opened too: Highland Spring on Heath Street from 1867 and Haffenreffer, near Boylston and Amory Streets, from 1871 until 1964.

Triple-deckers first appeared in the 1870s and spread quickly in the 1890s. Larger houses went up on former estates: Sumner Hill on the Greenough land, Parley Vale and Robinwood Avenue in the 1880s, and Moss Hill and Woodland Road on Bowditch family land in the 1890s. By the early 20th century most buildable lots were filled. Much of the neighborhood's edge is parkland in Frederick Law Olmsted's Emerald Necklace: Olmsted Park and Jamaica Pond along the Jamaicaway, the Arnold Arboretum and the Arborway in the south, and Franklin Park beside the Parkside section; the 1848 Forest Hills Cemetery is in the Forest Hills section.

In 1969 Governor Francis Sargent cancelled a planned extension of I-95 through the area after community opposition. The cleared land became the Southwest Corridor: the Orange Line moved from the elevated Washington Street line into a below-grade cut in May 1987, and the park above it was completed in 1990. On March 2, 2017, the Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted PLAN: JP/ROX, guidelines for about 250 acres between Forest Hills, Egleston Square and Jackson Square, generally bounded by Washington Street, Columbus Avenue and Amory Street, covering housing, jobs, urban design and transportation.

Homes in Jamaica Plain

Jamaica Plain's housing mixes wood-frame triple-deckers, built from the 1870s and especially in the 1890s, with late-19th-century single-family houses on former estates such as Sumner Hill and Moss Hill. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 18,129 occupied housing units in the neighborhood, 45.6% of them owner-occupied, compared with 35.3% citywide.

Corners ofJamaica Plain.

The Monument and Centre Street

The area around the intersection of Centre and South Streets, named for the Soldiers' Monument there; the 1760 Loring-Greenough House stands on Monument Square at 12 South Street.

Hyde Square

The area around Centre, Day and Perkins Streets, extending east along Centre Street toward Roxbury.

Jackson Square and Egleston Square

Squares at the northern edge, on Columbus Avenue and Washington Street at the Roxbury line; Jackson Square has an Orange Line station, and both fall within the PLAN: JP/ROX study area.

Forest Hills

The section roughly bounded by the Arborway, Morton Street, Walk Hill Street, South Street and Forest Hills Cemetery, with Forest Hills station at Washington Street and Hyde Park Avenue, rebuilt in 1987 for the Orange Line and commuter rail.

Getting around

Commuting from Jamaica Plain

The Orange Line runs below street level through Jamaica Plain to Back Bay and downtown, and Forest Hills station, at Washington Street and Hyde Park Avenue, is also a Needham Line commuter rail stop and the terminal for 16 MBTA bus routes. The Green Line E branch ends at Heath Street, and the 39 bus is the neighborhood's main bus route. The Southwest Corridor Park carries a bike path beside the Orange Line, and the Jamaicaway and Arborway run along the Emerald Necklace.

  • Orange Line: Jackson Square, Stony Brook, Green Street, Forest Hills
  • Needham Line (commuter rail): Forest Hills
  • Green Line E branch: Heath Street (terminus)

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Jamaica Plain is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Jamaica Plain.

Jamaica Pond

A spring-fed glacial kettle hole more than 50 feet deep, taken into the park system in 1891, with a boathouse and bandstand from 1912 and boat rentals in summer.

Arnold Arboretum

A 281-acre preserve founded in 1872 and part of the Emerald Necklace, open free every day.

Franklin Park

Olmsted's 485-acre park, Boston's largest open space, with the Franklin Park Zoo, White Stadium and the William J. Devine Golf Course; it connects Jamaica Plain with Roxbury, Dorchester, Mattapan and Roslindale.

Forest Hills Cemetery

A garden cemetery founded in 1848 as Roxbury's municipal cemetery and designed by Henry A. S. Dearborn, entered from Morton Street; it was added to the National Register in 2004.

Loring-Greenough House

A 1760 country house at 12 South Street, owned since 1924 by the Jamaica Plain Tuesday Club and designated a Boston Landmark in 1999.

Southwest Corridor Park

A linear park managed by DCR from Back Bay station to Forest Hills, built over the Orange Line on land cleared for a cancelled highway; its first segments opened in 1987.

Haffenreffer Brewery buildings

The former Haffenreffer brewery near Boylston and Amory Streets, which brewed from 1871 until 1964; the old buildings now house the Boston Beer Company, brewer of Samuel Adams.

History

1676

John Ruggles and Hugh Thomas donated land for the community's first school, later named the Eliot School.

1851

Jamaica Plain became part of the new town of West Roxbury, set off from Roxbury.

1874

Annexation to Boston took effect after West Roxbury voters approved it in 1873.

1987

The rebuilt Forest Hills station opened on May 4, and Orange Line trains began running through the Southwest Corridor on May 7, replacing the elevated line on Washington Street.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Jamaica Plain?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Jamaica Plain on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $1,256,250, across 69 sales. In 2024 it was $1,157,500, across 64 sales. Condominium figures for Jamaica Plain could not be read from the table. The same report's condominium median for Jamaica Plain was $670,000 in 2025, across 324 sales, against $720,000 across 289 sales in 2024.

Does Jamaica Plain have commuter rail?

Yes. The Needham Line stops at Forest Hills, which is also the southern terminal of the Orange Line. The Orange Line has three more Jamaica Plain stations: Green Street, Stony Brook and Jackson Square.

Which school district serves Jamaica Plain?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

Which parks are in or next to Jamaica Plain?

Parks of Olmsted's Emerald Necklace line much of its edge: Jamaica Pond and Olmsted Park along the Jamaicaway, the Arnold Arboretum and the Arborway in the south, and Franklin Park, Boston's largest open space at 485 acres, beside the Parkside section.
