Living in Mattapan, Boston, MA

The trolley's last stop at Mattapan Square,triple-deckers, a nature sanctuary and the Neponset trail.

Mattapan is a neighborhood in southern Boston, part of the town of Dorchester when Boston annexed it in 1870, centered on Mattapan Square, where Blue Hill Avenue, River Street and Cummins Highway meet. Its housing includes triple-deckers, two- and three-family houses, single-family houses and small apartment buildings, and the Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 23,834 people there in 2020. The Mattapan Line trolley ends at Mattapan station, and the Fairmount Line stops at Morton Street and Blue Hill Avenue.

About Mattapan

Where it is

Mattapan is in the south of Boston, centered on Mattapan Square at the meeting of Blue Hill Avenue, River Street and Cummins Highway. Harambee Park in Dorchester's Franklin Field lies beside it, and the Boston Nature Center and Forest Hills Cemetery, in the adjoining Forest Hills area, are sometimes counted as its green space.

Mattapan takes its name from the Massachusett name for the Dorchester area, recorded as 'Matampan' in 1634. Paths that became Adams Street and River Street led to fishing weirs at Lower Mills, and Mattapan and Lower Mills were the main fords of the Neponset River. On December 1, 1847, the Dorchester and Milton Branch Railroad began service to a station at Upper Mills that was later renamed Mattapan, and on December 18, 1849, the Mattapan Library Association opened. Mattapan was part of Dorchester when Boston annexed that town in 1870, and it grew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as railroads and streetcars brought downtown within reach. The present Mattapan station building opened on July 4, 1902, after Blue Hill Avenue was widened.

In 1929 the Milton branch railroad became the Mattapan Line, a trolley that opened from Ashmont to Milton on August 26 and on to Mattapan on December 21; it runs beside the Neponset River and still uses PCC streetcars built in 1945 and 1946. The housing around the square includes triple-deckers, two- and three-family houses and single-family houses, with apartment buildings on parts of River Street. Boston State Hospital, in Mattapan and Dorchester, expanded onto the former Austin Farm in 1887 and closed in 1979; part of the grounds became Mass Audubon's Boston Nature Center, and 40 acres were redeveloped as Olmsted Green, 497 homes built in phases over 18 years, whose last phase, The Preserve, was sold out by February 2026.

The Fairmount Line's Morton Street station, a stop originally called Forest Avenue, reopened in October 1987 and was rebuilt to be accessible in 2007, and a new station at Blue Hill Avenue and Woodhaven Street opened on February 25, 2019. In April 2023 The Loop at Mattapan Station opened on the station's former parking lot, with 135 rental apartments and ground-floor retail. The Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted PLAN: Mattapan on May 11, 2023; residential zoning allowing two- and three-unit infill and accessory dwelling units followed on February 7, 2024, and on May 30, 2024 Mattapan Square became the first place in the city mapped with Squares + Streets zoning, which allows mixed-use buildings and multifamily housing.

Homes in Mattapan

The Boston Planning Department describes Mattapan's housing mix as small apartment buildings, single-family homes, public housing and Boston's traditional triple-deckers; two- and three-family houses are also part of the stock, and there are apartment buildings on parts of River Street. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 9,214 occupied housing units in Mattapan, 40.4% of them owner-occupied, compared with 35.3% citywide.

Corners ofMattapan.

Mattapan Square

The commercial center where Blue Hill Avenue, River Street and Cummins Highway meet, with Mattapan station at River Street and Blue Hill Avenue, the end of the trolley line and of eight bus routes; it was the first area mapped with Squares + Streets zoning, in 2024.

Blue Hill Avenue

With Mattapan Square, the neighborhood's main commercial street, with the Mattapan Branch Library at number 1350 and the Fairmount Line's Blue Hill Avenue station at Woodhaven Street.

Former Boston State Hospital grounds

The old hospital campus, closed in 1979, now divided between the 67-acre Boston Nature Center on Walk Hill Street and Olmsted Green, a 40-acre development of 497 homes, 80% of them deed-restricted affordable and 28% for ownership.

Morton Street

The corridor around Morton Street station at 865 Morton Street, a Fairmount Line stop between Talbot Avenue and Blue Hill Avenue.

Getting around

Commuting from Mattapan

The Mattapan Line trolley runs about 2.6 miles from Mattapan station along the Neponset River to Ashmont, where it meets the Red Line. The Fairmount Line stops at Morton Street and Blue Hill Avenue on its way between Readville and South Station, through Dorchester. Eight MBTA bus routes end at Mattapan station, and Blue Hill Avenue, River Street, Cummins Highway and Morton Street are the main roads.

Schools

Public schools

Mattapan is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Mattapan.

Boston Nature Center and Wildlife Sanctuary

Mass Audubon's 67-acre sanctuary at 500 Walk Hill Street, with gardens, meadows, woods and wetlands; admission is free, and part of the land was once Boston State Hospital.

Neponset River Greenway

A trail of about nine miles through Readville, Hyde Park, Mattapan and Dorchester and into Milton; a 1.3-mile section opened in May 2017 between the Mattapan and Central Avenue trolley stations, linking Mattapan Square to the trail toward Pope John Paul II Park.

Franklin Park

Olmsted's park of roughly 485 acres, designed in the 1890s and Boston's largest open space, with the Franklin Park Zoo, the William Devine Golf Course and White Stadium; it connects Mattapan with Roxbury, Dorchester, Jamaica Plain and Roslindale.

Harambee Park

A park built in 1898 to a Frederick Law Olmsted design and originally called Franklin Field, in Dorchester beside Mattapan.

Mattahunt Woods

A city Urban Wild with an entrance at 206 Colorado Street, where Boston Parks and Recreation, the Trustees and the Friends of Mattahunt Woods hold public programs.

Mattapan Branch Library

A Boston Public Library branch at 1350 Blue Hill Avenue, opened in February 2009; its history goes back to the Mattapan Library Association of 1849.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Mattapan?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Mattapan on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $627,500, across 34 sales, against $599,950 across 48 sales in 2024. The condominium median was $420,000 in 2025, across 94 sales, against $417,450 across 34 sales in 2024. Two- and three-family houses are not covered by either figure.

Does Mattapan have commuter rail?

Yes. The Fairmount Line stops at Morton Street and at Blue Hill Avenue, which opened in 2019, on its way between Readville and South Station. The Mattapan Line trolley also runs from Mattapan station to Ashmont, where it meets the Red Line.

Which school district serves Mattapan?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

What is the Mattapan trolley?

The Mattapan Line, opened in 1929 on a former branch railroad, is a streetcar line of about 2.6 miles with eight stations between Ashmont and Mattapan that follows the Neponset River and uses PCC streetcars built in 1945 and 1946. The MBTA's Mattapan Line Transformation project plans to replace them with newer light rail vehicles and rebuild the stations.

What is Squares + Streets zoning in Mattapan Square?

It is a set of Boston zoning districts, adopted in April 2024 as part of the city's Squares + Streets initiative to increase housing production near transit; each district allows mixed-use buildings and multifamily housing. Mattapan Square was the first place mapped with it, on May 30, 2024, following PLAN: Mattapan, which the Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted on May 11, 2023.
