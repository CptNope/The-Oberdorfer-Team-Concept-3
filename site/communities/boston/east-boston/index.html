Living in East Boston, Boston, MA

Former harbor islands joined by landfill,with five Blue Line stops, Piers Park and Logan Airport on the water.

East Boston is a Boston neighborhood across Boston Harbor from downtown, built on several former islands joined by landfill and laid out on a street grid by the East Boston Company in the 1830s. The Boston Planning Department counted 43,066 residents there in 2020. Its housing runs from Greek Revival brick bowfront houses of 1847 to about 1852 on Jeffries Point to Italianate and Second Empire houses on Eagle Hill, and the Blue Line stops at Maverick, Airport, Wood Island, Orient Heights and Suffolk Downs.

About East Boston

Where it is

East Boston lies across Boston Harbor from downtown and Charlestown and borders Winthrop, Revere and Chelsea. Logan International Airport lies mainly in East Boston, with part of the airfield in Winthrop.

East Boston was built on Noddle's Island, the largest of five islands (with Hog, Governor's, Bird and Apple) that landfill had joined into one landmass, including the ground under Logan Airport, by the 1940s. The island was farmed through the 1700s. By 1833 William H. Sumner, with Steven White and Francis J. Oliver, had bought up half of it, and by 1834 their East Boston Company controlled the island and laid it out on a grid of streets, the first planned neighborhood in Boston. Donald McKay opened his shipyard on Border Street in 1845, and his clipper Flying Cloud (1851) twice sailed from New York to San Francisco in 89 days. Shipbuilding became the mainstay of the local economy.

The oldest streets are on Jeffries Point, at the southern end facing downtown, where Belmont Square (now Brophy Memorial Park) on Camp Hill survives from the original plan and is edged by brick bowfront houses built around 1847 to 1852. Eagle Hill has a National Register historic district of houses built roughly between 1834 and 1900. Maverick Square and Central Square are the main commercial areas. Transportation reshaped the neighborhood through the 20th century: the East Boston streetcar tunnel, now the Blue Line, opened in 1904 as the first underwater tunnel in North America; the airfield that became Logan opened on September 8, 1923; and rows of houses were cleared for the Sumner Tunnel (opened June 30, 1934) and the Callahan Tunnel (1961). The Ted Williams Tunnel brought I-90 to East Boston in 1995.

Recent planning has covered both the established neighborhood and its largest open site. The Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted PLAN: East Boston on January 18, 2024, after work that began in 2018; it covers roughly two square miles, excluding Logan Airport and the Suffolk Downs site, and the zoning amendments to Article 53 that carry it out were adopted by the Boston Zoning Commission on April 24, 2024. At the former Suffolk Downs racetrack, whose last day of live racing was June 30, 2019, the agency approved in September 2020 a redevelopment of the 161-acre site in East Boston and Revere with 10,000 housing units and almost three million square feet of office and retail space. On the waterfront, Massport opened a 4.5-acre second phase of Piers Park in December 2023.

Homes in East Boston

East Boston's housing includes Greek Revival brick bowfront houses of about 1847 to 1852 on Belmont Square, Greek Revival, Italianate and mansard double houses on Jeffries Point (where most residential building came between 1840 and 1890), and Italianate, Second Empire and Renaissance Revival houses in the Eagle Hill historic district. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 17,123 occupied housing units, 25.6% of them owner-occupied and 74.4% renter-occupied.

Corners ofEast Boston.

Jeffries Point

The earliest-settled part of East Boston, at the southern end facing downtown, developed by the East Boston Company in the 1830s; nine Greek Revival brick bowfront houses at 177 to 193 Webster Street line Belmont Square (Brophy Memorial Park) on Camp Hill.

Eagle Hill

A National Register historic district, listed on February 26, 1998, roughly bounded by Meridian, Princeton and White Streets meeting in Prescott Square, with mostly Italianate, Second Empire and Renaissance Revival buildings from about 1834 to 1900.

Maverick Square

One of East Boston's two main commercial areas, with Central Square, and the site of the Blue Line's Maverick station, opened on April 18, 1924.

Orient Heights

The section of East Boston around Constitution Beach, across the water from the runways of Logan Airport, and the Orient Heights Blue Line station, opened on January 5, 1952.

Getting around

Commuting from East Boston

The Blue Line runs under the harbor from Maverick to downtown and stops at five stations in East Boston: Maverick, Airport, Wood Island, Orient Heights and Suffolk Downs. Massport runs free shuttle buses from Airport station to the Logan terminals. The MBTA's seasonal East Boston Ferry links Lewis Mall with Long Wharf. East Boston has no commuter rail station. I-90 reaches it through the Ted Williams Tunnel, and Route 1A through the Sumner and Callahan Tunnels.

  • Blue Line: Maverick, Airport, Wood Island, Orient Heights, Suffolk Downs
  • East Boston Ferry (seasonal): East Boston (Lewis Mall), Long Wharf (North)

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

East Boston is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Things to doEast Boston

Places to knowin East Boston.

Piers Park

A Massport park on the southwest side of East Boston facing downtown: the 6.5-acre first phase opened in 1995 with a 600-foot brick promenade, four shade pavilions (one dedicated to Donald McKay) and the Piers Park Sailing Center, and a 4.5-acre second phase opened in December 2023.

Belle Isle Marsh Reservation

A DCR reservation at 1399 Bennington Street, described by DCR as Boston's largest remaining salt marsh, with accessible trails.

Constitution Beach

A public beach in Orient Heights with a bathhouse, snack bar, playground, courts, baseball fields and an indoor ice rink, about 2,000 feet from Logan's runways 22L and 22R.

East Boston Greenway

A path on a former Grand Junction Railroad freight line, opened in 2007, linking Piers Park, Memorial Stadium, Bremen Street Park, Wood Island Bay Marsh and Belle Isle Marsh; it is planned to run 3.3 miles when complete.

Bremen Street Park

A park on the Greenway with an entrance to the Blue Line's Airport station.

Belmont Square (Brophy Memorial Park)

A square at the top of Camp Hill, developed by the East Boston Company in the 1830s, with views of Boston Harbor, Greek Revival bowfront houses on Webster Street, and the Golden Stairs descending to Marginal Street at the water's edge.

Donald McKay House

The shipbuilder's house on White Street, a Boston Landmark since 1977 and listed on the National Register; it is a private residence.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in East Boston?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists East Boston on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $649,000, across 41 sales, against $700,000 across 23 sales in 2024. The condominium median was $645,000 in 2025, across 263 sales, against $665,000 across 302 sales in 2024.

Does East Boston have commuter rail?

No. East Boston is served by the Blue Line at Maverick, Airport, Wood Island, Orient Heights and Suffolk Downs, and by the MBTA's seasonal East Boston Ferry from Lewis Mall to Long Wharf.

Which school district serves East Boston?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is by address under the district's home-based assignment system; review the district's profile with DESE.

What is planned for Suffolk Downs?

In September 2020 the Boston Planning & Development Agency approved the redevelopment of the 161-acre former racetrack in East Boston and Revere with 10,000 housing units, including 930 affordable units on site, and almost three million square feet of office and retail space. Live racing ended on June 30, 2019.

What is PLAN: East Boston?

A neighborhood-wide plan for land use, zoning, transportation and the public realm, adopted by the Boston Planning & Development Agency board on January 18, 2024. Its zoning amendments to Article 53 were adopted by the Boston Zoning Commission on April 24, 2024. It excludes Logan Airport and the Suffolk Downs site.
