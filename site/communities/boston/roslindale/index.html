Living in Roslindale, Boston, MA

A 1911 substation on the square,the Needham Line and the Arboretum's Peters Hill.

Roslindale is a Boston neighborhood about six miles south-southwest of downtown, between the Arnold Arboretum and the George Wright Golf Course, and was part of the town of West Roxbury until annexation to Boston took effect in 1874. One- and two-family houses make up much of its housing, with newer apartment buildings around Roslindale Square. The Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 29,386 people there in 2020, and the Needham Line stops at Roslindale Village.

About Roslindale

Where it is

Boston's open space plan notes that Roslindale's edges are not distinct, but describes the planning district as bounded by the Arnold Arboretum and the Walter Street Tract to the north, the former Boston State Hospital campus and the Forest Hills and Mount Hope cemeteries to the east, the West Roxbury Parkway to the west, and the George Wright Golf Course and part of the Stony Brook Reservation to the south. Jamaica Plain, Hyde Park, West Roxbury and Mattapan are its neighbors.

Roslindale was part of the town of Roxbury until 1851, when it, Jamaica Plain and West Roxbury left Roxbury to form the town of West Roxbury. The Boston and Providence Railroad opened its West Roxbury Branch from Forest Hills to Dedham on July 14, 1849, with a station at South Street, and by the 1860s the area was known as South Street Crossing. When residents sought their own post office district, the government rejected that name, and John Pierce proposed Roslindale, after Roslin, near Edinburgh. Voters approved annexation to Boston in 1873, and it took effect in 1874. Horse-drawn street railway service between Forest Hills and Dedham followed in the 1880s.

Washington Street runs through Roslindale Square, which took its present layout in the 1920s around Adams Park. In the square stands the Roslindale Substation, built for the Boston Elevated Railway in 1911 to a design by Robert S. Peabody and listed on the National Register in 2013. In 1929 Ashland Street, by then widened, was renamed Cummins Highway, from Washington Street to River Street. Roslindale Village Main Street, established in 1985 as one of the first urban Main Street programs in the country, runs the farmers' market and events in the square. To the north, the part of the Arnold Arboretum south of Bussey Street, including Peters Hill, at 240 feet, lies in Roslindale, and rail lines divide the neighborhood roughly into thirds.

Boston's 2008-2014 open space plan counted one- and two-family houses as 60% of Roslindale's housing, against 32% citywide; the western streets resemble West Roxbury, while the northern streets mix two- and three-family houses with light industrial buildings. Roslindale Square was the first plan adopted under the city's Squares + Streets initiative: the planning process ran from February 2024 to February 2025, the Boston Planning & Development Agency board adopted the Roslindale Square Small Area Plan on February 13, 2025, and the Zoning Commission approved new zoning in May 2025, effective June 2, 2025. It was the second area of the city with Squares + Streets districts in force, and the zoning allows multifamily housing near and above ground-floor businesses.

Homes in Roslindale

Roslindale is mostly one- and two-family houses: Boston's 2008-2014 open space plan put single-family homes at 33% and two-family homes at 27% of units, 60% together, compared with 32% citywide. Its western streets are mainly one- and two-family houses, its northern streets have more two- and three-family houses, and newer apartment buildings stand around Roslindale Square. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 53.8% of occupied units as owner-occupied (6,539 units), compared with 35.3% citywide.

Corners ofRoslindale.

Roslindale Square

The business district around Adams Park, where Washington Street runs through the square, with Roslindale Village station at 1 Belgrade Avenue, the 1911 Roslindale Substation and the Squares + Streets zoning adopted in 2025.

The Arboretum edge

The northern side, where the part of the Arnold Arboretum south of Bussey Street, including 240-foot Peters Hill, lies within Roslindale; the 1887 Bussey Street bridge collapse took place on what is now Arboretum land.

Cummins Highway

Formerly Ashland Street, which had been widened to 60 to 80 feet before it was renamed in 1929; it runs from Washington Street to River Street and was the first road in Boston called a highway.

Getting around

Commuting from Roslindale

Roslindale Village station, at 1 Belgrade Avenue in the square, is on the Needham Line, which runs through Forest Hills, Ruggles and Back Bay to South Station; Forest Hills, in Jamaica Plain, connects with the Orange Line. Ten MBTA bus routes run on Washington Street between Forest Hills and Roslindale Square and fan out to the south and west. The West Roxbury Parkway runs along the western edge.

Schools

Public schools

Roslindale is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Roslindale.

Roslindale Substation

A 1911 Boston Elevated Railway substation at 4228 Washington Street, designed by Robert S. Peabody of Peabody and Stearns and listed on the National Register on August 27, 2013; the city sold it in 2013 to Historic Boston Incorporated and Roslindale Village Main Street, and its ground floor opened as a beer hall in spring 2022.

Adams Park

The green at the center of Roslindale Square, renovated by the city with a performance space, walkways and landscaping; Roslindale Village Main Street's farmers' market has been held there on Saturdays.

Arnold Arboretum and Peters Hill

Harvard's 281-acre arboretum, founded in 1872 and open free every day, lies in Jamaica Plain and Roslindale; Peters Hill, at 240 feet the tallest of its hills, is in the Roslindale section.

Roslindale Wetlands

A city urban wild, once called the Eldon Street Urban Wild, with a wetland, woodland and meadow, a perimeter loop trail built in improvements between 2020 and 2024, and entrances on Walter Street, Weld Street, Hazelmere Road and Coniston Road.

Healy Playground and Fallon Field

City recreation grounds: Healy Playground has three ball fields and a stairway on Washington Street, and Fallon Field has a playground, two basketball courts and a street hockey court.

George Wright Golf Course

An 18-hole municipal course designed by Donald Ross and opened on April 23, 1938, at 420 West Street; it lies in Hyde Park and Roslindale and marks Roslindale's southern edge.

History

1849

The Boston and Providence Railroad's West Roxbury Branch opened on July 14 with a station at South Street, today's Roslindale Village station.

1874

Annexation to Boston took effect, after voters in the town of West Roxbury, which included Roslindale, approved it in 1873.

1887

On March 14, a railroad bridge at Bussey Street collapsed under a train carrying more than 200 passengers, killing 23; the site is now in the Arnold Arboretum.

2025

The Roslindale Square Small Area Plan, the first plan adopted under Squares + Streets, was adopted on February 13, and its zoning took effect on June 2.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Roslindale?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Roslindale on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $778,000, across 86 sales, up from $762,000 across 96 sales in 2024. The condominium median was $615,000 across 106 sales in 2025, against $590,000 across 95 sales in 2024.

Does Roslindale have commuter rail?

Yes. The MBTA Needham Line stops at Roslindale Village, at 1 Belgrade Avenue in Roslindale Square, and runs through Forest Hills, Ruggles and Back Bay to South Station.

Which school district serves Roslindale?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

What is Squares + Streets zoning in Roslindale Square?

Roslindale Square was the first area to have a plan adopted under Boston's Squares + Streets initiative, on February 13, 2025. Its zoning, effective June 2, 2025, allows mixed-use development and multifamily housing near and above ground-floor businesses.

Is the Arnold Arboretum in Roslindale?

Partly. The arboretum's 281 acres lie in Jamaica Plain and Roslindale, and the section south of Bussey Street, including Peters Hill, is in Roslindale.
