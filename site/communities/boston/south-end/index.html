Living in South End, Boston, MA

Brick and brownstone rows around small squares,built on the marsh around Boston Neck.

The South End is a Boston neighborhood built from 1849 on filled tidal marsh around Boston Neck, with 30,904 residents in 2020 (2020 Census, Boston Planning Department tabulation, which counts Bay Village with the South End). Its housing is brick and brownstone row houses built from 1850 to 1880 around residential squares such as Union Park, protected as the South End Landmark District since 1983. The Orange Line stops at Back Bay and Massachusetts Avenue, and Silver Line SL4 and SL5 buses run on Washington Street.

About South End

Where it is

The South End Historic District on the National Register is bounded by the Southwest Corridor Park to the northwest, the Massachusetts Turnpike to the north, Herald Street to the east, Albany Street to the southeast and Massachusetts Avenue to the southwest; the neighborhood borders Back Bay, Chinatown and Roxbury.

The South End was tidal marsh around Boston Neck, the strip of land joining Boston to Roxbury; Washington Street follows the original causeway between them. In 1801 Charles Bulfinch proposed a single oval park there, Columbia Square, which by 1814 appeared as two halves on either side of Washington Street, today's Blackstone and Franklin Squares; the area developed slowly until the 1850s. Large-scale filling made the rest of the neighborhood, and construction began on January 7, 1849. The main period of development, from 1850 to 1873, produced brick and brownstone row houses built as single-family homes. After the Panic of 1873 many of the houses were converted to multi-unit housing, and by the turn of the century lodging houses were almost the only residential buildings being built.

The row houses, built between 1850 and 1880 in brick and brownstone, mix Greek Revival, Italianate and later Victorian styles, and the neighborhood has eleven residential parks. Union Park, an elliptical park laid out by Ellis S. Chesbrough, had its lots auctioned in June 1851 and every house finished by 1859, with bowfront and square-front bays facing a cast-iron fence copied from Louisburg Square. Chester Square, laid out in 1850 as a larger oval, became part of Massachusetts Avenue in 1891. The Cyclorama, built in 1884 to show a panoramic painting of the Battle of Gettysburg, is now part of the Boston Center for the Arts, and Patrick Keely's Gothic Revival Cathedral of the Holy Cross, at 1400 Washington Street, was dedicated in 1875.

The South End Landmark District, designated in 1983, requires South End Landmark District Commission approval for exterior work on front facades, rooftops and some side and rear elevations before it begins. The National Register district was listed on May 8, 1973, with boundary increases in 2014 and 2025. Along the northwest edge, the Southwest Corridor Park, managed by the state Department of Conservation and Recreation, opened in segments from 1987 and was completed in 1990 over and beside the Orange Line and the rail corridor, with paths, courts and playgrounds. East of Washington Street, the area known as SoWa (South of Washington) runs roughly from Albany to Washington Street between East Berkeley Street and Massachusetts Avenue.

Homes in South End

The South End's row houses were built as single-family brick and brownstone homes between 1850 and 1880, and many were converted to multi-unit housing after the Panic of 1873. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2014 to 2018 American Community Survey counts 17,936 housing units, 16,606 of them occupied; 38.6% of occupied units were owner occupied and 61.4% renter occupied.

Blackstone and Franklin Squares

Two squares facing each other across Washington Street, the halves of Charles Bulfinch's 1801 Columbia Square, with brownstone rows such as 35 to 45 West Newton Street (1853) on Blackstone Square.

Chester Square

Laid out in 1850 as an oval park on a larger scale than Union Park and Worcester Square; Chester Street became part of Massachusetts Avenue in 1891.

Getting around

Commuting from South End

The Orange Line runs along the Southwest Corridor with stops at Back Bay and Massachusetts Avenue, and Back Bay station also serves four commuter rail lines and Amtrak. Prudential on the Green Line is nearby, and Silver Line SL4 and SL5 buses run on Washington Street between Nubian Square and downtown, with stops from Lenox Street to Herald Street. The Massachusetts Turnpike forms the northern edge.

  • Orange Line: Back Bay, Massachusetts Avenue
  • Green Line: Prudential
  • Commuter rail: Framingham/Worcester, Needham, Franklin/Foxboro and Providence/Stoughton Lines: Back Bay
  • Silver Line SL4 and SL5 (bus rapid transit): Washington Street, Lenox Street to Herald Street

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

South End is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin South End.

Union Park

The elliptical park behind a cast-iron fence with a lotus motif copied from Louisburg Square, ringed by houses finished by 1859.

Southwest Corridor Park

A linear state park opened in segments from 1987 and completed in 1990, running from the South End and Back Bay to Forest Hills in Jamaica Plain, with paths, tennis and basketball courts, and playgrounds.

Boston Center for the Arts and the Cyclorama

An arts complex founded in 1970 around the Cyclorama, built in 1884 by Cummings and Sears to show Paul Philippoteaux's painting of the Battle of Gettysburg; it houses Boston Ballet's headquarters, the Community Music Center of Boston, theaters and a gallery.

Cathedral of the Holy Cross

Patrick Keely's Gothic Revival cathedral at 1400 Washington Street, begun on April 29, 1866, and dedicated on December 8, 1875; it is 364 feet long.

Community gardens and pocket parks

Sixteen community gardens and pocket parks operated by the Trustees of Reservations, alongside newer parks such as Peters Park.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in the South End?

The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table does not list the South End as its own row. It reports some Boston neighborhoods, such as Charlestown, Roxbury and South Boston, separately, and has a Boston row that the PDF does not define by neighborhood; see the Boston city guide for that row.

Does the South End have commuter rail?

Yes. Back Bay station, on the Orange Line along the Southwest Corridor, serves the Framingham/Worcester, Needham, Franklin/Foxboro and Providence/Stoughton Lines and Amtrak. The Orange Line also stops at Massachusetts Avenue, and Silver Line SL4 and SL5 buses run on Washington Street.

Which school district serves the South End?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district under its own citywide rules; review the district's profile with DESE.

Do South End homes need approval for exterior changes?

Inside the South End Landmark District, designated in 1983, yes. The South End Landmark District Commission reviews proposed exterior work on front facades, rooftops and some side and rear elevations, and approval is needed before work begins.
