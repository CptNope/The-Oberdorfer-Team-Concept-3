Living in Back Bay, Boston, MA

Brownstone rows on filled tidal flats,laid out one alphabetical cross street at a time.

Back Bay is a Boston neighborhood built on tidal flats filled between 1857 and 1882, with 18,190 residents in 2020 (2020 Census, Boston Planning Department tabulation). Its housing is mostly Victorian brownstone row houses of three to five stories on Beacon, Marlborough and Commonwealth, with shops along Newbury and Boylston Streets. Back Bay station serves the Orange Line, four commuter rail lines and Amtrak, and the Green Line stops at Arlington, Copley, Hynes Convention Center and Prudential.

About Back Bay

Where it is

As defined by the Neighborhood Association of the Back Bay, the Charles River to the north, Arlington Street and Park Square to the east, Columbus Avenue, Huntington Avenue, Dalton Street and the Massachusetts Turnpike to the south, and Charlesgate East to the west. The smaller Back Bay Architectural District runs from Back Street to Boylston Street between Arlington Street and Charlesgate East.

Back Bay was a tidal bay of the Charles River until the 19th century. The Boston and Roxbury Mill Corporation, chartered in 1814, built a milldam across it that doubled as a toll road; the basin behind it trapped sewage and the venture failed. Filling of the area enclosed by the dam began in 1857, and construction of houses followed from 1859. By 1882 the present neighborhood had been filled, and the City of Boston credits the mid-century landfilling with more than 450 acres of usable land by the 1880s. Arthur Gilman's plan, modeled on Haussmann's Paris, set five parallel east-west streets, Beacon, Marlborough, Commonwealth, Newbury and Boylston, crossed by streets named in alphabetical order from Arlington to Hereford.

Commonwealth Avenue runs down the middle of the grid on a 32-acre mall that Gilman designed in 1856 in the French boulevard style, the green link between the Public Garden and Frederick Law Olmsted's park system. The row houses, three to five stories, were held to setbacks written into the lot deeds, so the blocks read as continuous walls. Commercial use reached Boylston Street around 1880 and Newbury Street in the early 20th century, often by adapting existing row houses. Copley Square gathers H. H. Richardson's Trinity Church (1872 to 1877) and the Boston Public Library's McKim Building (1895), and the 52-story Prudential Tower, completed in 1964, rises to the south.

The Back Bay Architectural District was established in 1966 to regulate exterior changes, and it was expanded in 1974, 1979 and 1981. All proposed exterior work goes to the Back Bay Architectural Commission, and approval is needed before work begins. The district was added to the National Register of Historic Places on August 14, 1973. Along the river, the Charles River Esplanade was built after the Charles River Dam was completed in 1910, and pedestrian overpasses connect it to the city's streets.

Homes in Back Bay

Back Bay's housing is mostly Victorian brownstone row houses of three to five stories, built from 1859 as the land was filled. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2014 to 2018 American Community Survey counts 11,992 housing units, 9,842 of them occupied; 31.8% of occupied units were owner occupied and 68.2% renter occupied.

Corners ofBack Bay.

Back Bay Architectural District

The residential core from Back Street to Boylston Street, between Arlington Street and Charlesgate East: Beacon, Marlborough and Commonwealth crossed by the alphabetical streets, where all exterior work is reviewed by the Back Bay Architectural Commission.

Newbury and Boylston Streets

The shopping streets of the neighborhood; commercial development started on Boylston Street around 1880 and on Newbury Street in the early 20th century, in some cases by adapting existing row houses.

Copley Square

A public square in the southeast of the neighborhood, framed by Trinity Church and the Boston Public Library's Central Library.

Prudential Center

The mixed-use complex in the south of the neighborhood around the 52-story Prudential Tower, completed in 1964, with Prudential station on the Green Line.

Getting around

Commuting from Back Bay

Back Bay station, at 145 Dartmouth Street, serves the Orange Line, the Framingham/Worcester, Needham, Franklin/Foxboro and Providence/Stoughton commuter rail lines, and Amtrak's Acela, Northeast Regional and the Boston section of the Lake Shore Limited. The Green Line stops at Arlington, Copley, Hynes Convention Center and Prudential. The Massachusetts Turnpike forms part of the neighborhood's southern boundary.

  • Orange Line: Back Bay
  • Green Line: Arlington, Copley, Hynes Convention Center, Prudential
  • Commuter rail: Framingham/Worcester, Needham, Franklin/Foxboro and Providence/Stoughton Lines: Back Bay, 145 Dartmouth Street

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Places to knowin Back Bay.

Commonwealth Avenue Mall

A 32-acre promenade designed by Arthur Gilman in 1856 in the French boulevard style, with monuments along its center and a link from the Public Garden to the Olmsted parks.

Trinity Church

H. H. Richardson's Richardsonian Romanesque church on Copley Square, built from 1872 to 1877 and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1970.

Boston Public Library, Central Library

At 700 Boylston Street on Copley Square: Charles Follen McKim's 1895 building, with the Bates Hall reading room, and Philip Johnson's 1972 Boylston Street building in the same pink Milford granite.

Charles River Esplanade

State parkland along the Charles River created with the 1910 dam and expanded between 1928 and 1936, with the Hatch Shell stage, built in 1941, near the Arthur Fiedler footbridge at Arlington Street.

Public Garden

Established in 1837 across Arlington Street as the first public botanical garden in America, with the Lagoon and the Swan Boats run by the Paget family for over 100 years.

Gibson House Museum

A Victorian house museum at 137 Beacon Street, home to three generations of the Gibson family from 1859 to 1954.

Buying in Back Bay

That row's single-family median was $3,092,500 in 2025, across 50 sales, and its condominium median was $945,000, according to The Warren Group. What is the median home price in Back Bay?

The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table does not list Back Bay as its own row. Does Back Bay have commuter rail?

Yes. Back Bay station at 145 Dartmouth Street is a stop on the Framingham/Worcester, Needham, Franklin/Foxboro and Providence/Stoughton Lines, as well as the Orange Line and Amtrak.

Which school district serves Back Bay?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district under its own citywide rules; review the district's profile with DESE.

Do Back Bay homes need approval for exterior changes?

Inside the Back Bay Architectural District, yes. All proposed exterior work is reviewed by the Back Bay Architectural Commission, and approval is needed before work begins.
