Living in Roxbury, Boston, MA

A puddingstone hill with an 1869 standpipe tower,row houses and triple-deckers around Nubian Square.

Roxbury is a Boston neighborhood that the city's planning department describes as the geographic heart of Boston, settled in 1630 and annexed to Boston in 1868. Its housing runs from Greek Revival and Italianate houses on Fort Hill to brick row houses and triple-deckers, and the Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 54,905 people there in 2020. The Silver Line runs on Washington Street from Nubian Square, and the Orange Line and commuter rail stop at Ruggles.

About Roxbury

Where it is

Roxbury sits at the center of Boston: Columbia Road separates it from Dorchester, Franklin Park is divided between Roxbury and Jamaica Plain along Walnut Avenue and Sigourney Street, and Ruggles station stands where Roxbury, the Fenway and Mission Hill meet. Melnea Cass Boulevard runs roughly over the line of the old Roxbury Canal.

Roxbury was settled on September 28, 1630. William Pynchon, one of the founders, left in 1636 with a group of the town's men to found Springfield. The town's houses were set on Roxbury puddingstone, a conglomerate quarried for foundations. Several 18th-century buildings survive: Governor William Shirley built the Shirley-Eustis House as a summer house between 1747 and 1751, and the Dillaway-Thomas House, built as a parsonage about 1750, served as a Continental Army headquarters during the Siege of Boston in 1775. Roxbury became a city in 1846, part of it was set off as the town of West Roxbury in 1851, and Boston annexed the rest on January 5, 1868.

Row houses and multi-family buildings followed trolley service, which began in 1887, and the Washington Street Elevated reached Dudley Square, now Nubian Square, on June 10, 1901. The building stock shows each period. On Fort Hill, around Highland Park, Greek Revival and Italianate houses that predate the Civil War stand among triple-deckers and row houses, below an 1869 standpipe tower built for Roxbury's water system. The Moreland Street district was built up between about 1840 and 1920 with Second Empire, Italianate and Queen Anne houses, and in Lower Roxbury the Tremont Improvement Company built row houses on filled salt marsh around what became Frederick Douglass Square in 1917.

The last train ran on the Washington Street Elevated on April 30, 1987, and the Orange Line moved to the Southwest Corridor, with new stations at Ruggles and Roxbury Crossing opening on May 4. Silver Line buses began running on Washington Street on July 20, 2002. The Boston Planning & Development Agency started PLAN: Dudley Square in 2016 to guide public and vacant parcels around the square, and its board approved the plan's development guidelines on July 11, 2019. On December 19, 2019, the city's Public Improvement Commission renamed Dudley Square as Nubian Square, and in 2022 the city designated the Highland Park Architectural Conservation District, about 170 acres of steep, puddingstone-studded land also called Roxbury Highlands or Fort Hill.

Homes in Roxbury

The Boston Planning Department describes Roxbury's housing as historic brick row houses and Boston's traditional triple-deckers; Fort Hill adds Greek Revival, Italianate and later Victorian houses, and Cedar Street has a run of 1871 Second Empire row houses. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 20,681 occupied housing units in Roxbury, 26.1% of them owner-occupied, compared with 35.3% citywide.

Corners ofRoxbury.

Nubian Square

The commercial center formerly called Dudley Square, framed by Washington Street, Malcolm X Boulevard, Dudley Street and Guild Row, with Nubian station, the Silver Line terminus and a large MBTA bus terminal, and the Ferdinand Building, now Boston Public Schools headquarters.

Fort Hill (Highland Park)

A 0.4-square-mile hill bounded by Malcolm X Boulevard, Washington Street and Columbus Avenue, named for a Revolutionary War earthwork; listed on the National Register as the Roxbury Highlands Historic District on February 22, 1989, it holds Greek Revival and Italianate houses, triple-deckers and row houses, and John Eliot Square with the First Church and the Dillaway-Thomas House.

Moreland Street Historic District

A 63-acre, mainly residential district roughly bounded by Kearsarge and Blue Hill Avenues and Warren, Waverly and Winthrop Streets, developed between about 1840 and 1920 and listed on the National Register on March 29, 1984.

Frederick Douglass Square

Lower Roxbury row houses in Second Empire, Queen Anne and Gothic styles, built on filled salt marsh between Hammond, Cabot, Windsor and Westminster Streets; the district was listed on the National Register on October 3, 1996.

Getting around

Commuting from Roxbury

The Silver Line runs on Washington Street from Nubian station, the SL4 to South Station and the SL5 to Downtown Crossing, following the route of the elevated line that closed in 1987. The Orange Line stops at Ruggles, on Tremont Street, and at Roxbury Crossing, at Tremont Street and Columbus Avenue, on the neighborhood's western side; Ruggles is also a stop on the Providence/Stoughton, Franklin/Foxboro and Needham commuter rail lines, though some trains pass it. Washington Street, Blue Hill Avenue, Columbus Avenue and Melnea Cass Boulevard are the main roads.

  • Orange Line: Ruggles, Roxbury Crossing
  • Commuter rail (Providence/Stoughton, Franklin/Foxboro and Needham Lines): Ruggles
  • Silver Line SL4 and SL5 (bus rapid transit): Nubian

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Roxbury is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Roxbury.

Franklin Park

Olmsted's park of roughly 485 acres, designed in the 1890s and Boston's largest open space, with the Franklin Park Zoo, the William Devine Golf Course and White Stadium; it connects Roxbury with Dorchester, Jamaica Plain, Mattapan and Roslindale.

Franklin Park Zoo

A 72-acre zoo in the northeast part of Franklin Park, opened on October 4, 1912 and run by Zoo New England, with a 3-acre Tropical Forest building under a fabric dome.

Roxbury Heritage State Park

A state park at 183 Roxbury Street in John Eliot Square, built around the Dillaway-Thomas House of about 1750, now a museum, with landscaped grounds looking toward the Boston skyline.

Shirley-Eustis House

Governor William Shirley's summer house at 33 Shirley Street, built between 1747 and 1751 and later owned by Governor William Eustis; a National Historic Landmark since October 9, 1960, open to the public since 1991.

Highland Park and the Cochituate Standpipe

The park on the summit of Fort Hill, landscaped by Frederick Law Olmsted, with a Victorian tower designed by Nathaniel J. Bradlee around the standpipe Roxbury built in 1869 for its water system.

First Church in Roxbury

The congregation's meetinghouse in John Eliot Square, described as Boston's oldest surviving wooden meeting house of the Federal period.

Malcolm X-Ella Little-Collins House

A house at 72 Dale Street, built on part of the former Abijah Seaver estate, designated a Boston Landmark in 1998 and surveyed by city archaeologists in 2016.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Roxbury?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Roxbury on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $712,000, across 20 sales, against $654,125 across 24 sales in 2024. The condominium median was $618,000 in 2025, across 117 sales, against $600,000 across 149 sales in 2024. Much of Roxbury's housing is row houses and multi-family buildings, which the single-family figure does not cover.

Does Roxbury have commuter rail?

Yes, at Ruggles, on Tremont Street, a stop on the Providence/Stoughton, Franklin/Foxboro and Needham Lines (some trains pass it) and on the Orange Line, which also stops at Roxbury Crossing. The Silver Line SL4 and SL5 run on Washington Street from Nubian station to South Station and Downtown Crossing.

Which school district serves Roxbury?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

Why is Dudley Square now called Nubian Square?

After a non-binding advisory question on the November 5, 2019 ballot, the city's Public Improvement Commission approved the change on December 19, 2019, and the MBTA's bus station there is now Nubian station. The new name refers to Nubia, a region in present-day Egypt and Sudan; the old one honored Thomas Dudley, a governor of the Massachusetts Bay Colony.
