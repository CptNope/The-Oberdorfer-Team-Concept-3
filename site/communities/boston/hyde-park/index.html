Living in Hyde Park, Boston, MA

Three commuter rail lines, Mother Brookand the Neponset River at the city's southern edge.

Hyde Park is Boston's southernmost neighborhood, 7.9 miles south of downtown, incorporated as a town in 1868 from parts of Dorchester, Dedham and Milton and annexed to Boston in 1912. Single-family and two-family homes made up nearly three-quarters of its housing units in the city's open space plan, and the Boston Planning & Development Agency counted 33,009 people there in 2020. Three commuter rail lines stop at its Hyde Park, Fairmount and Readville stations.

About Hyde Park

Where it is

Hyde Park is Boston's southernmost neighborhood. The George Wright Golf Course lies partly in Hyde Park and partly in Roslindale, the Stony Brook Reservation runs south from West Roxbury to Mother Brook in Hyde Park, and the city's open space plan places Hyde Park's eastern sections next to Mattapan. In Readville it meets Milton to the south and Dedham to the west. Hyde Park Avenue and the Amtrak and MBTA tracks cross the neighborhood, with most of its open space to their west.

Hyde Park was assembled from the edges of three older towns: Grew's Woods in Dorchester, Fairmount in Milton and Readville in Dedham. Mother Brook, ordered dug in 1639 to divert Charles River water to the Neponset, powered mills in what is now Readville, which was called Dedham Low Plains until 1847, when it was renamed for James Read of the Dedham Manufacturing Company. Henry Grew settled there in 1847, rail lines extended from Boston in the 1850s, and in 1856 Alpheus P. Blake's Fairmount Land Company and Twenty Associates bought 100 acres and began building houses. On April 22, 1868, the Town of Hyde Park was incorporated in Norfolk County from about 1,300 acres of Dorchester, 800 of Dedham and 700 of Milton. Its voters chose to join Boston in 1912.

Cleary Square and Logan Square frame the central business district, and the Hyde Park Branch Library, which opened in its present building in September 1899 and became a Boston Public Library branch at annexation, is the only branch that began as a town library. Fairmount Hill, on a hillside above the Neponset River, keeps late-19th- and early-20th-century houses in Italianate, Gothic Revival and Victorian styles. Readville was industrial: Camp Meigs trained Union regiments there from 1862 to 1865, including the 54th Massachusetts; the Readville Trotting Park followed on the site until 1937; the B.F. Sturtevant Company had a 20-acre plant employing 1,500 people by 1912; and the New Haven Railroad's locomotive and car shops ran until 1960.

Boston's 2008-2014 open space plan counted single-family homes as 48% of Hyde Park's housing units and two-family homes as 24%, and found most of its open space west of Hyde Park Avenue and the rail line; the state Department of Conservation and Recreation held 470 acres in the neighborhood, mainly in the Stony Brook and Neponset River reservations. A rezoning begun in April 2008 was approved by the Boston Zoning Commission in February 2012. Cleary Square is now the subject of a Squares + Streets small area plan: the Planning Department released a draft plan and zoning map on March 17, 2026, followed by a revised draft.

Homes in Hyde Park

Hyde Park's housing is mainly single-family and two-family homes: Boston's 2008-2014 open space plan put single-family homes at 48% of units and two-family homes at 24%, nearly three-quarters together. Fairmount Hill keeps houses in late-19th- and early-20th-century styles, including Italianate, Gothic Revival and Victorian. The Boston Planning & Development Agency's tabulation of the 2016-2020 American Community Survey counts 59.5% of occupied units as owner-occupied (7,893 units), compared with 35.3% citywide.

Within the neighborhoodHyde Park

Corners ofHyde Park.

Cleary Square and Logan Square

The two squares between which Hyde Park's central business district lies; Cleary Square is the focus of a draft Squares + Streets plan released in 2026.

Fairmount Hill

A hillside above the Neponset River, laid out from 1856 by the Fairmount Land Company, with late-19th- and early-20th-century Italianate, Gothic Revival and Victorian houses; Fairmount station sits at Fairmount Avenue and the Truman Highway.

Readville

The southern section, formerly Dedham Low Plains, bordered by Milton and Dedham, with Wolcott Square, Readville station at 1800 Hyde Park Avenue, a commuter rail maintenance and layover yard, and the Neponset River and Fowl Meadow wetlands.

Getting around

Commuting from Hyde Park

The Fairmount Line runs from Readville through Fairmount and six more stops to South Station, and Fairmount station is in fare zone 1A. Hyde Park station, at 1 Pingree Street and reached by ramps from the River Street bridge, is a Providence/Stoughton Line stop with some Franklin/Foxboro trains, running to Ruggles, Back Bay and South Station; Readville, at 1800 Hyde Park Avenue, is the outer terminus for most Fairmount trains and a Franklin/Foxboro stop. MBTA bus routes connect the neighborhood with the subway at Forest Hills and Mattapan.

  • Fairmount Line: Fairmount, Readville
  • Providence/Stoughton Line: Hyde Park, Readville (some trains)
  • Franklin/Foxboro Line: Readville, Hyde Park (some trains)

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Hyde Park is served by Boston Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Hyde Park.

Stony Brook Reservation

A 616-acre DCR reservation, one of the five original reservations of the Metropolitan Park Commission, running south from Washington Street in West Roxbury to Mother Brook in Hyde Park, with hiking and biking trails, fishing at Turtle Pond and an ice rink.

George Wright Golf Course

An 18-hole City of Boston course designed by Donald Ross and opened on April 23, 1938, at 420 West Street, on 156 acres enclosed by a stone wall.

Mother Brook

A canal ordered by Dedham in 1639 and flowing by 1641, about 3.6 miles long, carrying Charles River water to the Neponset River in Hyde Park; the page calls it the first man-made canal in the present-day United States, and it now forms part of a flood-control system.

Camp Meigs Park

A park of nearly three acres at 11 Stanbro Street in Readville, on part of the Civil War training camp, dedicated by the Commonwealth on July 11, 1903, with a cannon-shaped granite monument and a later marker to the regiments that trained there, among them the 54th and 55th Massachusetts Infantry and 5th Massachusetts Cavalry.

Hyde Park Branch Library

Opened in its present building in September 1899 as the town library, which had started in 1873; it joined the Boston Public Library at annexation in 1912, and a January 2000 addition by Schwartz/Silver Architects doubled its size.

Neponset River Reservation

DCR land along the Neponset River, which runs past Readville and below Fairmount Hill; with Stony Brook, it accounts for most of the 470 acres the state holds in the neighborhood.

The Boston city guide

What is the median home price in Hyde Park?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, which lists Hyde Park on its own row, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $675,000, across 101 sales, up from $655,000 across 118 sales in 2024. The condominium median was $482,000 across 54 sales in 2025, against $441,500 across 48 sales in 2024.

Does Hyde Park have commuter rail?

Yes, on three lines. The Fairmount Line stops at Fairmount and Readville, Hyde Park station is on the Providence/Stoughton Line with some Franklin/Foxboro trains, and Readville is also a Franklin/Foxboro stop. Fairmount station is in fare zone 1A.

Which school district serves Hyde Park?

Boston Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district; review its profile with DESE.

When did Hyde Park become part of Boston?

Hyde Park was incorporated as a town in Norfolk County on April 22, 1868, from parts of Dorchester, Dedham and Milton, and its voters chose to join Boston in 1912.

Is Hyde Park getting Squares + Streets zoning?

Cleary Square is the subject of a Squares + Streets small area plan. The Planning Department released a draft plan and zoning map on March 17, 2026, and a revised draft later in 2026; check the city's planning page for its current status.
