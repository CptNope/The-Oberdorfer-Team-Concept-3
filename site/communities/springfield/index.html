Living in Springfield, MA

Porches, parks and river light,in a city that knows its neighborhoods.

Springfield is the seat of Hampden County, on the eastern bank of the Connecticut River about 24 miles north of Hartford. Sixty-eight percent of its housing is single-family homes and duplexes, and 41 percent was built before 1940. Union Station offers Amtrak and Hartford Line trains, and I-91 and I-291 run through the city.

About Springfield

Springfield sits on the eastern bank of the Connecticut River, about 24 miles north of Hartford, and serves as the Hampden County seat. William Pynchon founded it in 1636 as Agawam Plantation, and it became a city in 1852. The federal armory here began making small arms in 1794 and closed in 1968; its Main Arsenal now holds the National Park Service's collection of historic military firearms. James Naismith invented basketball in Springfield in 1891, and the Hall of Fame stands on the riverfront.

The city calls itself the City of Homes, a name tied to its Victorian houses. The McKnight Historic District, planned in 1881, has more than 900 of them, and the neighborhood around Forest Park has more than 600. Sixteen Acres holds much of the city's post-World War II building, and Indian Orchard grew up as its own mill town on the Chicopee River. Forest Park covers about 735 acres, with a zoo, Porter Lake and the Bright Nights holiday display. Union Station handles Amtrak, Hartford Line trains and intercity buses.

Homes in Springfield

The city's 2018 Housing Study found that 68 percent of Springfield's housing stock is single-family homes and duplexes, and that only 10 percent of units are in buildings with 20 or more units. It also found that 41 percent of units were built before 1940 and only 14 percent since 1980.

Getting around

Commuting from Springfield

Springfield has no MBTA commuter rail. Union Station on Frank B. Murray Street serves Amtrak, the CTrail Hartford Line to New Haven, PVTA local buses and Peter Pan intercity buses. I-91 follows the riverfront, I-291 loops northeast from downtown to the Massachusetts Turnpike, and the Turnpike itself runs just north of the city through Chicopee.

  • Amtrak (Northeast Regional, Valley Flyer, Vermonter, Lake Shore Limited): Springfield Union Station
  • CTrail Hartford Line: Springfield Union Station

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Questions

Asked aboutSpringfield.

What is the median home price in Springfield, MA?

For calendar year 2025, The Warren Group reports a single-family median sale price of $290,000 in Springfield, across 1,040 sales. In 2024 the median was $280,000 on 1,030 sales. Source: The Warren Group, MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town.

Does Springfield have commuter rail?

Not MBTA commuter rail. Springfield Union Station is served by Amtrak (Northeast Regional, Valley Flyer, Vermonter and Lake Shore Limited) and by the CTrail Hartford Line to Hartford and New Haven.

Which school district serves Springfield?

Springfield Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

Why is Springfield called the City of Homes?

The nickname comes from its Victorian residential architecture. The McKnight Historic District alone has more than 900 Victorian houses.
