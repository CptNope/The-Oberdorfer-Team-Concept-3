Living in Northborough, MA

Northborough, along the Post Road:mill villages, an aqueduct and Mount Pisgah.

Northborough is a Worcester County town of 15,741 people (2020 Census), set off from Westborough as a district in 1766 and made a town in 1775. Its center lines the old Boston Post Road, now Route 20, with colonial, Federal and Greek Revival houses and later subdivisions around it. I-290 has three Northborough exits, Route 9 crosses the south end, and the nearest MBTA station is Westborough.

About Northborough

Northborough was the north part of Westborough until January 23, 1766, when it became a separate district; it became a town on August 23, 1775, and in 1807 annexed part of Marlborough. Its first meetinghouse went up in 1746 near the Post Road, where Cold Harbor and Howard Brooks meet. The Assabet River and the brooks powered grist mills, a fulling mill by 1749 and, from 1814, textile mills at Woodside; manufacturing began at Chapinville in 1832. Boots and shoes peaked in the 1860s, and comb and button making, begun in the late 1830s, was the leading industry by 1855. A railroad to Framingham opened through town in 1855 and was extended to Fitchburg in 1866.

Main Street is the old Upper Boston Post Road, and Milestone 33, at 143 East Main Street, is still engraved '33 Miles to Boston.' After the railroad arrived, the center spread southeast with a bank organized in 1854 (later the Northborough National Bank), a Second Empire town hall completed in 1868 and the Italianate First Baptist Church of 1860 at 52 Main Street, the address of the Northborough Historical Society. The Boston-Worcester Turnpike across the southern tip of town was rebuilt as four-lane Route 9 in 1930 and 1931, and the Massachusetts Historical Commission describes the 1930 Route 9 and Route 20 interchange as one of the earliest cloverleafs in the state. To the north, the Wachusett Aqueduct, built from 1897 to 1905, crosses the Assabet on its way to Marlborough, and Mount Pisgah rises to 715 feet, the highest point in town.

Suburban building after the Second World War followed the construction of I-290, which the Commission's 1983 survey saw as a pressure on the remaining orchards of Ball Hill and the farmsteads on South Street. As an MBTA adjacent community, Northborough must zone a district of at least 50 acres where multifamily housing is allowed at 15 units per acre. The 2024 Annual Town Meeting warrant carried a Multifamily Development Overlay District with two sub-districts, Southwest Connector and Downtown (about 47.94 and 11.29 acres on nine parcels in the Planning Board's February 2024 presentation), and a new Highway Business Southwest district for the area around Avalon of Northborough and Northborough Crossing. On December 10, 2024, the state's Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities found the 59.2-acre district, with an estimated capacity of 886 multi-family units, compliant with the law, and the state's compliance list (as of October 21, 2026) includes Northborough.

Homes in Northborough

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1983 survey found seven surviving colonial two-story central-chimney houses, including the 1743 Samuel Gamwell house, and seven Federal double-chimney houses, from the period when brick came into use. Greek Revival two-story sidehall houses followed from 1830 to 1870, then Queen Anne and Colonial Revival frame houses, and post-war subdivisions came with I-290.

Where you mightlive in Northborough.

Northborough Center

The Main Street (Route 20) center, which spread southeast from the 1746 meetinghouse site after the 1855 railroad, where a bank organized in 1854 and the 1868 town hall were built.

Woodside

A mill site on the Assabet River where cotton and woolen manufacturing began in 1814; by 1915 the Woodside mill was the last one in town still running.

Chapinville

A manufacturing village from 1832, with the one-story brick Davis Mill of the 1830s and the four-story E. W. Chapin factory of the 1890s, with its central tower.

Ball Hill

An orchard area that the 1983 state survey named as farmland under pressure from post-war development.

Bartlett Pond

A pond where recreational cottages were built in the early 20th century.

Southwest Cutoff

The Route 20 corridor where Avalon of Northborough and Northborough Crossing stand; in 2024 the town proposed a Highway Business Southwest district to replace the zoning there.

Getting around

Commuting from Northborough

Northborough has no commuter rail station; the state classes it as an MBTA adjacent community because it borders Westborough, which has a stop on the Framingham/Worcester Line. I-290 has Northborough exits at Church Street and Hudson Street and at Solomon Pond Mall Road on the Marlborough line. Route 20 runs through the center, Route 9 crosses the south of town and Route 135 leads to Westborough.

Schools

Public schools

Northborough is served by Northborough Public Schools (prekindergarten to grade 8) and the Northborough-Southborough Regional School District (grades 9 to 12). Review the district's profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education, and confirm school assignment for any address with the district.

Places to knowin Northborough.

Mount Pisgah

At 715 feet the highest point in Northborough, on MassWildlife land with about three miles of trails kept by the Northborough Trails Committee and connections north to Linden Street in Berlin.

First Baptist Church of Northborough

An Italianate church of 1860 by Richard W. Newton at 52 Main Street, listed on the National Register in 2016; the Northborough Historical Society, founded in 1906, gives the same address for its museum.

Milestone 33

One of the 1767 Milestones on the Upper Boston Post Road, at 143 East Main Street, engraved '33 Miles to Boston'; the milestones were listed on the National Register in 1971.

Wachusett Aqueduct

A nine-mile aqueduct built from 1897 to 1905 from the Wachusett Reservoir through Berlin and Northborough to Marlborough, crossing the Assabet River in Northborough; listed on the National Register in 1990.

Northborough Free Library

The town library, founded in 1868 in the newly built town hall and now at 34 Main Street.

Daniel Holbrook Sr. House

A house at 35 Whitney Street, added to the National Register on May 11, 2026.

Northborough Historical Society museum

Exhibits of 1800s clothing, an 18th-century kitchen, a general store display and a working 1874 George Stevens pipe organ; the society counts 28 houses in town built before the War of Independence.

What is the median home price in Northborough, MA?

The median single-family sale price in Northborough for calendar year 2025 was $727,500, across 126 sales, up from $718,000 across 113 sales in 2024, according to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report. The condo median was $451,000 across 38 sales.

Does Northborough have commuter rail?

No. Northborough has no MBTA station; it borders Westborough, whose station is on the Framingham/Worcester Line, and the state classes Northborough as an MBTA adjacent community.

Which school district serves Northborough?

Northborough Public Schools serves prekindergarten through grade 8, and the Northborough-Southborough Regional School District serves grades 9 to 12 at Algonquin Regional High School on Bartlett Street. Review both profiles with DESE, and confirm school assignment for any address with the districts.

What is the property tax rate in Northborough?

Northborough's fiscal year 2026 tax rate is $14.82 per $1,000 of assessed value, according to the town's Assessors Department, which lists a single rate.

What is Northborough's MBTA Communities district?

As an MBTA adjacent community, Northborough must allow multifamily housing at 15 units per acre in a district of at least 50 acres. Its Multifamily Development Overlay District covers 59.2 acres in a Southwest Connector sub-district and a Downtown sub-district, with an estimated capacity of 886 units; the state found it compliant on December 10, 2024, and lists Northborough as compliant as of October 21, 2026.
