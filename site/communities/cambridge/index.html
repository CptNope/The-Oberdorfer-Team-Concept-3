Living in Cambridge, MA

Newtowne of 1630 on the Charles,five Red Line stops and a reservoir to the west.

Cambridge is a Middlesex County city of 118,403 people (2020 Census) on the north bank of the Charles River, across from Boston. Single-family homes are a small share of its housing: 6.4% of units, against 34.1% in buildings of more than 100 units, by the city's own count. The Warren Group recorded 105 single-family sales in 2025, a small market for a city of this size. Five Red Line stations run through Cambridge, from Alewife to Kendall/MIT, and Porter also has commuter rail.

About Cambridge

Cambridge was founded in December 1630 as Newtowne and renamed in May 1638 after the English university town, two years after the college that became Harvard was founded. George Washington took command of the Continental Army on Cambridge Common in 1775 and used the house at 105 Brattle Street as his first long-term headquarters during the siege of Boston. The West Boston Bridge of 1792 tied the town directly to Boston, and new bridges and roads turned estates and marshland into industrial and residential districts; the New England Glass Company, founded in 1818, with plants in what are now Kendall Square and East Cambridge, was the city's largest employer for much of the 19th century. Cambridge became a city in 1846. MIT moved here from Boston in 1916.

The city recognizes 13 neighborhoods on about 6.4 square miles of land. Squares strung along Massachusetts Avenue anchor them: Harvard Square beside the 22.4-acre Harvard Yard, where Massachusetts Hall has stood since 1720, Central Square with City Hall, and Porter Square farther north, while Kendall Square grew from salt marsh and 19th-century industry into an office and laboratory district beside MIT. Cambridgeport keeps streets of triple-deckers near the river. In the west, Fresh Pond Reservation surrounds the city's drinking-water reservoir, and Mount Auburn Cemetery, founded in 1831, straddles the line with Watertown. Danehy Park, a 50-acre former city dump in North Cambridge, opened as parkland in 1990.

On February 10, 2025, the City Council voted to allow multifamily housing citywide. Multifamily housing is now allowed in every zoning district except Open Space, and in the new base district, Residence C-1, residential buildings may rise to four stories (45 feet), or to six stories (74 feet) on lots of at least 5,000 square feet when 20 percent of the housing is income-restricted. Existing single-family homes may remain and new ones may still be built, and the city's inclusionary housing program continues. Since October 2020, the 100 Percent Affordable Housing Overlay, amended in 2023 and 2025, has allowed permanently affordable buildings denser than base zoning; completed projects include 52 New Street (106 rental units) and 116 Norfolk Street (62). The state lists Cambridge as compliant with the MBTA Communities law.

Homes in Cambridge

By the city's inventory as of June 30, 2025, 6.4% of Cambridge's housing units are single-family homes, 11.9% are in two-unit buildings and 10.5% in three-unit buildings, while 34.1% are in buildings of more than 100 units. About two-thirds of occupied units (66.5%) are renter-occupied and 33.5% owner-occupied (2020 to 2024 American Community Survey). With so few single-family homes, The Warren Group's 105 single-family sales in 2025 reflect only a narrow part of the market.

Where you mightlive in Cambridge.

Harvard Square

A triangular plaza where Massachusetts Avenue, Brattle Street and John F. Kennedy Street meet beside Harvard Yard, served by Harvard station on the Red Line and a bus hub.

Central Square

The seat of city government, centered where Massachusetts Avenue, Prospect Street and Western Avenue meet; a state-designated Cultural District since 2012, with Central station on the Red Line.

Kendall Square

Former Charles River salt marsh and industrial district where Main Street meets Broadway, named for the Kendall Boiler and Tank Company, now an office and lab district beside MIT with the Kendall/MIT Red Line stop.

Cambridgeport

A mostly residential neighborhood of triple-deckers between Massachusetts Avenue and the Charles River, with Magazine Beach and Fort Washington Park.

East Cambridge

The old Lechmere Point, once home to glassworks and factory cottages, between the Charles River, Somerville and Broadway, with Lechmere station relocated in 2022 for the Green Line Extension.

Porter Square

The square where Massachusetts Avenue meets Somerville Avenue, spanning Cambridge and Somerville, renamed in 1896 for Porter's Hotel and once lined with cattle yards; the Red Line reached Porter station in 1984, and it is also a Fitchburg Line commuter rail stop.

Getting around

Commuting from Cambridge

Five Red Line stations serve Cambridge, from Alewife, which has a 2,471-space MBTA garage, through Porter, Harvard and Central to Kendall/MIT. Porter is also a Fitchburg Line commuter rail stop, about 15 minutes from Boston's North Station, and Lechmere in East Cambridge is on the Green Line. Route 2 enters at Alewife and runs with US 3 and Route 16 along Alewife Brook and Fresh Pond Parkways, then with US 3 along Memorial Drive beside the Charles.

  • Red Line (subway): Alewife, Porter, Harvard, Central, Kendall/MIT
  • Fitchburg Line (commuter rail): Porter
  • Green Line (subway): Lechmere

Check current schedules with the MBTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Places to knowin Cambridge.

Fresh Pond Reservation

162 acres of open space around the 155-acre Fresh Pond reservoir, part of the city's drinking-water system, with nature walks and ranger programs.

Mount Auburn Cemetery

Established in 1831 between Cambridge and Watertown, a National Historic Landmark open to the public at no cost every day of the year.

Longfellow House-Washington's Headquarters

A National Historic Site at 105 Brattle Street, Washington's headquarters during the siege of Boston and later Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's home, with seasonal tours and a formal garden.

Cambridge Common

Public parkland where Washington took command of the Continental Army in 1775.

Harvard Yard

A 22.4-acre grassy enclosure bounded principally by Massachusetts Avenue, Cambridge Street, Broadway and Quincy Street, with Massachusetts Hall (1720), Harvard's oldest building, and Widener Library.

Danehy Park

A 50-acre North Cambridge park on a former brickyard and city dump, opened in 1990, with athletic fields, paths, a wetland area and the city's first Miyawaki forest, planted in 2021; the eastern entrance is at 99 Sherman Street.

MIT Museum

MIT's museum, founded in 1971, reopened on October 2, 2022 in the Gambrill Center at 314 Main Street in Kendall Square, beside the Kendall/MIT station; its collections include holography, robotics, architecture and the history of MIT.

Cambridge Public Library

The main library at 449 Broadway joins a Richardsonian Romanesque building of 1888 by Van Brunt & Howe with an expansion that opened on November 8, 2009; six neighborhood branches serve the rest of the city.

What is the median home price in Cambridge, MA?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, the median single-family sale price in Cambridge for calendar year 2025 was $2,175,000, across 105 sales. In 2024 it was $2,135,500, across 98 sales. Single-family homes are only 6.4% of the city's housing units, so these figures describe a small part of the market.

Does Cambridge have commuter rail?

Yes. Porter is a stop on the MBTA Fitchburg Line, about 15 minutes from North Station. Cambridge also has five Red Line stations, Alewife, Porter, Harvard, Central and Kendall/MIT, and Lechmere on the Green Line.

Which school district serves Cambridge?

Cambridge Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

What is the property tax rate in Cambridge, MA?

According to the Massachusetts Division of Local Services, Cambridge's FY2026 residential property tax rate is $6.67 per $1,000 of assessed value, and commercial, industrial and personal property pay $14.07. Owner-occupants who qualify for Cambridge's 30% residential exemption have $510,208 taken off their assessed value in FY2026, according to the City of Cambridge.

What changed in Cambridge's zoning in 2025?

On February 10, 2025, the City Council voted to allow multifamily housing in every district except Open Space. In the base Residence C-1 district, residential buildings may reach four stories (45 feet), or six stories (74 feet) on lots of at least 5,000 square feet when 20 percent of the housing is income-restricted. Single-family homes may still be kept or built.
