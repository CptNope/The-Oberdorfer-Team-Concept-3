Living in Westborough, MA

Westborough, mapped out:the center, the lake and the trails.

Westborough is a Worcester County town of 21,567 people (2020 Census), incorporated in 1717. Its center keeps colonial and 19th-century homes, and The Warren Group counted 134 single-family sales in 2025. Westborough station is on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line, and the Massachusetts Turnpike, I-495 and Route 9 cross the town, linking it east to Boston and west to Worcester.

About Westborough

Westborough was incorporated in 1717 as the hundredth town in Massachusetts, set off from Marlborough with twenty-seven families. Eli Whitney, later the inventor of the cotton gin, was born here in 1765, and a stone on Eli Whitney Street marks the spot. In 1810 the road between Boston and Worcester became an official turnpike through town, today's Route 9. Once the railroad came through the center, the commercial life of the town followed it, leaving brick blocks downtown and smaller turnpike villages like Wessonville.

The land is a broad central plain ringed by rounded hills, with a large cedar swamp at its eastern end and the Assabet River along part of the northern edge. Lake Chauncy, in the north, has a summer beach open to Westborough and Northborough residents and is used for swimming, boating and fishing. The Westborough Charm Bracelet, a roughly 28-mile loop trail developed since 2000 by the Westborough Community Land Trust and the town's Open Space Preservation Committee, links most of the major open spaces, with side trails planned to reach schools and neighborhoods.

The north end of town, around Lake Chauncy, was long given over to state institutions. The state opened a reform school on the lake in 1848, described as the first publicly funded reform school in the United States. In 1884 its grounds passed to the new Westborough State Hospital, which opened in 1886 as the first state homeopathic psychiatric hospital in New England, on more than 600 acres between Lyman Street and the lake. The hospital closed in 2010, and the complex was demolished in 2019 despite its 1994 National Register listing. After voters approved a sale in 2016, the town sold 37 acres of the former hospital site on Lyman Street, at the Northborough line, to Pulte Homes, which the Worcester Business Journal reported was planning 700 units in 14 buildings.

Homes in Westborough

The Massachusetts Historical Commission notes two-story, center-chimney colonial houses, worker cottages grouped in the center and Italianate, Second Empire and Queen Anne houses on High Street. The Vintonville Historic District, just east of the center, is made up of modest houses on smaller lots, largely built between 1860 and 1890.

Where you mightlive in Westborough.

Westborough Center

The downtown, with multi-story brick commercial blocks near the railroad and the former meetinghouse site.

Vintonville

A 20-acre historic district east of the center with about 80 properties, mostly modest houses from 1860 to 1890, on streets such as Cottage, Elm and South.

Wessonville

A former turnpike village on Route 9 that once had a tavern, store and thread factory.

Lake Chauncy area

The northern part of town around Lake Chauncy, once home to the state reform school and state hospital grounds.

West Main Street Historic District

About 45 acres of the historic center along West Main Street, roughly bounded by Milk, Main, Blake and Fay Streets, with Greek Revival and late Victorian buildings; listed in 1987 and enlarged in 1990, 2006 and 2009.

Getting around

Commuting from Westborough

Westborough station, at Smith Parkway and Fisher Street with 448 parking spaces, sits between Grafton and Southborough on the Framingham/Worcester Line. The Turnpike and I-495 meet at the Hopkinton line, and Route 9 follows the old Boston-Worcester turnpike.

What is the median home price in Westborough, MA?

The median single-family sale price in Westborough for calendar year 2025 was $752,000, across 134 sales, down from $780,000 in 2024, according to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Does Westborough have commuter rail?

Yes. Westborough station, at Smith Parkway and Fisher Street, is on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line between Grafton and Southborough.

Which school district serves Westborough?

Westborough Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.

What is the Cedar Swamp in Westborough?

A wetland of about 2,675 acres east of the town center, extending into Hopkinton, that contains the headwaters of the Sudbury River. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Cedar Swamp Archeological District, and I-90 and I-495 cross parts of it.
