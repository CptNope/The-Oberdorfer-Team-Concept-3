Living in Grafton Hill, Worcester, MA

Three-decker rows on the hillthat Grafton Street climbs out of downtown.

Grafton Hill is a neighborhood on Worcester's East Side, on one of the city's seven named hills, anchored by Grafton Street (Route 122) and Providence Street in southeastern Worcester. Its western slope along Dorchester Street holds rows of three-deckers from the 1890s, among the earliest in the Grafton Hill and Vernon Hill area. WRTA Routes 5 and 12 run on Grafton Street, and Worcester's Union Station, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line, is downtown.

About Grafton Hill

Where it is

Grafton Hill rises in southeastern Worcester, anchored by Grafton Street and Providence Street; its lower western slope reaches Dorchester Street, and the city places Mulcahy Field, at 158 Dorchester Street, in the neighborhood. Massasoit Road spans Grafton Hill, Union Hill and the Broad Meadow Brook area.

Grafton Hill takes its name from the road that climbs it. The hill, once called Sagatabscot, is counted by the city among Worcester's seven hills, and Grafton Street was an early route out of the town center: the Blackstone Canal, which opened in 1828, ran south parallel to Summer and Grafton Streets, and the Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey records a brewery built at the head of Grafton Street between 1830 and 1870. Public transit followed the street up the hill. Worcester's first horsecar line, opened in 1863, had a spur on Front and Grafton Streets to the railroad station, and a streetcar line on Grafton Street opened in 1890.

The streetcar years shaped what stands on the hill today. The Grafton Street School, on the north side of Grafton Street between Fay and Wall Streets, opened in 1879 and gained a second building in 1899; its Wikipedia article notes that the area began as suburban and filled in with triple-deckers, which drove the school's expansions. The National Register nomination for the Patrick McGrath Three-Decker, a Queen Anne house of about 1894 on Dorchester Street, describes it as part of a row of similar houses on the lower western slope of Grafton Hill, in the earliest core of three-decker building in the Grafton Hill and Vernon Hill area. North of Grafton Street, four Colonial Revival three-deckers of about 1928 on Ingleside Avenue, which runs north from Grafton Street to Plantation Street, form a small National Register historic district.

The city marked the neighborhood formally in October 2025, when it installed a granite Grafton Hill sign on Grafton Street, paid for by the CSX Community Investment Fund. Recreation centers on Mulcahy Field on Dorchester Street, a city ballfield and playground, and a Grafton Hill neighborhood meeting on the city's calendar in April 2025 was held in the community room at Mass Audubon's Broad Meadow Brook sanctuary on Massasoit Road, a large urban sanctuary of woods, fields, streams and marsh.

Homes in Grafton Hill

The National Register record for the Patrick McGrath Three-Decker places Dorchester Street, on Grafton Hill's lower western slope, in the earliest core of Worcester's three-decker development, built up as rows of similar houses in the 1890s. Later three-deckers on the hill, such as the four c. 1928 houses of the Ingleside Avenue Historic District, carry Colonial Revival details. No published count of housing types or ownership for Grafton Hill alone was found.

Corners ofGrafton Hill.

Grafton Street

The neighborhood's spine and Route 122, with the 1879 and 1899 Grafton Street School buildings on its north side between Fay and Wall Streets and WRTA buses toward Blithewood and Edgemere.

Dorchester Street and the western slope

Rows of 1890s three-deckers on the lower western slope, including the Patrick McGrath Three-Decker at 50 Dorchester Street, with Mulcahy Field farther along at 158 Dorchester Street.

Ingleside Avenue

A street running north from Grafton Street to Plantation Street; four Colonial Revival three-deckers at 218-220 and 226-228 Ingleside Avenue, built about 1928, form a National Register historic district.

Getting around

Commuting from Grafton Hill

Grafton Hill has no rail station of its own; the nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Route 5 (Blithewood/Massasoit via Grafton Street) and Route 12 (Edgemere Crossing and DTA/RMV via Grafton Street) run through the neighborhood.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line: Worcester (Union Station, 2 Washington Square)

Check current schedules with the MBTA and the WRTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Grafton Hill is served by Worcester Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Grafton Hill.

Grafton Street School

Two school buildings at 311 Grafton Street: a Queen Anne building of 1879 by E. Boyden & Son, enlarged in 1897, and a Colonial Revival building of 1899 by J. William Patston; listed on the National Register in 1980.

Mulcahy Field

A city park of about 3.4 acres at 158 Dorchester Street with baseball and softball fields, a playground and a basketball court, named for Capt. Michael J. Mulcahy, a physical education instructor at the junior high school on Dorchester Street.

Patrick McGrath Three-Decker

A Queen Anne three-decker of about 1894 at 50 Dorchester Street, built as one of a row of similar houses and listed on the National Register in 1990.

Ingleside Avenue Historic District

Four Colonial Revival three-deckers built about 1928 north of Grafton Street, listed on the National Register in 1990.

Grafton Hill neighborhood marker

A granite sign installed on Grafton Street in October 2025, designed by Serrato Signs with granite from Empire Granite and funded by the CSX Community Investment Fund.

Broad Meadow Brook Wildlife Sanctuary

Mass Audubon's sanctuary at 414 Massasoit Road, with more than 400 acres of woods, fields, streams and marsh on well-marked trails; a Grafton Hill neighborhood meeting was held in its community room in April 2025.

The Worcester city guide

What is the median home price in Grafton Hill, Worcester?

The Warren Group does not report Grafton Hill separately. Citywide, the median single-family sale price in Worcester for calendar year 2025 was $430,000 across 884 sales, up from $420,000 across 904 sales in 2024, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Is there commuter rail near Grafton Hill?

Grafton Hill has no station of its own. The nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Routes 5 and 12 run along Grafton Street.

Which school district serves Grafton Hill?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is by address and is set by the district; review the district's profile with DESE.

Is Grafton Hill the same as Union Hill?

The names overlap. Wikipedia's Grafton Hill article treats Grafton Hill and Union Hill as names for the hill once called Sagatabscot, one of the city's seven hills, while its list of Worcester neighborhoods names Union Hill, Grafton Hill and Vernon Hill as separate East Side neighborhoods.

What kind of housing is on Grafton Hill?

Three-deckers are the documented type. The National Register record for 50 Dorchester Street places the hill's lower western slope in the earliest core of Worcester's three-decker building in the 1890s, and four c. 1928 Colonial Revival three-deckers on Ingleside Avenue form a historic district.
