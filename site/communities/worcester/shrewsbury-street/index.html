Living in Shrewsbury Street, Worcester, MA

A restaurant corridor from Union Stationto East Park and the hills beyond.

Shrewsbury Street is a neighborhood on Worcester's East Side, named for the commercial street that runs about 7,000 feet from I-290 near Union Station to Route 9. The street is known for its restaurants, the 1936 Boulevard Diner and the 23-acre East Park, and in 2023 a Telegram & Gazette opinion column counted several apartment projects under construction or approved on or near it. Union Station's footbridge has an entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street, a short walk to the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line.

About Shrewsbury Street

Where it is

The neighborhood follows Shrewsbury Street on the city's East Side, a corridor of about 7,000 feet running at an angle from I-290 near Union Station to Route 9, with East Park at 180 Shrewsbury Street and side streets such as Mulberry Street climbing toward Belmont Hill.

The western end of Shrewsbury Street was once the Meadows, where the workers building the Blackstone Canal were kept in camps in the 1820s. Older landmarks along it include the Greek Revival Leonard Sturtevant House, built about 1849 at 84 Mulberry Street near the base of Belmont Hill, and the stone griffins at the gate of East Park, which once supported the arch of the train shed at the old Union Station and were moved to the park in 1916.

A 2016 city announcement quotes a city councilor calling Shrewsbury Street one of Worcester's most successful commercial corridors, and it is best known for restaurants: a July 2023 Telegram & Gazette opinion column by Raymond V. Mariano counted more than 30 restaurants and places to eat, the stretch some call restaurant row. The Boulevard Diner, a 1936 Worcester Lunch Car at 155 Shrewsbury Street with yellow ceramic panels and a barrel roof, is on the National Register. Our Lady of Mount Carmel Church, completed in 1928 on Mulberry Street about 25 feet from an interstate highway built in front of it in the late 1950s and early 1960s, was relegated to non-sacred use by a diocesan decree in 2017. A streetscape project designed for the city by BETA Group added street trees, stamped and colored crosswalks, neckdowns and seasonal plantings in the median, and the whole street was resurfaced in 2016.

Housing along the corridor is changing. Mariano's column counted six apartment projects on or near the street, three under construction, two approved and one in review, that together would add nearly 750 apartments, nearly three-quarters of them studios or one-bedrooms, with some buildings six or seven stories high; in the column's view, a city request for proposals for public works land on Shrewsbury and Albany Streets could add more. In 2024 Union Station gained a center platform and a pedestrian bridge with an entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street.

Homes in Shrewsbury Street

Shrewsbury Street's newer housing is in mid-rise apartment buildings: a July 2023 Telegram & Gazette opinion column by Raymond V. Mariano counted six projects in progress or planned that would add nearly 750 apartments, nearly three-quarters of them studios or one-bedrooms, with some buildings six or seven stories high. Older houses stand on the side streets toward Belmont Hill, among them the Leonard Sturtevant House (about 1849) on Mulberry Street.

Corners ofShrewsbury Street.

Washington Square end

The western end by Union Station and I-290, once the Meadows beside the canal, where the station's footbridge now has an entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street.

Restaurant row

The central blocks with the street's restaurants and bars, including the Boulevard Diner at 155 and a former Buick dealership at 72 now used for dining.

Mulberry Street and Belmont Hill

Side streets climbing toward Belmont Hill, with the c. 1849 Leonard Sturtevant House at 84 Mulberry Street and the site of Our Lady of Mount Carmel Church at 24 Mulberry Street.

East Park

The 23-acre city park at 180 Shrewsbury Street, whose wooded hillsides connect by trail to Bell Pond, Green Hill Park and Lake Quinsigamond.

Getting around

Commuting from Shrewsbury Street

Union Station, at the street's western end, is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston's South Station, reached by a footbridge with an entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street. I-290 crosses the western end and Route 9 meets the eastern end. WRTA Route 15 runs to Shrewsbury Center via Shrewsbury Street and Route 9.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line: Worcester (Union Station, 2 Washington Square)

Check current schedules with the MBTA and the WRTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Places to knowin Shrewsbury Street.

Cristoforo Colombo (East) Park

A 23-acre park at 180 Shrewsbury Street with an amphitheater, ball fields, courts, a playground, a spray park and trails; its 1916 gateway uses two stone griffins from the old Union Station, and the Massachusetts Symphony Orchestra plays there before the city's Independence Day fireworks.

Boulevard Diner

Worcester Lunch Car No. 730, built in 1936 at 155 Shrewsbury Street, with a barrel roof, yellow ceramic panels with red lettering and restored marble counters; listed on the National Register in 2000.

Union Station footbridge

The pedestrian bridge to the station's center island platform, opened in 2024 as part of a project of about $45 million, with a street entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street.

Leonard Sturtevant House

A locally distinctive Greek Revival house of about 1849 at 84 Mulberry Street, at the corner of Prospect Street; listed on the National Register in 1980.

The Worcester city guide

Is there a median home price for the Shrewsbury Street area?

The Warren Group reports Worcester only as a whole city, so there is no Shrewsbury Street figure. Citywide, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $430,000, across 884 sales, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

How close is Shrewsbury Street to the commuter rail?

Union Station is at the street's western end, and since 2024 its pedestrian bridge has had an entrance at 45 Shrewsbury Street. Worcester station is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston's South Station.

What kind of housing is being built on Shrewsbury Street?

Mostly mid-rise apartment buildings. A July 2023 Telegram & Gazette opinion column by Raymond V. Mariano counted six projects that would add nearly 750 apartments, nearly three-quarters of them studios or one-bedrooms, with some buildings six or seven stories high.

Which school district serves the Shrewsbury Street area?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district by address; review the district's profile with DESE.
