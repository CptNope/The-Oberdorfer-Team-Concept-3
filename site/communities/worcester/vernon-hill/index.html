Living in Vernon Hill, Worcester, MA

Three-decker streets on one of the seven hills,climbed by Vernon and Providence Streets.

Vernon Hill is a neighborhood in southeastern Worcester on one of the city's seven named hills, climbed by Vernon Street and by Providence Street, which carries Route 122A. Streetcars reached its slopes from the 1890s, and three-deckers built mostly from the 1910s to 1930 line its streets, with groups on View Street, Providence Street and Perry Avenue listed as National Register historic districts. The 15-acre Vernon Hill Park sits atop the hill at 144 Providence Street.

About Vernon Hill

Where it is

Vernon Hill rises in southeastern Worcester, along Vernon Street and Providence Street, with Vernon Hill Park at its top at 144 Providence Street. Perry Avenue, which runs parallel to and east of I-290, lies on its lower western slopes, and Wikipedia's list of Worcester neighborhoods places Vernon Hill on the East Side.

Vernon Hill is one of the seven hills on which Worcester, like Rome, is said to stand. Its main streets follow old routes: the Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey of the city infers a native trail running south toward Singletary Pond along the line of Vernon Street and Greenwood Street. In the Early Industrial period (1830 to 1870) the survey records a residential cluster on the west slope of Vernon Hill along the Ward Street and Millbury Street axis. The Upsala Street School, a Romanesque Revival building by the Worcester architect George Clemence at Upsala and Louise Streets, was finished in 1894 and enlarged in 1902 and 1923.

The street railway opened the hilltop. As the system was electrified, from the first electric line in 1891 to the last horsecars in November 1893, it added a line on Providence Street; Upsala Street cars crossed Vernon Hill, and tracks laid across Vernon Square in 1902 let them reach downtown by Green Street. In September 1912 the Providence Street line was extended 3,605 feet along Holcombe and Granite Streets. A streetcar also ran on Vernon Street, and as development pushed into the hill's steeper terrain in the 1910s, View Street was laid out and built with three-deckers. The George Fontaine Three-Decker at 141 Vernon Street, built about 1918 while the area was developing as a streetcar suburb, dates from this phase, as do the 40 three-deckers of the Euclid Avenue and Montrose Street district on a hillside between Vernon Street and Perry Avenue, most built between 1910 and 1930 and 27 of them with gambrel roofs.

The last wave came in the late 1920s. Ten three-deckers with seven period garages at 127 to 145 Providence Street, built between 1926 and 1930, and four on Perry Avenue, built about 1928 as infill on the lower slopes, date from Worcester's last period of three-decker construction. The National Register listed these two groups on February 9, 1990, with the View Street district, the George Fontaine and David Dworman three-deckers and the Euclid Avenue-Montrose Street district. The summit is public land: Vernon Hill Park, at 144 Providence Street, has ballfields, tennis and basketball courts, a pool run by the state Department of Conservation and Recreation, and the city's first off-leash dog park, opened in August 2017.

Homes in Vernon Hill

The three-decker is the documented housing type on Vernon Hill. National Register records describe groups built from the 1910s to 1930 on View Street, Euclid Avenue and Montrose Street, Providence Street and Perry Avenue, mostly in Colonial Revival and Craftsman styles, many with gambrel roofs and some with period garages, plus single listed three-deckers at 141 Vernon Street and 159 Providence Street. The 1894 Upsala Street School has been converted into apartments. No published count of housing types or ownership for Vernon Hill alone was found.

View Street

A street laid out in the 1910s on the hill's steeper ground, with eleven Colonial Revival three-deckers built between 1916 and 1930 at 7-17 and 8-16 View Street.

Perry Avenue and the lower slopes

A residential street running parallel to and east of I-290, where four three-deckers of about 1928 were built as infill on the lower slopes of Vernon Hill.

Getting around

Commuting from Vernon Hill

Route 122A leaves Route 146 at Providence Street and follows Providence and Winthrop Streets to cross I-290 before meeting Route 122 at Kelley Square. WRTA Route 1 runs via Providence and Granite Streets and Route 11 to The Fair Plaza via Vernon Hill and Greenwood Street. Commuter rail leaves from Worcester station at Union Station, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line.

Things to doVernon Hill

Places to knowin Vernon Hill.

Vernon Hill Park

A 15-acre city park atop the hill at 144 Providence Street with baseball and softball fields, tennis, basketball and handball courts, a playground, a spray park, the Dennis F. Shine Jr. pool (renovated and rededicated in 2009) and the city's first off-leash dog park, opened in August 2017.

Providence Street Historic District

Ten three-deckers and seven period garages at 127-145 Providence Street, built between 1926 and 1930 with Colonial Revival and Craftsman detail, on 1.39 acres; listed on the National Register in 1990.

Euclid Avenue-Montrose Street Historic District

Forty Colonial Revival three-deckers on a steep hillside between Vernon Street and Perry Avenue, most built between 1910 and 1930 and 27 of them with gambrel roofs; listed on the National Register in 1990.

View Street Historic District

Eleven Colonial Revival three-deckers built between 1916 and 1930 on a street laid out as building reached the hill's steeper terrain; listed on the National Register in 1990.

Upsala Street School

A Romanesque Revival school at 36 Upsala Street, designed by George Clemence, finished in 1894 and enlarged in 1902 and 1923; listed on the National Register in 1980 and converted into apartments.

David Dworman Three-Decker

A Craftsman three-decker of about 1926 at 159 Providence Street, across from Vernon Hill Park, listed on the National Register in 1990.

The Worcester city guide

What is the median home price in Vernon Hill, Worcester?

The Warren Group does not report Vernon Hill separately. Citywide, the median single-family sale price in Worcester for calendar year 2025 was $430,000 across 884 sales, up from $420,000 across 904 sales in 2024, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Is there commuter rail near Vernon Hill?

Vernon Hill has no station of its own. The nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Routes 1 and 11 serve the hill, and Providence Street carries Route 122A.

Which school district serves Vernon Hill?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is by address and is set by the district; review the district's profile with DESE.

Is Vernon Hill one of Worcester's seven hills?

Yes. Vernon Hill is one of the seven hills Worcester is traditionally said to stand on, with Airport Hill, Bancroft Hill, Belmont Hill (Bell Hill), Grafton Hill, Green Hill and Pakachoag Hill. The 15-acre Vernon Hill Park sits on its top.

What kind of housing is on Vernon Hill?

Mostly three-deckers. Groups built from the 1910s to 1930 on View Street, Providence Street and Perry Avenue are National Register historic districts, as is a fourth on Euclid Avenue and Montrose Street between Vernon Street and Perry Avenue, many with Colonial Revival or Craftsman detail and gambrel roofs.
