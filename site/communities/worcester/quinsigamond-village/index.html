Living in Quinsigamond Village, Worcester, MA

A wire-mill village in the Blackstone valley,where the river path to Millbury begins.

Quinsigamond Village is a South Worcester neighborhood in the narrow Blackstone River valley that grew around Washburn & Moen's South Works wire mill after 1846. Millbury Street was its spine, built up with three-deckers, churches, a firehouse and a 1913 Carnegie library. Route 146 has an exit signed for Millbury Street and Quinsigamond Village, and the Blackstone River Bikeway starts at the visitor center beside it.

About Quinsigamond Village

Where it is

Quinsigamond Village lies in South Worcester, in a narrow valley carved by the Blackstone River between two steep ridges. It spread along Millbury Street, and Blackstone Gateway Park on McKeon Road sits at its edge.

Quinsigamond is an Algonquian name, translated as 'pickerel fishing place', that first belonged to the lake between Worcester and Shrewsbury and then to the English settlement that became Worcester. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey suggests that a Native village at Quinsigamond served as a gathering place before settlement. Industry came early to this stretch of the Blackstone River: in 1794 the printer Isaiah Thomas built a two-vat paper mill at Quinsigamond Village, and in 1846 Ichabod and Charles Washburn bought the site for their South Works, an iron rolling mill and wire works. By 1900 the South Works covered the land between Millbury and Ballard Streets; American Steel & Wire bought it in 1899, it became part of U.S. Steel by 1901, and it closed in 1977 as the last major steel mill in New England.

The village grew along Millbury Street as mill workers looked for housing near the works, much of it in three-deckers, alongside earlier houses such as the Berg and Dahlin double cottages of 1875 to 1878. Institutions followed: the Quinsigamond United Methodist Church on Stebbins Street (1884), the First Swedish Co-op Store (1883), a Swedish Congregational chapel on Greenwood Street (1891), Lutheran Emmanuel Church on Greenwood Street (1898 to 1899), the Romanesque Quinsigamond Firehouse (1891 to 1892) and the Quinsigamond School buildings of the 1890s. In 1913 Andrew Carnegie came to lay the cornerstone of the Quinsigamond Branch Library, one of three city branches built partly with his grant. Streetcars ran from City Hall to Quinsigamond via Front, Green and Millbury Streets by 1886, and the company's final extension, around September 1917, added 0.9 miles of track on the Quinsigamond Village line to the Millbury town line.

The river is now the neighborhood's front door. Route 146 runs through the valley with an exit signed for Millbury Street and Quinsigamond Village, and the Massachusetts Turnpike-Route 146 interchange project opened the first 2.5-mile section of the Blackstone River Bikeway, from Worcester to Millbury, in 2005. On the village's edge, the city's 30-acre Blackstone Gateway Park has about half a mile of accessible paths, boardwalks and three bridges over the Middle River, and the Blackstone Heritage Corridor's visitor center opened beside it on a former brownfield on October 27, 2018, and was planned as the Central Region headquarters of the state Department of Conservation and Recreation. The 1892 firehouse, replaced in 1994 by a district station on McKeon Road, stands vacant.

Homes in Quinsigamond Village

The Blackstone River Valley National Heritage Corridor's walking tour describes the village spreading along Millbury Street as workers sought housing near the mills, with many households in three-deckers by 1900, and earlier houses such as the Berg and Dahlin double cottages (1875 to 1878) and the George Reeves House (1857 to 1862), built for a Washburn & Moen foreman. No current count of housing types or ownership for the neighborhood alone was found.

Corners ofQuinsigamond Village.

Village center, Blackstone River Road and Stebbins Street

The institutional core: the 1892 Quinsigamond Firehouse at 15 Blackstone River Road and the 1913 branch library at 14 Blackstone River Road, at the corner of Stebbins Street, now joined to Quinsigamond Elementary School.

Millbury Street

The village's historic spine, built up with three-deckers and cottages as mill workers sought housing near the works; WRTA Route 4 runs on it to the Shoppes at Blackstone Valley.

Greenwood Street

The street of the village's 1890s churches, including a Swedish Congregational chapel of 1891 and Lutheran Emmanuel Church of 1898 to 1899, with the 14-acre Greenwood Park off Forsberg Street.

Getting around

Commuting from Quinsigamond Village

Route 146 runs through the valley, with exit 19 signed for Millbury Street and Quinsigamond Village and exit 20 for McKeon Road (Route 122A) toward I-290; to the south, exit 18 connects to the Massachusetts Turnpike. WRTA Route 4 runs via Millbury Street and Route 11 via Vernon Hill and Greenwood Street. Commuter rail leaves from Worcester station at Union Station, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line.

Places to knowin Quinsigamond Village.

Blackstone Gateway Park

A 30-acre city riverfront park at 15 McKeon Road on the edge of the village, with about 3,000 feet of accessible paths, elevated boardwalks and three bridges over the Middle River, plus interpretive signs.

Blackstone Heritage Corridor Visitor Center

A LEED Silver visitor center at 3 Paul Clancy Way, opened October 27, 2018, with a 4,000-square-foot exhibit hall on innovation and invention, immigration and cultural traditions, and transportation history.

Blackstone River Bikeway

The bikeway's 2.5-mile Worcester-to-Millbury segment, starting at the visitor center, with bike repair stations at the center and at the 1265 Millbury Street parking lot.

Greenwood Park

A 14-acre city park at 14 Forsberg Street, assembled from purchases in 1905, 1912 and 1917, with a spray park, playground, tennis and basketball courts, a softball diamond and nature trails.

Quinsigamond Firehouse

A Romanesque firehouse at 15 Blackstone River Road, built in 1891 and 1892 for $7,112, in service until 1994 and listed on the National Register in 1980; it is vacant.

Quinsigamond Branch Library

A Beaux Arts library of 1913 by the Fuller & Delano Company at Blackstone River Road and Stebbins Street, built partly with an Andrew Carnegie grant; it closed as a library in 1990 and is joined to Quinsigamond Elementary School.

Quinsigamond United Methodist Church

A church at 9 Stebbins Street, built in 1884, given a brick first floor in 1893 and rebuilt after a fire in 1975.

The Worcester city guide

What is the median home price in Quinsigamond Village, Worcester?

The Warren Group does not report Quinsigamond Village separately. Citywide, the median single-family sale price in Worcester for calendar year 2025 was $430,000 across 884 sales, up from $420,000 across 904 sales in 2024, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Is Quinsigamond Village on Lake Quinsigamond?

No. The village is in the Blackstone River valley in South Worcester. It shares its name with Lake Quinsigamond, on the Worcester and Shrewsbury line, because Quinsigamond was the Algonquian name first given to the lake and then to the early settlement of Worcester.

Is there commuter rail near Quinsigamond Village?

The village has no station of its own. The nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Routes 4 and 11 serve the neighborhood, and Route 146 has an exit for Millbury Street and Quinsigamond Village.

Which school district serves Quinsigamond Village?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is by address and is set by the district; review the district's profile with DESE.

Where can you walk or bike in Quinsigamond Village?

Blackstone Gateway Park, a 30-acre city park on McKeon Road, has about half a mile of accessible paths and boardwalks along the Middle River, and the Blackstone River Bikeway runs 2.5 miles from the visitor center at 3 Paul Clancy Way to Millbury. Greenwood Park on Forsberg Street adds nature trails.
