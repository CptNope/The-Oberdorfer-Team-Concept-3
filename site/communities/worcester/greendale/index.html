Living in Greendale, Worcester, MA

A factory village on West Boylston Street,with its library and meeting hall side by side.

Greendale is a North Worcester neighborhood along West Boylston Street that grew around the Norton Company's abrasives works, built on the city's outskirts in 1886 and now part of Saint-Gobain. Its civic block on West Boylston Street holds a fire station, a 1913 branch library and the Greendale Village Improvement Society building, put up between 1897 and 1900. WRTA Routes 30 and 31 run on West Boylston Street, and I-190 passes Indian Lake nearby.

About Greendale

Where it is

Greendale is in North Worcester, strung along West Boylston Street. Its civic buildings stand together on the east side of West Boylston Street at Kendrick Avenue, the article on the Thomas Giguere Three-Decker, on Fairhaven Road east of West Boylston Street, places it in 'Worcester's northern Greendale neighborhood', and the old Greendale YMCA stood beside Indian Lake where I-190 now runs.

Greendale grew up around a factory. In 1886 the partners of the Norton Company, an abrasives maker, built a new plant on the outskirts of Worcester near two rail lines, in what Wikipedia describes as the Greendale neighborhood, and by 1900 the company employed more than 200 people. Transit followed: the street railway's North End line was extended north to Greendale in the early 1890s. The Greendale Improvement Society, founded in 1895 and rechartered in 1897 as the Greendale Village Improvement Society, built, maintained and plowed roads, lobbied the city for fire protection and arranged trash removal; the Kendrick family gave it land for a meeting house, which was put up between 1897 and 1900 at 480 West Boylston Street.

The city's own buildings came next to it. In 1895 the library set up a book delivery point at a Greendale shop, and in 1913 it opened a branch at 470 West Boylston Street, a single-story brick building with limestone trim, a tile hipped roof and Corinthian pilasters designed by Lucius W. Briggs; renamed for Frances Perkins in 1994, it is still a branch of the Worcester Public Library. The Greendale fire station stands nearby at 438 West Boylston Street. On Fairhaven Road stands a National Register-listed Colonial Revival three-decker of about 1926.

Norton shaped the neighborhood well into the 20th century. Its Greendale YMCA branch, founded in 1929, ran programs at a Norton fieldhouse and had its own building on Stores Street by 1950, on land the company gave; in 1970 discussions began about building Route 52, now I-190, and tearing it down. Norton was bought by Saint-Gobain of France in 1990, and its Worcester site is now part of the Saint-Gobain complex. Indian Lake, once North Pond and dammed in 1828 to feed the Blackstone Canal, lies close by, with a renovated beach and bathhouse at Shore Park on its north end.

Homes in Greendale

No published count of housing types or ownership for Greendale alone was found. The National Register lists the Thomas Giguere Three-Decker at 18 Fairhaven Road, a Colonial Revival three-decker of about 1926 with a full-width colonnaded first-floor porch, half-width porches above and a three-car garage. The city notes that the land around Indian Lake has remained primarily residential.

Getting around

Commuting from Greendale

Greendale has no rail station; the nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Route 30 (West Boylston Walmart via Grove and West Boylston Streets) and Route 31 (Lincoln Plaza via Elm and West Boylston Streets) serve West Boylston Street, and I-190 runs beside Indian Lake.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line: Worcester (Union Station, 2 Washington Square)

Check current schedules with the MBTA and the WRTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Things to doGreendale

Places to knowin Greendale.

Frances Perkins Branch Library

The former Greendale Branch Library, opened in 1913 at 470 West Boylston Street to a design by Lucius W. Briggs, renamed for Frances Perkins in 1994 and listed on the National Register in 1980.

Greendale Village Improvement Society Building

The village society's meeting hall at 480 West Boylston Street, put up between 1897 and 1900 on land given by the Kendrick family and listed on the National Register in 1976; it later housed veterans' organizations.

Shore Park, Indian Lake

A 7-acre city park on Shore Drive at the north end of Indian Lake, with a large beach, a bathhouse renovated in 2018, picnic areas and a volleyball court.

Indian Lake

A 193-acre lake, originally North Pond, dammed in 1828 to supply the Blackstone Canal and later an ice-harvesting site; it is open to boating, swimming and fishing, with personal watercraft prohibited.

Thomas Giguere Three-Decker

A Colonial Revival three-decker of about 1926 at 18 Fairhaven Road with a full-width colonnaded porch on the first level and half-width porches above, listed on the National Register in 1990.

The Worcester city guide

What is the median home price in Greendale, Worcester?

The Warren Group does not report Greendale separately. Citywide, the median single-family sale price in Worcester for calendar year 2025 was $430,000 across 884 sales, up from $420,000 across 904 sales in 2024, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Is there commuter rail near Greendale?

Greendale has no station of its own. The nearest is Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, downtown, on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line. WRTA Routes 30 and 31 run along West Boylston Street.

Which school district serves Greendale?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is by address and is set by the district; review the district's profile with DESE.

Is there a public beach near Greendale?

Yes. Shore Park, a city park on Shore Drive at the north end of Indian Lake, has a large beach and a bathhouse renovated in 2018, and the city's Indian Lake Beach is on the lake's eastern side at 34 Sherburne Avenue.
