Living in Canal District, Worcester, MA

Brick mills over a buried canal,between Union Station and Polar Park.

The Canal District is a Worcester neighborhood between Union Station and Kelley Square, named for the Blackstone Canal, which opened in 1828 and still runs underground beneath Harding Street. Its housing mixes apartments and condominiums in 19th-century factories and schools with new apartment buildings beside Polar Park, the 9,508-seat ballpark that opened in 2021. Union Station, the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line, is at its northern edge.

About Canal District

Where it is

The Canal District runs from Union Station in Washington Square along Green, Water, Harding and Winter Streets to Kelley Square, and takes in the Polar Park blocks on Madison Street. It was once the northern part of Green Island, the area between the canal and Mill Brook; the section south of it keeps the Green Island name.

The Blackstone Canal, built from 1824 to 1828, linked Worcester with Providence; it opened on October 7, 1828, and the packet boat Lady Carrington was the first vessel to make the trip. The canal generally followed Mill Brook through the city, and the land between the two was first known as the Island, later Green Island after Green Street. Railroads to Boston and Providence ended canal traffic by 1848, and the Worcester stretch was folded into the sewer system and covered over in the late 19th century; it still runs underground beneath Harding Street. Historical markers about the canal stand on Quinsigamond Avenue at Canton Street, south of the district in Green Island.

Industry followed the water and the rails. The Crompton Loom Works at 132-142 Green Street has a section from 1860, the Heywood boot and shoe factory went up at 68-80 Winter Street in 1879, and the W. H. Hill Envelope Company moved into a four-story, 90,000-square-foot building at 48 Water Street in 1891. Green and Water Streets filled with brick commercial blocks, clubhouses and churches, among them St. John's on Temple Street (1845), the city's oldest Roman Catholic church, and the Goldberg Building at Water and Harrison Streets (about 1908). Construction of I-290 in the 1960s took a large share of the area's housing.

Since the 2000 reopening of Union Station, the district's old buildings have been reused: the 1850 Ash Street School became apartments in 2002, and the Winter Street factory blocks have shops below and condominiums above. Polar Park opened on May 11, 2021, on about 18 acres of former industrial land at 100 Madison Street, part of a Downtown Urban Revitalization Plan the City and the Worcester Redevelopment Authority adopted in 2016. MassDOT rebuilt Kelley Square, a seven-way intersection, as a modified roundabout with shared-use paths on Madison and Green Streets. By August 2024 four apartment buildings with nearly 550 units were open or nearly complete around the ballpark, one of them entirely at below-market rents.

Homes in Canal District

Housing in the Canal District is mostly apartments and condominiums. Older stock is in converted 19th-century buildings, such as the Ash Street School apartments (2002) and condominiums above shops on Winter Street, while the Polar Park blocks added four apartment buildings with nearly 550 units by August 2024, including one entirely at below-market rents. The city's plan also lists The Cove, a 173-unit building with first-floor retail, and Table Talk Lofts, a mixed-income project that will reopen Spruce Street between Green and Washington Streets.

Within the neighborhoodCanal District

Corners ofCanal District.

Kelley Square

A seven-way intersection whose approaches include Water, Harding and Madison Streets, rebuilt by MassDOT as a modified roundabout in a $14.7 million project, with wider sidewalks.

Green and Water Streets

The older core of brick commercial blocks, clubhouses and mills, including the Cahill House (1852) at 42 Green Street, Father Mathew Hall (1888) at 116-120 Green Street and the Crompton Loom Works at 132-142 Green Street.

Harding and Winter Streets

A former boot and shoe manufacturing district over the buried canal, with the 1870 Walker factory at 28 Water Street and the 1879 Heywood factory (Wachusett Building) at 68-80 Winter Street, whose ghost sign faces Harding Street.

Polar Park blocks

About 18 acres of former industrial land on Madison Street, with the ballpark and new apartment buildings; the first, with 228 units, began construction in August 2021.

Getting around

Commuting from Canal District

Union Station, at the district's northern end, is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston's South Station, with Amtrak and intercity buses. I-290 was built through the area in the 1960s, removing much of its housing and part of the 82-88 Winter Street building. WRTA buses serving the area include Route 4 via Millbury Street and Route 42 via Southbridge Street.

Schools

Public schools

Canal District is served by Worcester Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Canal District.

Polar Park

The ballpark at 100 Madison Street, home of the Triple-A Worcester Red Sox since its first game on May 11, 2021, with 9,508 seats and use for concerts and community events.

Rockland Trust (Pickett) Plaza

A pedestrian connection from the Green Street Bridge to Polar Park, with a 90-foot canal replica and fountain.

Crompton Loom Works

The Crompton loom factory at 132-142 Green Street; its earliest section, from 1860, is one of Worcester's oldest surviving factory buildings.

Ash Street School

An 1850 Italianate schoolhouse at 4 Ash Street by Elbridge Boyden, Worcester's second-oldest surviving public school building, converted to apartments in 2002.

St. John's Church

A Greek Revival church at 44 Temple Street, built in 1845 and dedicated in 1846, Worcester's oldest Roman Catholic church; listed on the National Register in 1980.

Cahill House

An Italianate brick house completed in 1852 at 42 Green Street to a design by Elbridge Boyden, home of the Worcester Hibernian Cultural Center since 2005.

Goldberg Building

A four-story yellow-brick commercial building of about 1908 at 97-103 Water Street, on the corner of Harrison Street, with segmented window arches and a corbelled cornice; listed on the National Register in 2007.

The Worcester city guide

Is there a median home price for the Canal District?

The Warren Group reports Worcester only as a whole city, so there is no Canal District figure. Citywide, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $430,000, across 884 sales, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Is the Canal District near commuter rail?

Yes. Union Station, at the district's northern end, is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston's South Station.

Can you still see the Blackstone Canal in Worcester?

Very little of it. The canal was covered over in the late 19th century and still runs underground beneath Harding Street; historical markers stand on Quinsigamond Avenue at Canton Street in Green Island, and a 90-foot canal replica with a fountain sits on the plaza leading to Polar Park.

What kind of housing is in the Canal District?

Mostly apartments and condominiums: units in converted 19th-century buildings such as the Ash Street School, and nearly 550 units in four new buildings around Polar Park as of August 2024, one of them with below-market rents.

Which school district serves the Canal District?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district by address; review the district's profile with DESE.
