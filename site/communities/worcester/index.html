Living in Worcester, MA

Getting to know Worcester,street by street and hill by hill.

Worcester is a city of 206,518 people (2020 Census) in Central Massachusetts and the historic seat of Worcester County. Its housing ranges from 19th-century triple-deckers to single-family homes, with 884 single-family sales in 2025. Worcester station at Union Station is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston, and I-290 runs through the city.

About Worcester

Worcester was incorporated as a town in 1722 and became a city in 1848, and it has served as the seat of Worcester County since 1731. That long run shows in the streets: Victorian-era mill buildings, lunch-car diners and a large stock of triple-decker houses, a building type the city is often credited with originating. Downtown gathers around Worcester Common and Union Station in Washington Square, where the Framingham/Worcester Line begins its run east to Boston's South Station.

Open space threads through the city. Elm Park, whose first 27 acres were bought in 1854, carries a plaque calling it the first purchase of land for a public park in the United States. Mass Audubon's Broad Meadow Brook covers more than 400 acres of woods, fields and marsh, and Quinsigamond State Park offers swimming beaches and a 2,000-meter rowing course on Lake Quinsigamond. The Worcester Art Museum, open since 1898, holds nearly 40,000 objects, and colleges including Clark, Holy Cross and WPI sit across the city's hills.

Homes in Worcester

Worcester has many 19th-century triple-decker houses, a building type that likely originated in the city, along with Victorian-era mill buildings. Single-family homes are also traded in volume: The Warren Group counted 884 single-family sales in 2025.

Getting around

Commuting from Worcester

Worcester station is the western end of the Framingham/Worcester Line, with stops through Grafton, Westborough and Framingham to Boston's South Station. I-290 runs through the city and meets the Massachusetts Turnpike (I-90) and I-395 just south in Auburn.

Elm Park

About 58 acres with Newton Hill, ponds, trails, a disc golf course and summer concerts; its original 27 acres were bought in 1854.

Worcester Art Museum

Open since 1898 at 55 Salisbury Street, with a collection of nearly 40,000 objects.

Broad Meadow Brook Wildlife Sanctuary

Mass Audubon's large urban sanctuary at 414 Massasoit Road, with trails through woods, fields, streams and marsh.

Quinsigamond State Park

Two DCR sites on Lake Quinsigamond, Regatta Point and Lake Park, with swimming, sailing, picnicking and a 2,000-meter rowing course.

What is the median home price in Worcester, MA?

The median single-family sale price in Worcester for calendar year 2025 was $430,000, across 884 sales, up from $420,000 in 2024, according to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

Does Worcester have commuter rail?

Yes. Worcester station at Union Station, 2 Washington Square, is the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line, which runs to Boston's South Station.

Which school district serves Worcester?

Worcester Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.
