Living in Main South, Worcester, MA

Three-deckers and Victorian housesaround Clark University and University Park.

Main South is a neighborhood in southern Worcester named for Main Street, the city's central roadway, with Clark University and the 14.3-acre University Park near its center. Much of its street plan and housing dates from before 1900: three-deckers, multi-unit houses and Victorian houses, several of them on the National Register. WRTA Routes 19 and 27 run along Main Street, and Union Station's commuter rail is downtown.

About Main South

Where it is

Main South follows Main Street through southern Worcester, with commercial areas on Main Street and Park Avenue and residential streets such as Woodland, Freeland, Beaver, Beacon and Oread Streets around Clark University. The city does not publish a fixed boundary.

In the late 1840s Eli Thayer bought pasture along southern Main Street and, in 1849, opened the Oread Collegiate Institute, one of the country's first colleges for women, on a 10-acre hill off Main Street. Building spread slowly, reaching the area of the future campus in the 1880s and 1890s, when Jonas Gilman Clark bought land for the university. The legislature chartered Clark University on March 31, 1887, and it opened in 1889. The city bought the land for University Park across Main Street in 1887 and 1889. By 1890 the area had major industry and hundreds of new multi-unit houses.

Most of Main South's physical structure was in place before the end of the 19th century, and its streets still show it. Victorian houses, many renovated, stand beside three-deckers. The Woodland Street Historic District, next to the Clark campus, holds 19 Victorian houses built from 1868 to about 1904 in Second Empire, Italianate, Gothic Revival, Queen Anne and Colonial Revival styles. Three Queen Anne triple-deckers of about 1892 face University Park on Crystal Street, and five Colonial Revival triple-deckers of about 1916 line Beaver Street; both groups were listed on the National Register in 1990. Main Street itself carries churches listed in 1980, including Pilgrim Congregational at 909 Main Street and St. Peter's at 935.

Clark University and the Main South Community Development Corporation work together through the University Park Partnership. The CDC has renovated multi-family houses as affordable rentals in the Beacon and Oread Street block east of campus, and in 2013 the Kilby-Gardner-Hammond project added a Boys and Girls Club with a gymnasium, a learning center, an Olympic-size pool and an athletic field, along with renovated affordable housing. University Park Campus School, a Worcester public school for grades 7 to 12 founded with Clark in 1997, is on Freeland Street, and Clark's Center for Media Arts, Computing and Design opened at 7 Hawthorne Street in 2023.

Homes in Main South

Main South's housing is mostly 19th- and early 20th-century: three-deckers, multi-unit houses and Victorian houses, many renovated. National Register groups include the 19 houses of the Woodland Street Historic District and the triple-deckers on Crystal Street (about 1892) and Beaver Street (about 1916). The Main South CDC develops and manages affordable rental and ownership housing, including renovated multi-family houses on Beacon and Oread Streets and in the Kilby, Gardner and Hammond Street area.

Corners ofMain South.

Clark University and University Park

Clark's National Register-listed campus, bounded by Maywood, Woodland and Main Streets, faces the 14.3-acre University Park across Main Street.

Woodland Street Historic District

Nineteen houses on Woodland Street between Charlotte and Oberlin Streets, with frontage on Hawthorne, Loudon and Norwood Streets, built from 1868 in Second Empire, Italianate, Gothic Revival, Queen Anne and Colonial Revival styles; listed in 1980.

Beacon and Oread Streets

A block east of campus where the Main South CDC renovated multi-family houses as affordable rentals; the C.H. Fitch and Edward Stark houses at 15 and 21 Oread Street are on the National Register.

Kilby, Gardner and Hammond Streets

Blocks rebuilt in a project completed in 2013, with a Boys and Girls Club, an athletic field, a gymnasium, an Olympic-size pool and renovated affordable housing.

Getting around

Commuting from Main South

WRTA Route 19 (Webster Square to Clark University) and Route 27 (Auburn Mall) run along Main Street, and Route 825 is the Park Avenue Connector. The nearest commuter rail is Worcester station at Union Station downtown, the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line to Boston's South Station.

  • Framingham/Worcester Line: Worcester (Union Station, 2 Washington Square)

Check current schedules with the MBTA and the WRTA; compare the trip from each address before you buy.

Schools

Public schools

Main South is served by Worcester Public Schools. School assignment depends on the address and the district's own rules; confirm it with the district, and review its profile with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

Places to knowin Main South.

University (Crystal) Park

A 14.3-acre city park at 965 Main Street, bought in 1887 and 1889, with Crystal Pond, walking trails, basketball, tennis and volleyball courts, a playground and a spray park opened in August 2025.

Clark University

A 72-acre campus chartered in 1887, with Jonas Clark Hall (1887), Goddard Library, the 658-seat Atwood Hall and the Kneller Athletic Center; the campus's historic core is on the National Register.

Oread Castle Park

A 3.2-acre city park at 20 Alden Street on the site of Eli Thayer's Oread Collegiate Institute (1849 to 1881), with basketball courts, fitness stations, a playground and a nature trail.

Crystal Street Historic District

Three Queen Anne triple-deckers of about 1892 at 30-34 Crystal Street, facing University Park, each with a gable-roofed entry porch on one side and a rounded bay with a conical roof at the other end; listed in 1990.

Beaver Street Historic District

Five Colonial Revival triple-deckers of about 1916 at 31-39 Beaver Street, near Clark, with three-story front porches; listed in 1990 under the Worcester Three-Deckers submission.

Main Street churches

South Unitarian (888 Main Street), Pilgrim Congregational (909 Main Street) and St. Peter's Catholic Church (935 Main Street), all listed on the National Register in 1980.

The Worcester city guide

Is there a median home price for Main South?

The Warren Group reports Worcester only as a whole city, so there is no Main South figure. Citywide, the median single-family sale price for calendar year 2025 was $430,000, across 884 sales, according to its MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town report.

What kind of housing is in Main South?

Mostly 19th- and early 20th-century housing: three-deckers, multi-unit houses and Victorian houses. Several groups are on the National Register, including the Woodland Street Historic District and the triple-deckers on Crystal and Beaver Streets.

How do you get from Main South to the commuter rail?

WRTA buses run along Main Street, including Route 19 and Route 27, toward downtown. The commuter rail stop is Worcester station at Union Station, the western end of the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line.

Which school district serves Main South?

Worcester Public Schools. School assignment is set by the district by address; review the district's profile with DESE.
