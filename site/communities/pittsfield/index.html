Living in Pittsfield, MA

A county seat around Park Square,with two lakes and a state forest at its edges.

Pittsfield is a city of 43,927 people (2020 Census) and the seat of Berkshire County. A city housing analysis describes its homes as mostly single-family houses and small two- and three-unit buildings, and The Warren Group counted 434 single-family sales in 2025. Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited stops at the Joseph Scelsi Intermodal Transportation Center, and the seasonal Berkshire Flyer runs from there to New York. US 7 and US 20 meet in the city, and the Massachusetts Turnpike is about 10 miles south in Lee.

About Pittsfield

Settlement here dates to 1752, and the plantation of Pontoosuc was organized in 1753 before being incorporated as a town in 1761 and named for William Pitt. Park Square was laid out in 1790 on donated land, and the Old Town Hall, the county courthouse, the Berkshire Athenaeum and the First Church of Christ still face it. Late in the 19th century downtown pushed north along North Street, helped by an 1891 trolley line. In 1890 William Stanley Jr. founded the Stanley Electric Manufacturing Company here, and General Electric bought it in 1903. Pittsfield became a city in 1891.

Water and woods frame the city. Onota Lake, 617 acres and owned by the city, and Pontoosuc Lake are used for swimming, boating and fishing, and Pittsfield State Forest covers 11,000 acres with Berry Pond and 30 miles of trails. Herman Melville finished Moby-Dick at Arrowhead, his home on Holmes Road from 1850 to 1863. On West Housatonic Street, Hancock Shaker Village keeps 20 historic buildings, including a round stone barn from 1826. Downtown, the Colonial Theatre of 1903 reopened after restoration in 2006, and the Berkshire Museum, founded by Zenas Crane in 1903, holds more than 40,000 objects.

Homes in Pittsfield

A 2009 city housing analysis describes Pittsfield's housing stock as relatively old, made up mainly of single-family homes and small multifamily buildings of two or three units, and counted 21,633 units citywide in 2008. In the Westside and Morningside neighborhoods next to downtown, more than 80% of homes were built before 1969, and many larger houses from the 1890s to 1930s have been divided into apartments.

Neighborhoods and villagesPittsfield

Where you mightlive in Pittsfield.

Park Square

The downtown green laid out in 1790, ringed by the Old Town Hall, the county courthouse, the Berkshire Athenaeum and the First Church of Christ; its historic district reaches roughly to East Housatonic, South, North and Fenn Streets and Wendell Avenue.

Upper North Street

Late-19th-century commercial blocks along North Street, built as downtown grew north from Park Square after an 1891 trolley line.

Westside

The neighborhood just west of North Street, split by the West Branch of the Housatonic River, with denser older houses to the east and larger lots to the west.

Morningside

East of North Street between Springside Avenue and East Street, with one- and two-family homes, side-by-side duplexes and newer single-family homes on larger lots to the northeast.

Getting around

Commuting from Pittsfield

Pittsfield is outside the MBTA. Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited stops daily at the Joseph Scelsi Intermodal Transportation Center on its Boston section, with Massachusetts stops at Springfield, Worcester, Framingham and Boston. In 2026 the Berkshire Flyer ran from Pittsfield to New York's Moynihan Train Hall via Albany-Rensselaer on Fridays and Sundays from June 12 to September 19. The Berkshire Regional Transit Authority is based at the same center, with routes to North Adams, Lee, Dalton, Lanesborough and an express to Great Barrington. US 7 and US 20 meet in the city, and the Massachusetts Turnpike (I-90) is about 10 miles south in Lee.

  • Amtrak Lake Shore Limited (Boston to Chicago, daily): Pittsfield (Joseph Scelsi Intermodal Transportation Center, 1 Columbus Avenue)
  • Amtrak Berkshire Flyer (seasonal, to New York): Pittsfield (Joseph Scelsi Intermodal Transportation Center, 1 Columbus Avenue)

Places to knowin Pittsfield.

Pittsfield State Forest

An 11,000-acre DCR forest at 1041 Cascade Street with Berry Pond, 30 miles of trails, an accessible Tranquility Trail and three campgrounds.

Onota Lake

A 617-acre lake owned by the city and used for fishing, swimming, water skiing and sailing.

Arrowhead

The 1785 house at 780 Holmes Road where Herman Melville lived from 1850 to 1863 and finished Moby-Dick, run by the Berkshire County Historical Society.

Hancock Shaker Village

A living history museum at 1843 West Housatonic Street on the site of a Shaker community active from 1790 to 1960, with 20 historic buildings and a round stone barn from 1826.

Colonial Theatre

A 1903 theater at 111 South Street whose restoration was completed in 2006.

Berkshire Museum

Founded in 1903 by Zenas Crane, with art, natural history and ancient-civilization collections of more than 40,000 objects.

Every guide in the Berkshires

What is the median home price in Pittsfield, MA?

According to The Warren Group's MA December 2025 Sales, Prices by Town table, the median single-family sale price in Pittsfield for calendar year 2025 was $293,000, across 434 sales. In 2024 it was $286,000, across 373 sales.

Does Pittsfield have train service?

Yes. Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited stops daily at the Joseph Scelsi Intermodal Transportation Center, 1 Columbus Avenue, on its Boston to Chicago run. The seasonal Berkshire Flyer runs from the same station to New York on summer weekends; in 2026 it ran Fridays and Sundays from June 12 to September 19. Pittsfield is not served by the MBTA.

Is there local bus service in Pittsfield?

Yes. The Berkshire Regional Transit Authority runs its buses from the Intermodal Transportation Center, with city routes and routes to North Adams, Lee, Dalton, Lanesborough and Great Barrington. The authority says its service is fare-free through June 2027.

Which school district serves Pittsfield?

Pittsfield Public Schools. Review its profile with DESE.
