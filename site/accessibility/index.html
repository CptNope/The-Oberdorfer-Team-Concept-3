Accessibility statement

A home searchopen to everyone.

The standard this site aims for, how it is checked, what is not there yet, and how to tell us when something is hard to use.

Last updated November 16, 2026

The Oberdorfer Team wants everyone to be able to look at homes, read a town guide and reach us through oberdorferteam.com, including people who use a screen reader, a keyboard, screen magnification, voice control or other assistive technology.

Our standard

We aim for this site to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA, the standard published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

What the site does

  • Every page works with a keyboard alone. A visible outline shows where you are, and a "Skip to content" link jumps past the menu.
  • Text and controls meet the WCAG contrast minimums. Links inside sentences are underlined, not shown by color alone.
  • Pages reflow to the width of a small phone, the same as zooming a desktop browser to 400%, without scrolling sideways. Wide price tables scroll on their own.
  • Pages use headings, landmarks and labeled form fields, so screen readers can move around them.
  • If a form is missing something, the site says what in words, next to the field, and reads out the result when you send it.
  • Images have text descriptions. Decorative images are hidden from screen readers.
  • Animations stop when your device's "reduce motion" setting is on. Listing videos and 3D tours load only when you press play.
  • Booking a call online works with a keyboard and a screen reader: days and times are buttons, the month and the open times are read out when they change, and the form's fields are labeled.

How we check it

On November 15, 2026 every type of page on the site was tested with automated checks (axe-core) and by hand: keyboard use, visible focus, reflow at 320 pixels wide, and form errors. The issues found were fixed. The site is checked again when new kinds of pages or features are added. On November 16, 2026 the online booking calendar on Brandon's and Kaitlyn's pages was checked the same way at each step: choosing a day, choosing a time, and filling in and sending the form.

Known limitations

  • The Wakefield at Home PDF. The Fall 2026 issue is a print design and is not yet tagged for screen readers. If you would like its contents in another format, contact us and we will send them.
  • Spam protection. Our forms use Cloudflare Turnstile to stop spam. It usually works without asking you anything. If it ever stops you from sending a form, call or text us instead.
  • Other companies' content. Listing videos and 3D tours come from providers such as YouTube, Vimeo, Matterport, Kuula and CloudPano, and their players are theirs. Links to Instagram, Facebook, Google Maps and other sites lead to pages we do not control.

Tell us what is hard to use

If anything on this site is hard to use, or you need information in another format, please tell us which page and what happened.
