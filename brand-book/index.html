03

Why we do it.Where it leads.

Proposed

Mission

To help people across Massachusetts make confident real estate decisions, with personal attention, clear local knowledge and presentation worthy of every home.

Vision

To become one of Massachusetts' most trusted real estate teams, known for a family's care and a premium firm's preparation, carried into every community by agents who share that standard.

Why "confident decisions"

The publication's market page teaches rather than sells. "A useful snapshot helps you ask better questions." The mission keeps that educational promise.

Why "every home"

It restates the brand promise, Luxury at every price point, in plain words: the standard does not depend on the price.

Why "every community"

Growth is part of the vision but no market is named. The brand must not stay limited to Wakefield, and it must not claim offices it does not have.

Archetype

The Caregiver, guided by the Sage.

The Caregiver protects and supports. It fits "showing up for the people who count on us." The Sage informs, which fits the market education and "clear guidance and attention to the details." A trace of the Creator carries the creative marketing.

Why not the Ruler, the usual luxury archetype? Status and exclusivity would contradict "every price point" and push away first-time buyers. This brand's luxury is care and craft, not rank.

Watch for: a Caregiver can drift into softness. The Sage keeps the work expert and specific.

07

Speak like a neighbourwho knows the market.

The publication already has the voice: warm, plain, specific and useful. Its best lines are short sentences that turn on a single idea.

Four rules

Be useful first.

Every paragraph should help someone decide or act. "Ask for a property-specific market review before deciding what to offer."

Show the craft, don't name it.

Leave out "luxurious," "stunning" and "dream home." Name the real things instead: the light, the layout, the walk to the lake.

Use short sentences that answer.

"Close to Boston. Closer to what matters." Pair two lines when the second one moves the thought forward.

Source and date every fact.

Name the source, the period and the geography. "Figures may be revised" is part of the voice, not fine print.

By channel

ChannelRegisterExample
WebsiteClear, warm, scannable. Lead with the reader's goal."Selling this spring? Start with the right comparisons."
Agent biographiesFirst person, specific, verified. Values before credentials."I grew up … I help buyers who …" (only verified facts)
Property marketingConcrete and sensory, never generic. Facts in the specifications."Morning light across the kitchen; a ten-minute walk to the commuter rail."
Luxury listingsEven more restraint. Let the architecture and the details speak."An 1890 Queen Anne, restored with care." Not "Exquisite masterpiece!"
Social mediaPersonal and local, one idea per post. Brandon and Kaitlyn's own voice."Saturday at the farmers market. Here's what we brought home."
EmailA letter from people, not a blast. One clear next step."The fall issue is here. Inside: six ways to spend a Saturday close to home."
Community articlesEditorial, researched, credited, with dates for anything that changes."Seasonal schedules can change. Check each destination before you go."
Buyer and seller guidesStep by step and calm. Explain terms the first time they appear."A listing price is a seller's starting point. A closed sale shows what a buyer actually paid."

Rewrites

Avoid

Your dream home awaits! Unlock luxury living with Massachusetts' #1 team.

Hype, an unverifiable ranking, no information.

Use

For the home you have. For the life you are building. Let's start with what matters most to you.

Source line plus a clear next step.

Avoid

Wakefield home prices are up! Now's the time to sell.

No source, no date, and it implies advice that applies to everyone.

Use

Wakefield's median sale price was $786,979 for the three months ending August 2026 (Redfin). Your home's own comparisons matter more.

Sourced and dated, then turned toward the reader's own home.

Clear space and size

Clear space: keep at least the cap height of the "T" (marked x) clear on all sides. On covers, the inset frame counts as the edge.

Minimum sizes: primary 1.25 in / 120 px wide; stacked 0.9 in / 90 px; compact 24 px tall; monogram 0.25 in / 20 px tall (single frame below 32 px).

Approved colors: navy on ivory or sandstone; gold or ivory on navy; ivory on photography over a scrim; single color for production methods.

Lettering: Cormorant Garamond Light, mixed case, tracking +12. Caps lines use Cormorant Garamond Light tracked +160 to +420, outlined so they render identically everywhere.

Incorrect use

Gold on ivory

Fails contrast. Gold is for navy grounds.

Stretched

Never distort, condense or rotate.

Metallic gradients

Use no gold foil effects on screen, and no gradients or glows.

Merged with REWAP

Keep the brands apart. Use the plate or the typographic lockup.

Files

Vector SVG in /assets/logo/: primary (navy, gold, ivory, mono), stacked, compact, monogram, social avatar, favicon, and the brokerage lockup (navy, ivory). PNG exports: avatar 800 px, apple-touch-icon 180 px, favicon 32 px. Print-ready PDF and EPS versions will be exported after approval.

Rules

Gold is a line

Use gold for rules, frames, linework and labels on navy. Never use it for large fills, buttons on ivory, gradients or foil effects on screen.

Navy for big moments

Use navy for whole-section grounds such as covers, statistics and closings. Use it once or twice per page, not as scattered boxes.

Print

Proof navy and gold on press. Antique gold can be printed as a PMS metallic for covers only, after approval. Uncoated stock will shift values.

12

Real places.Natural light.

Photography carries this brand. It should look like an architecture and lifestyle magazine: honest, warm and composed.

Lake Quannapowitt Panorama, RedSoxFan274 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, cropped. Breakheart Reservation, Joseph Reagle / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, cropped. Team portrait from the publication (approval needed).

Favor and avoid

Favor

  • Natural light, including dusk and golden hour for exteriors
  • Warm neutral interiors with the real architectural detail
  • Real Massachusetts streets, commons, lakes and trails, credited
  • People in genuine moments: Brandon and Kaitlyn at work, with permission
  • Consistent, subtle grading: warm whites, deep but not crushed shadows

Avoid

  • Obvious stock photography and handshake clichés
  • HDR halos, over-saturated skies, fish-eye wide angles
  • AI-generated images presented as real clients, homes, agents or places
  • Fake testimonial portraits
  • Unlicensed images. Every image needs a source and a license on file.

Categories

Team and agent portraits

Use 4:5 framing and natural window light, on an ivory or deep navy backdrop or a real home setting. Wear navy, ivory or black, with no busy patterns. All agents get matching framing and grading.

Homes, at every price point

Show a modest condominium with the same care as an estate: straight verticals, lights on, rooms decluttered. Shoot exteriors at golden hour or blue hour.

Communities and seasons

Show the places locals love, such as town commons, lakes, Main Streets and farms. Shoot each community through the seasons for the At Home issues.

Lifestyle and details

Show tables, doors, hardware, light on a stair. Keep it tactile and specific, and stage it simply.

Massachusetts architecture

Colonials, Capes, triple-deckers, Victorians, mill conversions. Show the state's real housing stock, not just its grandest homes.

Office

Show the office once it opens: the entrance, the meeting table, the team at work. Office pending

Never publish

Not without written verification.

  • Awards, rankings or "Top Producer" claims
  • Production statistics or sales volume
  • Client testimonials without permission on file
  • Agent profiles, credentials or license numbers
  • Listings not supplied by an authorized MLS or IDX feed
  • Office addresses or brokerage approvals
  • Commission arrangements or recruiting benefits
  • Market data without a source, a period and a geography
